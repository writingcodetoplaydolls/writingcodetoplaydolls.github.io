family

What it is. The December 2018 Unity scene rebuilt for the web: a giants' landscape, every surface skinned with video. Two 24 m figures of Flannery and a third at the origin wearing the Quilt animations, a 7.9 m lamp wearing two quilt renders, a three-times-size oven playing the sky's animation, a washing machine (tipped in Unity, stood upright and dressed in enamel here), a 285 m hawk 220 m up, a ten-times city house, and the whole sky an equirectangular video. Added for the web: a ground that rises and falls to meet each object at its base (the house sits 42 m below the giants' feet; the hawk flies and is left out) with a sand path from the figures' feet down to the veranda's west edge so the house can be entered on foot, a doorway cut into the city house where its veranda meets the main block, its window glass and three door panels taken out at load time, a floor flood-filled over the whole inside at deck level, a plastered interior with lamps, the washing machine stripped of the room that came with it and scaled to 10 m, and a projector hanging in the hall throwing TraylorAnimation.mp4 out through the doorway onto the veranda deck. Walk mode, teleport by clicking any floor, deck or roof, a reset button and Home key.

Source and dates. Its lineage runs back through the Intro to Sound Art Unity projects (Sept–Dec 2018), the PlayHouse Processing sketch with the Quilt videos (July 2018) and the Skanect scans (2016). The Unity scene had no projector; the projector is a 2026 addition. Web rebuild: 7 October 2026.

puppet

What it is. The Mixamo movement with the Mixamo skin thrown away. Every joint in a Mixamo file is a plain THREE.Bone with a name, and a clip is tracks addressed to those names, so the skin is hidden and a body is written in code on the joints: spheres and struts hung on the bones, a lamp in the left hand, a trail drawn from the right hand each frame, and two sliders that bend a joint further after the clip has posed it. Nothing goes back to Mixamo.

Source and dates. Same rig and clips as mixamo. Written 8 October 2026, as "route 1" of the question "can I replace the model without going back to Mixamo": the joints were already addressable. Routes not built: skinning a new mesh onto the same armature in Blender (automatic weights), and retargeting the clips to a different rig with SkeletonUtils.retargetClip.

Files. src\sketches\puppet.js.

ik

What it is. Your own movement on the joints, with no clips at all. Four spheres are targets and the hands (feet optional) reach for them, solved every frame by an analytic two-bone solve with a pole: the triangle root–elbow–tip fixes the elbow angle, the pole says which way the elbow points (out and down for arms, forward for knees), and the shoulder or hip is turned so both bones land on that triangle. Every joint is clamped to angle limits measured on this rig, and the body is solid: capsules on the torso, head and legs, radii measured from the skin; a sphere stops at the body's surface and a limb that would pass through it is solved toward a goal slid along the surface instead. Drag a sphere with the mouse or a finger; in a headset, squeeze a controller near one to carry it; "targets drift" moves them from code.

Source and dates. Same rig. Written 8 October 2026. The solver went through four designs in a day, each replaced for a measured reason: cyclic coordinate descent (cannot bend a straight limb toward a target on its own line), per-axis angle clamps (flip a big swing to the wrong side), a swivel search (picked behind-the-back poses), and finally the two-bone pole solve with a swing-twist clamp that searches the cone's boundary for the nearest allowed direction. Two measurements matter: the joint axes (which local axis is each hinge, and which way is positive; Mixamo's Y is always the twist) and the fact that the rig's rest arm droops 35° below the T-pose, so the shoulder's cone is measured from the arm held level to the side.

Files. src\sketches\ik.js (the limits table LIMITS, the body table BODY, the solver solve(), all with their measurements in comments).

multiscreen

What it is. One sketch on several displays at once: a projector on HDMI, a second monitor, a laptop screen. The window it opens in is the controller. Its panel lists the displays the computer has (the Window Management API, Chrome and Edge, behind a permission the browser asks for when you press "find screens"), and each display's row opens an output window on it, fullscreen where the browser allows a fullscreen pop-up (Chrome 123 and later) and on the output's first click otherwise. Every output is the same scene (a turning sculpture, a satellite, a ring of coloured pillars) from its own fixed camera: front for a projector facing the scene, side, back, top, and a wide low throw for a projector on the floor. Nothing is streamed: the controller sends a few numbers twenty times a second over a BroadcastChannel (same machine, no server) and each output carries the motion forward from the moment they were sent, so all windows show the same instant.

With no second display attached, "simulated screens" opens movable, resizable frames on the page, all at once, one per view, each drawn as its own viewport of the same renderer after the main view; drag a frame by its bar, resize it by its corner, change its view in its bar. Several windows open at the same time on one screen, as Processing's multiple windows did, for composing the piece before the hardware is there.

Source and dates. Written 9 October 2026 as the first test toward outputting a piece to several devices. Built in separable pieces on purpose: src\lib\screens.js (finding the displays, opening a window on one, going fullscreen), src\lib\sync.js (the channel, who is on it, publishing and following a state), src\lib\panels.js (the simulated screens: movable frames and drawing a view into each), and the sketch (the shared scene, the named views, the two roles). The harness gained one hook for it, afterRender, for passes over the finished frame. Verified with two windows in step to a ten-thousandth of a radian and five simulated screens open at once; the multi-display placement itself needs a machine with a second display attached.

Files. src\sketches\multiscreen.js, src\lib\screens.js, src\lib\sync.js, src\lib\panels.js.

bouquet

What it is. A visualization of search-term hits read from a CSV: a cross between a particle system, a bouquet of balloons and fireflies. Every single hit is a firefly, a glowing point blinking to its own rhythm. All the hits of a term gather into a sphere of fireflies, sized so they fill it evenly (the radius grows with the cube root of the hits, so a term with eight times the hits is twice the size), and the spheres float together under the ceiling, bobbing and bouncing off each other like balloons in a breeze. A term that narrows another ("new materialism art education" under "new materialism", "positionality AND intersectionality" under "positionality") is a smaller sphere held against its parent, like a grape on the bunch. Colour by group, by parent or by term, in Amy's own palette (supplied 22 October 2026: periwinkle, olive, burnt orange and ochre for the first four groups, then the free ones; the near-black olive is the night the fireflies shine against). Hover a sphere for its hits, group and narrowings (its faint skin and label show); click to look closer; the largest broad terms are labelled, the rest show their label on hover. Reads the sample file, or ?csv=<url>, or any CSV dropped on the page: a term column and a number column are enough, a group column colours, a parent column fixes the narrowings, and rows whose number is not a number (SOURCE, NOTES) are set aside. Up to 200,000 fireflies; beyond that each point stands for more than one hit and the corner text says so. A "dispersed" control runs from tight spheres to loose ones: as it rises the fireflies drift further from their home spots and begin to leave on visits to the nearest neighbouring spheres, settling there for a few seconds and taking on that sphere's colour, then flying home with it, so the spheres fill with mixed colours (about a fifth of the fireflies are away at any moment at full dispersion; turned down to zero, they all come home and the colours fade back). The floor is a grey-blue line pattern, an Archimedean spiral, a Hilbert curve, a triangular lattice or a honeycomb (src\lib\floorcurve.js), and under it a sheet of old green bottle glass drawn by a shader (a tint that deepens where the glass lies low or is seen edge-on, a fresnel sheen, one soft highlight, the slight waviness of old glass), both on a height field that rises toward each sphere's underside, as if reaching for it, and swells slowly; "reach" and "swell" set how far, the tint can be changed, and either part can be turned off. "Lift" sets how high the spheres float, from resting on the floor to pressed under the ceiling, so floor and spheres can be brought to meet. Fireflies are larger in spheres with fewer hits ("bigger in small spheres"), so a single hit is still a point you can see. The data it opens with is the tidied list with "assemblage" at its inferred 1,192 results (Amy's decision of 2 November 2026, on the evidence file).

Source and dates. Written 1 November 2026 for the literature-review search log (K:\ResearchRepository\Dissertation_Hub\LitReviewProtocol\search_terms_totals.csv: 67 terms in 8 rounds, 16 of them narrowings of another, 44,809 hits in all), which it reads as it is. A first version drew solid balloons on strings with the fireflies swarming round them; it was rebuilt the same day to the brief as meant: one firefly per hit, the term's hits as the sphere itself. Built in separable pieces: src\lib\csv.js (reading, column guessing, drop and file picker), src\lib\terms.js (the tree of narrowings), src\lib\balloons.js (the floating, bouncing bodies: lift, ceiling, walls, collisions, tethers, pick spheres), src\lib\fireflies.js (one Points object, a home spot per fly inside its sphere, swirl, wander, blink), src\lib\labels.js (sprite labels), and the sketch (hits to radius and colour; hover, click, panel).

Seven states (2 November 2026). The data file became a states file, one row per term, a kind (keyword search, or one of the two forward-citation sweeps) and one number column per state, built by LitReviewProtocol\build_bouquet_states.py from the per-hit search record, the text inventory, the document files on disk and the reference list of the comprehensive exam. The keyword states: all hits (28,415 across 55 terms); distinct hits, the same with repeats of one work within a term removed (24,187 of 28,415); overlapping hits, the term's distinct works also returned under another keyword term (2,261 of 24,187, across 48 terms; the term pairs drawn as ties between the spheres, heavier for more shared works). The forward citations, Barad's Meeting the Universe Halfway and Shlain's Art and Physics, are a different instrument and have a state of their own (5,401 citing works of 9,863 returned; the Barad year slices overlapped heavily); only 2 works cite both, drawn as the one tie between the two spheres. Then, across both kinds: texts on disk, the works actually held as document files under K:\OneDrive\UNM (the Dissertation, PHD and Comps folders scanned, 1,453 files, plus the inventory's citation titles for them; 672 works matched, 739 term-works across 18 terms); used in the comps, the works whose title is in the reference list at the end of the comprehensive exam itself (ComprehensiveExam_AmyTraylor_April 22_2026.docx, 81 references, read and matched by LitReviewProtocol\comps_references_april.py into a review file with an exact / close / none column; 28 of the 81 were found by any search at all; 37 term-works across 12 terms, measured against the texts held). The fetch logs and the two .bib files were dropped as measures: the logs understated what is held, the .bib files hold works the exam did not cite. In those two states a snowball sphere of its own holds what no search returned (the states file's one row with kind "snowball": 702 of 1,353 distinct held works; 51 of the 81 exam references), so the texts that came by other routes are told apart from the search terms. A state menu switches; the corner text states each state's numbers against the state before it. The floor has its own on/off switch and an opacity control.

Ties (2 November 2026). The overlap states draw the works two spheres share, two ways, chosen by the "ties" control: between spheres, one line per term pair, heavier for more shared works; or work to work (the default), one line per shared work from the firefly that stands for it in one sphere to the firefly that stands for it in the other, following the fireflies as they drift and visit. The shared works come from a third file, bouquet_shared_works_<date>.csv (the overlap pairs opened up, one row per shared work, 1,582 rows); a sphere's k-th shared work is its k-th firefly. In the forward-citation state that is all the connections between Barad and Shlain: the 2 works that cite both.

The look (2 November 2026). The whole scene floats in the side of an old glass soda bottle: a full-screen background shader (src\lib\bottle.js) of pale blue-green glass read as the inside of a bottle. A ray from the eye at an angle round the bottle meets the glass: a round wall, or (the default, "bottle sides" eight) one of a ring of flat panes, each met at its own slant, so the bottle is an octagon seen from inside. Its profile with height is an octagon's too: every vertical line runs straight through the middle, turns inward at 45 degrees toward top and bottom, then runs straight again. On the glass: wide soft stripes of pressed glass, two to a pane, leaning a different way on every pane so they kink at the pane edges; the pane edges as bright ridges; hoops kept to a whisper; a band of light coming through, stepped from pane to pane; a slow waviness and a few bubbles. "Bottle curve" sets how tight (three to five panes in view), "line bend" how far the lines turn and lean; a round bottle keeps its mould seam instead. White semi-transparent boxes for the corner text and the panel, navy text; the fireflies drawn for a light background (a paler core when on, instead of the additive glow). The floor line stays, in grey blue and semi-transparent ("floor line opacity"); the bottle-glass floor sheet is an option, off by default.

Picking (2 November 2026). Not every sphere could be clicked in the overlapping state. Two causes. The skins are drawn back-side, so a ray cast at them lands on a sphere's far surface and a small sphere nested in or just behind a big one took the click meant for the big one. And a term sharing one or two works is a sphere a few pixels wide with no visible skin, nothing to aim at. The sketch now picks for itself (section 7): the sphere whose near surface the ray meets first; over empty glass, a visible label under the pointer (labels of the biggest terms would otherwise cover the small spheres behind them); then the sphere whose edge is nearest the pointer within 14 pixels. Hover and click use the same pick. Both harnesses now pass the ray to onSelect as a third argument, so a headset pick goes the same way; the harness's own hit is a fallback.

Files. src\sketches\bouquet.js, the modules above plus src\lib\bottle.js, public\data\bouquet-sample.csv and bouquet-sample-overlaps.csv (invented numbers in the shape of the real files).
