Layout specimen

Every part of the layout on one page, with the markdown that made it. Keep this chapter in the document while writing and drop it from thesis.json before anyone else reads the thing.

This page is the reference. Each section shows a feature and the markdown beneath it, so nothing has to be remembered.

#Prose

A paragraph is a run of lines with a blank line after it. code, bold, italic, a link, and a footnote marker that becomes a sidenote.1 A second marker, to show the numbering.2

1Wide enough for a gutter and this sits beside the paragraph that cites it. Narrower and it folds away until the marker is tapped. Either way it prints as a numbered note.
2Notes are numbered in the order they are first cited, not the order they are written, so they can be defined anywhere in the chapter.

A block quotation is a run of lines each beginning with a greater-than sign, and a line beginning with a double dash becomes its attribution.

Author, Title (year)

A rule is three or more asterisks or dashes on their own line.

#Headings

## Section and ### Sub-section get an id from their text and appear in the contents rail on the left. ## Section {#chosen-id} chooses the id instead, which is what to do once a heading has been cited somewhere and its wording changes. Hovering a heading shows a # that copies its address.

#Figures

A figure is a block with attributes on the opening line and its caption inside. Four kinds: a sketch run inline, an embed of a page already published, a video, or an image.

:::figure sketch=cube width=wide height=50vh title="A cube" label="Figure 3.1"
The caption, which takes inline markdown.
:::

#Widths

width=column keeps a figure inside the text measure.

width=wide is the default: the measure plus the sidenote gutter, and in book mode both columns of its page, with the text flowing round it. width=full goes edge to edge, which suits anything that is a place rather than a picture, and takes a page of its own in the book. width=spread is the same but takes the whole two-page spread: a plate, with nothing else on it.

#Starting and stopping

A figure starts when the reader comes near it and stops when they leave, and only four WebGL contexts stay alive at once — the figure furthest from the reader gives its context up and falls back to its still. Add autoplay=false to a figure with a large download behind it, and weight="..." to say what the wait is for:

:::figure sketch=family width=full height=80vh autoplay=false
           weight="about 40 MB of models and video — starts on a click"

#A page that is already finished

Some pieces are published as whole pages of their own — the Bouquet, the comps graph, an old p5 sketch. Those go in as an iframe and are not touched:

:::figure embed=/MaximalistAssemblage/Bouquet/ width=full height=70vh
:::figure sketch=bouquet site-embed=/MaximalistAssemblage/Bouquet/

The second line is the useful one: the module while writing on the dev server, the published page once it is on the site. dev-embed= does the reverse.

#The two ways of reading

The button in the top bar switches between them, and remembers the choice.

Book is the default where there is room: this spread, two columns to a page, turned with the chevrons either side, the arrow keys, PageUp and PageDown, space, Home and End, or a swipe. The keys are left alone whenever the pointer is over a figure, because a sketch in walk mode uses the arrows itself. A section in the contents, a link to a figure and a footnote marker all turn to the page they are on.

Scrolling is one column with the notes out in the margin. A window too small for a spread falls back to it by itself, and goes back to the book when there is room again.

The same markdown makes both, and the same markdown prints: the pages are flattened back into one flow, so nothing has to be written twice and nothing can be true on screen and not on paper.

Where a page break falls

Each page is a two-column box of a fixed height and the browser breaks the paragraphs across its two columns itself. lib/book.js only decides which blocks land on which page: it pours one block at a time and asks after each whether the page still fits. A heading left alone at the foot of a page goes forward with the paragraph under it. A width=wide figure spans both columns; a width=full figure takes a page of its own, usually the one facing the text that introduces it, and a width=spread figure takes the whole spread.

#Windows

The pages are sheets of near-black laid over a room that runs the whole time the document is open. A window is a hole cut in the sheet: the reader looks straight through to the room, and the floating skin figure comes and stands in one now and then to watch them read, then goes off again.

:::window width=wide aspect="21 / 9"
An optional caption.
:::

A figure is a window too. While its sketch is not running there is nothing in the opening but the room — which is why there is no still sitting in it on screen. Press start and the piece takes the opening over; give the figure up (scroll far enough away) and the room comes back.

The page slider in the top bar sets how solid the sheet is, from a quarter opaque to a wall. The windows stay windows at every setting.

#Finding your way

The contents are a figure, not a list. Every section of the document is a circle, laid on a lattice whose spacing is the radius, so each circle passes through its neighbours' centres: one place is a cell, seven is the Seed of Life, nineteen the Flower. The disc at a centre fills when you have read that place; the lens between two circles fills once you have read both, which is the membrane the two cells share. Click a centre to go there.

Turning to another chapter does not leave the page: the chapter is fetched and only the text is replaced, so the room behind the pages — and whatever she is in the middle of — carries on.

#Posters and print

Every figure needs a still, for three readers: the one printing it, the one whose browser has no WebGL, and the one who has scrolled far enough that the figure has given up its context.

  1. Run the dev server and open the chapter.
  2. Set the figure up the way it should be seen.
  3. Press poster in the figure's bar — it is only there on the dev server.
  4. Move the downloaded fig-….jpg into thesis/posters/ and build again.

The build names the poster it wants for every figure that has none. In print the poster replaces the figure and the live address is printed beneath the caption, so nothing in the argument is reachable only on a screen. print in the top bar is the same as the browser's own print, and saving as PDF from that dialog is what to hand a committee.
