## 1. The etymology

**stim ← self-stim ← self-stimulation ← stimulation.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Merriam-Webster** | First known use of *stimming* **1983**; "probably short for earlier *self-stimming*, from **self-stim(ulation) + -ing**" |
| **Dictionary.com** | Dates *stim* to **1980-85**, a shortening |
| **The clinical parent term** | "self-stimulatory behavior", in use in autism research from the 1960s-70s |
| **The behaviours** | Described by **Kanner (1943)** and Asperger before any of these words existed |

### The source document, and it is free

> **Lovaas, O. I., Newsom, C. & Hickman, C. (1987). "Self-Stimulatory Behavior and Perceptual Reinforcement." *Journal of Applied Behavior Analysis* 20(1), 45-68.** DOI 10.1901/jaba.1987.20-45. **Open**, PMC1285951.

**The title is the argument you are looking for.** The behaviourist account of stimming is that it is reinforced by *the perception it produces* — self-generated stimulus as its own reward. That is the same structure as proprioception, from the other side of the value judgement.

Its immediate rebuttal and reply are also free: Lewis, Baumeister & Mailman, "A Neurobiological Alternative to the Perceptual Reinforcement Hypothesis" (JABA 20(3), 253-258), and Newsom & Lovaas's rejoinder (259-262).

Lovaas's own position on the behaviours, from a 1974 interview: "before you can get very far with developing normal social behaviors, you have to **eliminate** these aberrant behaviours," including hand flapping, rocking and spinning, using aversives.

### The reclamation

> **Kapp, S. K., Steward, R., Crane, L., Elliott, D., Elphick, C., Pellicano, E. & Russell, G. (2019). "'People should be allowed to do what they like': Autistic adults' views and experiences of stimming." *Autism* 23(7), 1782-1792.** DOI 10.1177/1362361319829628. **Open**, PMC6728747. 32 autistic adults.

Also: **Walker, N. (2021). *Neuroqueer Heresies*.** And **Walker & Raymaker, "Toward a Neuroqueer Future: An Interview with Nick Walker," *Autism in Adulthood* 3(1), 5-10**, DOI 10.1089/aut.2020.29014.njw. And **Bakan, M., "Being Applied in the Ethnomusicology of Autism"** (Oxford Handbooks, 2015), DOI 10.1093/oxfordhb/9780199351701.013.11 — stimming and musicality.

## 3. The pivot, already in your corpus

38 passages have `stimul*` within 300 characters of proprioception or kinaesthesia. This is the one:

> "I am attracted to the term kinaesthesia through Dee Reynolds' use of proprioceptors. Dee Reynolds defines 'proprioception' as **'the sensing of one's own position and movement stimuli from within the body, through sense receptors in the muscles, joints, tendons and inner ear as distinct from exteroception, the detection of environmental events through receptors in the eyes, ears and skin'** (Reynolds 2012: 17). My creative practice..."

— from `Contemporary photographic landscape practice`, in `ScholarDownloads_Braidotti`.

**Proprioception is defined as stimuli from within the body, as distinct from stimuli received from outside.** Self-stimulation is defined the same way, and valued oppositely. Lovaas's "perceptual reinforcement" and Reynolds' "movement stimuli from within" describe one structure.

Two more, both in art practice:

- ***Art and Dance in Dialogue: Body, Space, Object*** — eating: "anticipation of flavour and texture stimulated further by the smell, to the **proprioception** of the contact of the lips and tongue with the temperature, texture and firmness"
- **Barrett & Bolt (eds.), *Practice as Research: Approaches to Creative Arts Enquiry*** — dancers working from touch: "the reception, memory and associations of these complex **stimuli**... the act of one person giving the tactile information and another receiving it"

All 38 passages are in `stimulation_pass.txt` in my working directory if you want the rest.
