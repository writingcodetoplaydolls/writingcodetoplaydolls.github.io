## 1. Appalachia: the claim is overturned

I wrote, twice and emphatically, that Haraway's Camille Stories were "the only substantive Appalachian siting in the entire corpus," and called it "the most robust" finding of the sweep.

It is wrong. In the folder, in `ScholarDownloads_newmaterial_posthuman_arted_makerspace`:

> **Thorp, Heather Anne (2022). *Ongoing Curiosities in Post-Formal Education: Making Kin, Becoming Kin, and Becoming Kin(d) With/In a Story Family Sojourn.* EdD dissertation, Appalachian State University, Educational Leadership.**

15,747 words extracted. Counts: **Appalachia 26, Haraway 86, Barad 53**.

From the abstract:

> "In a year-long inquiry, called a Story Family Sojourn, women participants who had completed their formal education met with each other in **the Southern Appalachian Mountains**. The purpose of this **expressive arts-based inquiry** was to explore what would emerge through knowing by making together and thinking/theorizing together with/in a Story Family composed of women, time, expressive arts, and storyplace. This inquiry is based in a theoretical framing of **feminist posthumanism, feminist new materialism**."

The sites are named: the Blue Ridge Parkway, Elk Knob, Mount Jefferson, the Mountains to Sea Trail, Bass Lake on the Moses Cone Estate, Price Lake. She takes "story place" from Haraway 2016, p. 39, and uses it 37 times.

### And it is not one dissertation, it is a cluster

Her committee:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Brooke Anne Hofsess** (chair) | Art educator at Appalachian State. Already surfaced in the first sweep, in an arts-based-research contributor note: "immersed in aesthetic and poetic approaches to inquiry" |
| **Alecia Jackson** | Of **Jackson and Mazzei**, *Thinking with Theory*, "Plugging One Text Into Another". One of the central figures in post-qualitative inquiry, and already cited across your corpus, including by Ravisankar |
| **Sally Atkins** | Founder of the Expressive Arts Collaborative at Appalachian State |

**There is a feminist-posthumanist, expressive-arts, post-qualitative research programme located in the Southern Appalachians**, and one of its members is a name you have already been reading as theory.

That changes the Appalachian node entirely. It is not an unoccupied position you bring to posthumanism from outside. It has an institution, a committee, a method and a dissertation two years old.

**The difference you still have:** Thorp's own line, in the Invocation, is that "only one of the women grew up here." Hers is a study of a place she moved to, with women who mostly moved to it. Yours is from there. That distinction is now the thing to make, rather than the presence of Appalachia at all.

Across the full 4.76 million OCR'd words, "Appalachia" occurs 39 times in 12 documents. Most are still incidental (the Appalachian Trail, author affiliations). Thorp is the substantive one, and Hofsess's affiliation is the thread.

## 2. Transposition now has a definition, in a research-creation thesis

Section 6 of the earlier findings lists transposition as having "zero definitional hits in 13.8 million words." Already corrected once (the *Transpositions* volume analysed in the parent folder). Now corrected again, from inside this folder:

> **Vacirca, Dario. *The Capital: Transforming Systems of Power and Personal Horizons of Possibility through Radical Cultural Praxis.*** Research-creation PhD, in `ScholarDownloads_newmaterial_posthuman_arted_makerspace`. 14,249 words extracted, 8 uses of transposition.

The definition, Braidotti's, stated plainly:

> "This is the very basis of what Braidotti would call transpositions, **repeating and evolving an idea through dynamic repetition and various perspectives.** Through transposition these various elements belie the contemporary experience of subjectivity via seriality and become lenses from which to explore other possibilities of who we might be/come."

And the whole method set, in a footnote:

> "Braidotti's Nomadic theory and methodology calls for eight key precepts: **disidentification, transpositional thinking and practice, multi-perspectivism, transdisciplinarity, conceptual diversity, non-linearity, process-ontology, practical method.**"

That is eight named precepts, including transposition *and* transdisciplinarity, attributed to Braidotti, inside a research-creation thesis whose methodology chapter has a section called "Transpositions and Nomadic Thinking."

It is a secondary source, so it points rather than cites. But it names the structure your chapter 2 is working with, and it shows the term operating as method in a practice thesis.

## 3. Making strange has its lineage, and the practice-research version of it

I wrote that "the Russian Formalist lineage (Shklovsky, *ostranenie*) and the Brechtian one (*Verfremdung*) are **not in the corpus at all**."

Wrong. In:

> **"From rational to relational: more-than-human perspectives on crafting technology."** PhD thesis in the same subfolder, 16,306 words, 7 uses.

> "**Viktor Shklovskij first wrote about defamiliarisation as a literary tool used to make the reader see something they take for granted in a new light.** The method has since become more widespread and is used as a tool to expose opportunities in **HCI design**. In particular, it is often used to uncover and challenge user attitudes and assumptions."

> "**Craft practice research approaches defamiliarisation slightly differently. Here it is often used as a strategy to engage critically with the researcher's material practice.**"

Three registers of the same term in one paragraph: the literary origin, the HCI use, and the **craft-practice-research** use where the object of defamiliarisation is *the researcher's own practice*. That last one is your use.

It also happens to be an HCI thesis, which is the transdisciplinary point from section 4 of the earlier findings, arriving from the other direction.

## 4. Autism: three documents that were invisible at 56% coverage

Across all 4.76 million OCR'd words: autism and neurodiversity terms 270 hits in 28 documents. Three matter.

### Wexler, "dis assembly: Collaborative Rituals with an Autistic Artist"

In the **complete *Journal of Cultural Research in Art Education*, vol. 39 (2022)**, themed issue "Rethinking Ritual Ecologies," eds. Cala Coats and Amanda Alexander. 37,661 words; the file is named for a different article in it.

Alice Wexler (SUNY New Paltz) in conversation with **Estée Klar**, founder of dis assembly, **Adam Wolfond** (Klar's son, a non-speaking autistic poet), and mentors Chris Martin, Ellen Bleiwas, Jessamyn Polson. Klar 38 mentions, Wolfond 45.

The editors' description:

> "**dis assembly, a neurodiverse art collective that explores movement and the ritualized use of objects and the environment as performative modes for learning other ways of navigating the world.** The collective supports and facilitates communication for non-speaking autists through ground-breaking experimental work."

**A neurodiverse art collective whose practice is movement and the ritualised use of objects**, written up in an art education journal. That is as close to stimming-as-art-practice as anything in the corpus, and the word is never used.

It also closes a loop: in the Manning and Bozalek conversation Manning says "here I learn a lot from Estee Klar and Adam Wolfond." They are the same people.

The issue's framing is useful on its own: "rituals... can also take form through individual habits, social norms, or superstitious routines... In this sense, **rituals are political**. They can act as non-linguistic signifiers of one's social status."

### Trento, "Protocols for a Procedural Space for Failing"

> **Trento, Francisco B.** University of the Arts Helsinki, CERADA. 6,931 words, 60 autism and neurodiversity hits.

> "This article presents **a glossary of protocols for dis(abling) artistic research in academic institutions** to activate a forum for institutional critique. It focuses on crafting spaces that foreground non-ableist modes of existence and socialities. The protocols welcome useless failures, not feeding a neoliberal discourse of coaching."

Their biography: "Their work emphasises **non-neurotypical queer modalities of perception**, critically examining techniques to make the university and arts education environments less ableist." Cites Yergeau and Egner on "neuroqueer body-minds."

A neurodivergent artistic-research methodology, written as **protocols**, which is the same form as Manning's numbered procedures.

### Jonker, *Towards Response-able Arts-based Practices in Higher Education*

This is the most important single document the OCR recovered, and the nearest neighbour to your project found so far.

> **Jonker, François (2024).** PhD, **Utrecht University** joint doctorate with the **University of the Western Cape**. Defended 4 October 2024.
> Supervisors: **Kathrin Thiele** and **Vivienne Bozalek**.
> Examination committee includes **Erin Manning**.

Counts in the 14,519 words extracted: **response-able 48, Barad 43, Manning 35, diffraction 25, arts-based 23, crip 16, Haraway 13, autism 7, cartography 7, Trinh 4, *agencement* 4, Braidotti 3**.

**It is the only document in the entire corpus that uses your apparatus and Manning and autism and arts-based practice at once.** Nothing else does all four.

Its chapter 2 is titled:

> **"Cripqueering Method in Posthuman Educational Research: Diffractive writing-with A/autisms"**

with sections **2.5.1 "Autistic Perception: on the More-than of Observation"** and **2.5.2 "Autistic Voicing: on the More-than of Representationalist Articulation."** Chapter 3 is **"Choreographic Cartographies with-in Learning."**

Two things to note about the supervision. **Kathrin Thiele** is the Thiele of Kaiser and Thiele (2014), the three-page diffraction genealogy your section 1.5 uses as the example of an origin story that omits Trinh. **Vivienne Bozalek** is the Bozalek of the Manning conversation. This dissertation sits exactly where your chapters 1 and 2 sit, supervised by people already in your bibliography.

**Where it is not your project.** Jonker's site is the institution: higher education pedagogy and assessment at the Cape Town Creative Academy, where he is a founding partner and academic head. His object is "how else to do higher education research, pedagogy and assessment," and his situated position is institutional privilege and responsibility, not first-person practice. Yours is your own art practice and your own perception. Same apparatus, different object.

It needs reading in full before chapter 2 is written.
