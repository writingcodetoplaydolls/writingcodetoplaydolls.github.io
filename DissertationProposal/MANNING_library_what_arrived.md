## 3. Where Manning's autism work comes from, in her own account

She says it twice, in two different interviews, and the account is the same both times.

From the arq interview (2024):

> "I didn't set out to theorise autism or become a theorist of neurodiversity. It happened much more organically. I had been trying to write about movement... Dancers have this incredible intelligence around movement, but it's not an intelligence that requires words for the most part... I wanted to find ways of articulating movement's own mode of thought, to see whether there was a way to grow a philosophy from the movement itself.
>
> In that context, I saw Mel Baggs's video 'In My Language', and that changed the whole conversation... When I wrote about the video in my book *Relationscapes*, I actually didn't write about autism."

From Manning & Bozalek (2024):

> "I watched Mel Baggs's video *In my Language* and saw thought in the act! I didn't think much about their autism at that time, my concern was with how the movement of sensation at the heart of their thinking shifted the whole question of thought and language."

That is where the title of *Thought in the Act* comes from.

Her sources are named repeatedly and are mostly autistic writers and bloggers rather than clinical literature: **Mel Baggs** (*Ballastexistenz*), **Tito Mukhopadhyay**, **DJ Savarese**, **Ralph Savarese**, **Ido Kedar**, **Jim Sinclair**, **Anne Corwin** (*Existence is Wonderful*), **Estee Klar** (*The Joy of Autism*) and **Adam Wolfond**, **Lydia X. Z. Brown** (*Autistic Hoya*), **Amy Sequenzia**, the *neuroqueer* blog.

## 4. The definition, and the constraint that comes with it

This is the sentence to read before using any of it. From *Always More Than One*:

> "let me repeat: **autistic perception is a tendency in perception on a continuum with all perception, not a definition of autism.** It is a style that has been remarked upon by the many autistics whose voices we've heard throughout, a perceptual style that actively thinks-feels the edgings and contourings of fields of relation coagulating into instances of shaped experience."

And from the Bozalek conversation, the same refusal in the register of identity:

> "neurodiversity is a mode that cannot be reduced to something someone 'is.' When we reduce the person to something they 'are' in contradistinction to a norm, what we are doing is imposing a pathological logic... **Neurotypicality is a systemic baseline according to which modes of self-presentation and modes of knowing are policed.**"

> "My aim is not to relativize. Rather, it is to refuse the neurotypical as the counterpoint."

**The constraint.** Manning deliberately detaches autistic perception from autistic people and puts it on a continuum available to everyone. That gives you a non-pathologising concept with real philosophical weight behind it. It also cuts against a standpoint claim: if autistic perception is a tendency in all perception, then being autistic is not what licenses your access to it, and a section built on *your* autism as the ground of *your* method has to say how it relates to a concept that explicitly refuses that grounding.

This is the same shape as the Ravisankar problem, and it is answerable rather than fatal. Manning herself says in the Bozalek piece, "When I started writing about neurodiversity, I never considered my own experience, it just never occurred to me to center myself in the conversation," and only later discloses having "spent time in psychiatric institutions." The gap between a philosopher theorising a perceptual tendency from other people's accounts and a researcher working from inside her own is the opening, and Manning has as good as named it.

One more thing to know before citing: the endnotes to chapter 4 of *Always More Than One* contain the phrase "those who **suffer from** motor activation problems such as autism" (n. 13). Her own vocabulary is not uniformly non-pathologising.

## 5. What this closes, and what it opens

**Closes:** the Manning gap. Your section 2.6 bullet said "To search: Whitehead in posthuman and new materialist texts... Manning, E. (2009). *Relationscapes*." You now have the Whitehead line running through Manning across six books, and the prehension concentration is in Immediation vol. 1 (30 occurrences) and *Thought in the Act* (11).

**Closes:** the claim in `SWEEP_FINDINGS` section 2 that nothing in the corpus is both posthumanist and about neurodivergence. That claim died with Ivinson & Renold 2022; it is now not even close.

**Opens, and this one is new:** `Heiredal, T. S. & Manning, E. (2024). "Exploring autistic perception in architecture." *arq: Architectural Research Quarterly* 28(1), 95-103.` DOI 10.1017/S1359135525000041.

Heiredal is an autistic architect and photographer. Figure 1 of the interview shows **a Hasselblad with the film case removed and cardboard squares fitted between body and film case to mask the frame**, which he uses to make multiple-exposure photographs "representing his autistic perception."

**An autistic artist modifying a camera as a method of rendering autistic perception, published in an architecture journal, in conversation with Manning.** Your Chapter 1 opens on "Camera" and "The 110 Camera." This is the closest thing yet found to what that chapter is doing, and unlike the Ivinson and Renold camera chapter it is first-person and the camera is the artist's own.

Manning in it defines autistic perception as "a kind of slowness, a quality of perception edging in, not to be categorised but experienced."

## 6. Stimming is still absent, and the denominator is now large

Across these 895,000 words, "stim" / "stimming" occurs **three times in total**, none of them definitional: once in a *Frontiers in Sociology* article, once in *Always More Than One*, once in *Politics of Affect*.

Added to the earlier sweeps, the word does not appear in any substantive sense in roughly **16.3 million words** of posthuman, new materialist and now neurodiversity-adjacent philosophy, outside Yergeau.

That is no longer a gap in your reading. It is a finding about the literature, with a denominator, and it is the strongest available argument that the methodological claim is a contribution rather than a repetition. Proprioception is thinly served too: 6 in *Always More Than One*, 7 in *Thought in the Act*, 7 in Immediation vol. 1.
