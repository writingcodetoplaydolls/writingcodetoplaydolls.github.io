**Quantum 55 in 68,849 words, against Derrida 201 and language 273.** This is a Derrida book about nature, culture, language and mathematics. "Quantum" in the title names a problematic, not a physics content.

The preface says what the problematic is. Kirby takes Derrida's "open system" and *différance* to their logical conclusion, so that particularisms (subjects, objects, words, methodologies, systems) lose their outlines as entities that relate to each other with causal effect, and can be read instead as expressions of the same phenomenon. Because the reader and writer of this "general text" is then not located in a human agent, the questions physics confronts about determination, agency, causality, space-time involvement and "spooky" entanglement are, in her words, all operative. That is the whole move, stated by p. ix.

## 2. The four passages worth having

### (a) The scale objection, answered, with page cites into Barad. Chapter 1, around pp. 3-5

This is the most immediately useful thing in the book. Kirby confronts the standard objection directly: the received wisdom that the minute scale of quantum behaviour can have no application in the macroscopic world of human affairs. She answers it by quoting Barad twice:

- Barad (2007), **p. 270**: "the question of macroscopic quantum states is not an idle matter"
- Barad (2007), **p. 291**: on how little attention Bell's theorem received for years after 1964, which Barad reads as a measure of the lack of interest in foundational issues "in a resolutely neo-positivist period"

She pairs that with the observation that physicists themselves ignored the implications of entanglement even as Bell's inequalities began appearing in 1964. Her point is structural: the quarantining of quantum implications from ordinary life and the quarantining of deconstruction inside a few humanities departments are the same gesture of controlling difficulty.

**Why it matters.** The published critiques of this field say its practitioners sidestep the scale and measurement problems. Here is a 2011 Duke monograph meeting the scale objection head on, with two specific page references into *Meeting the Universe Halfway*. Those two Barad pages are now the two most worth reading in that book for 1.5, and neither is currently cited.

### (b) The skin passage. Chapter 4, around p. 75

The nearest thing in either held text to the permeability question. Kirby asks the reader to imagine being without skin, able to witness the body's otherwise invisible processes while chatting, reading aloud, typing, or in an intense exchange with someone cared about, and says we might then have to concede that the "meat of the body is thinking material."

It is a thought experiment, not an empirical claim, and the skin is removed in it rather than examined. But it is the one place where the skin is explicitly the thing that keeps the body's processes from counting as thought. Set against the stretch-mark material in `PERMEABLE_BODIES_research_notes.md`, where the skin is what keeps the record, these are two opposite jobs for the same organ.

### (c) The entanglement sentence. Chapter 4, around p. 76

The sharpest statement of the apparatus argument in either book, and Kirby flags it herself as of crucial importance to her argument. Reading Barad: if the object under investigation, the inquiring scientist and the apparatus are entangled, that does not mean three entities interacting. Entanglement means the ontology of the entities emerges through relationality, and **the entities do not preexist their involvement.** Which implies, as she puts it, that the original intention to perform the experiment cannot be attributed to a human inquirer.

She then extends it to time: linear causal unfolding can appear synchronised at the quantum level, with experiments that appear to anticipate what will already have taken place, and results retrospectively actualised and empirically verifiable.

**Why it matters.** The grating definition from comps has the apparatus co-created with the material. This passage says the harder version: not co-created, but not prior to the investigation at all, the intention included. That is a sentence to argue with in 1.5 rather than adopt.

### (d) Forensic facial reconstruction. Chapter 2

Chapter 2 works on building a face from a skull: cephalometric points, each one a differential between the skull's bony proportions and the contours of the skin's surface, with the topography between the sites interpolated through the artist's feel and anatomical knowledge. Later, from an interview with a practitioner, the observation that **the skin seems to be the organ where art and science meet**, and that science has placed its primary investment below the surface.

This is an art-and-measurement chapter that nobody would find by searching for quantum. It bears directly on the `FaceMorph` work in `K:\ResearchRepository\Dissertation_Hub`, and it is an instrument that produces a face, alongside the voltage dye that finds a face before the face exists in `PERMEABLE_BODIES_research_notes.md`.

## 3. Chapter 6, and the porosity route that is not physics

Chapter 6 reads Irigaray with Merleau-Ponty. Merleau-Ponty 109 and Irigaray 102 are almost all in this chapter, and flesh 61 with them.

Kirby quotes Merleau-Ponty's *The Visible and the Invisible* on a "new type of being" that is "a being by porosity, pregnancy, or generality," where the one before whom the horizon opens is included within it, and body and distances participate in one corporeity "even beyond the horizon, beneath his skin, unto the depths of being" (1968, 149). Also a question "consonant with the porous being which it questions," which obtains not an answer but a confirmation of its astonishment (1968, 102). Kirby's reading of Irigaray is sharply critical: that Irigaray erases what is extraordinary in this generalised porosity of the "two lips" by treating it as masculinist theft.

**Porosity and pregnancy, in the same phrase, in Merleau-Ponty.** This is the phenomenology route to the permeable body, and it is a different route from the physics one. It is also the only place in either held text where porosity is a concept rather than a passing word. Three uses in the whole book, and they are here.

- Merleau-Ponty, M. *The Visible and the Invisible* (1968), pp. 102 and 149. **Not held**

## 4. A defence worth keeping

In the preface, Kirby works through John Protevi's *Political Physics* (2001) as the exemplary statement of the view that deconstruction has nothing to say about physical reality. She notes that Protevi cites Sokal and Bricmont from the science wars, and that they in fact **absolve Derrida**, on the grounds that there is no systematic misuse of, or indeed attention to, science in his work (Protevi 2001, 5).

That is a usable citation. The science-wars objection is the one a committee is most likely to raise, and here is its own authors' exemption, quoted by a Duke monograph.

## 5. The separate finding: two different Kirby books are held

`What if Culture Was Nature All Along_ - Vicki Kirby.pdf`, also at top level, is **not** chapter 4 of this book despite the nearly identical title. It is the 2017 **edited collection** of that name, Kirby as editor, Edinburgh University Press, 106,760 words, in the **New Materialisms** series edited by Iris van der Tuin and Rosi Braidotti.

Its editorial advisory board includes Karen Barad, Barbara Bolt, **Felicity Colman**, Manuel DeLanda and Brian Massumi. So the two quantum-feminist texts in this folder are linked by a series board, and Bolt, who is in the folder already as co-editor of *Practice as Research*, is on it too.

That collection is a third text and it is unread. It has been in this folder since 10 March 2026, so its 106,760 words were almost certainly swept into the 21 million in `SWEEP_FINDINGS_2026-10-27.md`; what has not happened is anyone telling the two books apart. Any count of "Kirby" in this folder has been pooling a 2011 monograph with a 2017 edited collection by the same author on a near-identical title.
