## 1. The project, and the fact that the teachers did it too

The project was called **"Self, Local, Global"**, run **April to June 2016** with **twelve Grade 10 learners** at the Tygerberg Art Centre, integrated into the visual arts course as the second-term practical assignment. It asked the learners "to engage with themselves in their situatedness in local contexts", and the activities and "their effects and affects were recorded and responded to by the learners in various forms of writing, ranging from scientific, factual reporting to creative writing; and art making, ranging from drawings, prints and 'earthwork' interventions to collages, montages and paintings." **Each learner kept a compulsory resource book** recording "ideas, thinking and writing processes, preparatory work and experiences."

It was adapted from her own master's coursework assignment for Grade 11s, and **designed not to be prescriptive**: "The process was intended to be open-ended, and any learner who discovered something in the process that he/she wanted to pursue or who wished to deviate from the guidelines was encouraged to do so."

**And the teachers did the project alongside the learners**, which she records in her own journal, dated, with the difficulty left in:

> "I decided this morning while reading a paper on public pedagogy that I was going to create new data from scratch by doing the project with the Grade 10's. And **we ([my colleague] and I) are going to do the project with the learners** . . . I found/find it very difficult to come up with anything that is not somehow prescriptive, or pre-planned or 'worked out' to achieve particular outcomes. **I seek: open-endedness, searching-in-between, acceptance of ambiguity, uncertainty. However, I find that I battle to express it, is it even possible? I don't have the words; I'm out of my depth** . . ." (29 March 2016)

She introduces that quotation as demonstrating "**the qualms and difficulties I experienced in terms of achieving the aim of open-endedness while having an agenda grounded in particular theories**, and of not knowing where the research might take me."

## 2. She refuses coding, and gives the reason

This is the part of the dissertation that is most directly useful, because the refusal is argued rather than asserted.

> "my decision . . . **not to employ qualitative research methodologies that rely on coding and broad, generalised themes** in my analysis of the project described above. It is my opinion that such an analysis **does not lend itself so easily to searching for the 'in-between', for elusive or unexpected insights while courting ambiguity.**"

And she is careful not to turn it into a dismissal: "my intention and concern is certainly not to critique these methods and disparage excellent work that is produced following them, I will refrain from further discussion of their 'unsuitability'."

**The procedure she puts in their place, stated twice.** From chapter 3:

> "I selected specific '**segments**' of data that seemed to '**speak' to me**, and attempted to read them through the theoretical perspectives. I also in some instances attempted to **read data through data, and theory through theory**, in other words, data and theory became inextricably entangled in a variety of different ways."

And from the data-analysis section, with the self-accounting attached:

> "I selected small '**cuts**' of data that presented themselves as meaningful to me and tried to read them in **their specific and nuanced details and their manifold shifting entanglements, rather than to fit them into broader, generalised themes**, while always being mindful of my own inseparability of what I was attempting to observe, in order to **pry open nuances of meanings that might otherwise have become lost**. In a sense, **the analysis was experimental in that it was done according to my own interpretation of what a post-qualitative, diffractive analysis might entail in practice**."

**The sources behind the refusal**, all with pages, and several of them new to this folder:

## 3. How she justifies selecting, which is the question 2.2 keeps running into

Her answer is that the selection is itself an agential cut, and she says so:

> "the insights or knowledge that might be gained from a diffractive analysis of these entities reflect the agential intra-actions and cuts that created these entities, and that therefore **knowledge will always be incomplete, different 'cuts' produce different knowledges**. Furthermore, as I decided to choose from all the material for analysis available to me only a few entities that resonated with me, **I performed, as researcher who is understood to be always already entangled in the phenomenon being analysed, further 'agential cuts'. This implies, once again, that that which had been excluded is still there, waiting to come to 'matter'** (in more than one sense of the word). Meanwhile, the entities that I analysed became phenomena in themselves. I therefore argue that **analysis is a continuous, iterative, entangled process** . . . never totally complete, and never done and established once and for all, always with open-ended possibilities for new 'becomings'."

Before that she names what the phenomenon consisted of, which is the part that makes the cut auditable: "the assignments, the learners, the teachers, the emerging concepts, the environment, the art and other materials which, through specific perspectives, choices and actions (agential cuts) made 'visible' particular material-discursive entities, various artworks, performances, events, **to the exclusion of others**."

**Chapter 4's epigraph belongs in the citation file as a found quotation.** Einstein on Bohr: "**He utters his opinions like one perpetually groping and never like one who believes himself to be in possession of definite truth**" (Barad 2007: 121).

## 4. The researcher's own insecurity, written into the method

Section 4.2.1 is titled "The researcher: Thoughts and feelings", and it is the strongest first-person methodological writing found in this folder, because the insecurity is not confessed and then set aside: it becomes the starting point.

> "mindful of **Gayatri Spivak's (2012) injunction that truthfulness is a necessary quality for an educator** (which in my case is entangled with my role as researcher), **I have to confess that I felt insecure, uncertain, unmoored and destabilised, fearing that I might be writing a dissertation that went nowhere and came up with nothing.** However, **Alice Fulton** (cited in Barad 2007: 39) remarked that "**nothing will unfold for us unless we move towards what looks to us like nothing**", and as I considered this sense of lack of certainty, of not-knowing, of indeterminacy, **I began to realise that for the kind of post-qualitative analysis I aimed to do . . . this might not be a bad position to find myself in.**"

> "Therefore, I concluded that **I should not view the situation that I was experiencing as the impasse and stumbling block that my first instincts made me inclined to believe it to be. Rather, it in fact already offered me a starting point for the employment of diffraction as a way of reading**, if I read it through the notions of Barad and others about identity and the 'I'."

**The sources she reads it through**, and two of them are a/r/tography, which this folder does not have:

**Springgay, Irwin and Kind (2005: 902)** on "a life of inquiry, where 'issues in question may permeate a life'", as being one of "**dis/comfort**" and anxiety, where the researcher, "in engaging in a '**continual process of not-knowing**', is often '**unable to come to conclusions or settle into a linear pattern of inquiry**'". **Jackson and Mazzei (2010: 10)** on how "**the 'I' of the researcher is always becoming in the process of researching, listening, and writing**", and **Hekman**, cited there, calling the "'I' . . . **a mangle of multiple elements**". And **Barad (2012: 32)**: "**identity is a phenomenal matter; it is not an individual affair. Identity is multiple within itself; or rather, identity is diffracted through itself, identity is diffraction/différance/differing/deferring/differentiating.**"

Her own gloss on that is a list rather than a position, and it is the part worth noticing as a formal device: her identities are "**struggling researcher, immersed teacher, insecure doubter, hesitant writer, cautiously optimistic thinker, jubilant discoverer** (when some bright insight flames up for a moment or two), and other indeterminate identities not yet thought of, **and with all the adjectives being interchangeable with one another**".

**One citation to check:** "Barad 2012: 32" is not identified by title. `Comps\Used texts` holds two Barad 2012 items, the dOCUMENTA nothingness essay and "Nature's Queer Performativity", and the plan already records that "Barad 2012b" names different texts for different authors. **The quotation should be located in one of the two held texts before it is used.**

## 5. Tuana at second hand, with pages, and one word to notice

Section 2.3.4 is a clean exposition of **Tuana (2008: 188-200)**, useful because it has the page numbers the plan's own Tuana row does not carry for every claim:

> "**Viscous porosity is a conceptual metaphor** that assists in a better understanding of interactionist ontology, which denotes the 'rich interactions [or, in Barad's parlance, intra-actions] between beings [and forces] through which subjects are constituted out of relationality' (Tuana 2008: 188)."

> the dualistic separations of "the natural from human production, the biological from the cultural, genes from their environments and the material from the semiotic" cause trouble once it is clear that these divisions are "**both permeable and shifting**" (189); bodies, not only human bodies, are not "fixed or inert" but "**fluid and emergent**", and differences are "fluid, evolving, and contextual" (189).

> "When we do make distinctions, it is done for particular purposes at particular times, which means that **we take epistemic responsibility, and therefore also ethical responsibility, for the various effects (such as how they enable and limit possibilities, and for whom) of the distinctions we bring to bear**" (192).

> The two halves: "'porosity' [means] that **the distinctions we employ do not signify a natural or unchanging boundary**", and "'viscosity' [draws] attention to the complex ways in which **material agency is often involved in inter-(or intra-)actions that include, but are not limited to, human agency**" (194).

With Katrina as Tuana's case (194-195): climate change from human activity, New Orleans built below sea level, its historical origins, political decisions that perpetuate poverty, bad infrastructure maintenance, poor waste management, environmental degradation.

**The word to notice is "metaphor".** Kotzé calls viscous porosity "**a conceptual metaphor**". That is her framing, not Tuana's, and it is a sixth position in the metaphor cluster assembled last week: a term used to make an ontology comprehensible. Recorded here, not adjudicated.

She also carries a second Alaimo source the folder does not use: trans-corporeality as the "material interchanges" across bodies and the material world, giving "a new material and posthumanist sense of the human as substantially and perpetually interconnected with the flow of substances and the agencies of environments" (**Alaimo 2012: 476**, a different text from the 2008 *Material Feminisms* chapter).

## 6. The data, and the ethics paragraph

**What counted as data**, which is wider than the usual list and includes the room: twelve learners' informal discussions; her own informal observations, photographs and video; **her journal, kept "both as researcher and as participant in the project"**; recorded informal discussions with her colleague; the learners' research, photographs, activities, writings, drawings, printed works, collages, montages, paintings and collaborative artworks; "and also from entities such as **the classroom and the garden and sensory perceptions such as smell and sound**."

**The ethics paragraph is a model, and short enough to use as one.** Approval from the **Western Cape Education Department Directorate of Research** and the departmental ethics screening committee. Casual discussions electronically recorded. Voice recordings, video and consent forms "kept confidential and stored in a locked drawer in my classroom". **"The participants could request to look at the video footage or listen to the voice recordings at any stage."** All participants briefed, participation voluntary, consent forms signed by all, **no names used**, and the data **deleted a year after submission**.

## 7. The finding, and what it is worth

From the abstract: "The study showed that **before learners will begin to feel genuinely concerned about the destruction of the earth, they must first understand the materiality of their own bodies, and the latter's intra-active co-forming with all other matter. Yet theorists argue that current curricula still avoid the body as the seat of learning.**" And the diagnosis of why: curricula "are entrenched in humanism's dualistic thought patterns with a tendency towards fixed, predetermined answers and outcomes to problematic questions, which excludes the discovery of new possibilities."

Chapter 4's three analyses are: an event involving "**two teachers, two learners, a garden, objects and fire**"; a rhizomatic analysis of one learner's painting, with a "**line of flight: Waste**"; and **"the self-adornment activity: The body and the senses"**, which is the embodiment section. Those were read only in outline.

**What it is worth, stated plainly.** It is a master's dissertation and it does not claim more than it has: the analysis is "experimental" and done "according to my own interpretation of what a post-qualitative, diffractive analysis might entail in practice". **Its value here is not authority but demonstration**: it shows what the method looks like when carried out, what it is defended against, and how a practitioner accounts for her own cuts. **The reading plan has no other worked example.**
