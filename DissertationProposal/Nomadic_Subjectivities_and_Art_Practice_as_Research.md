# Research Documentation: Nomadic Subjectivities, Art Practice as Research, and Braidottian Cartographies
**Date:** October 16, 2026  
**Framework:** Rosi Braidotti (*Transpositions*, *The Posthuman*), bell hooks, Adrienne Rich, Karen Barad.

---

## 1. Defining Braidottian Cartography: Beyond the Mapping of Power

### The Dual Mission: Potestas and Potentia
A Braidottian cartography is not a passive diagnosis of systemic oppression or structural containment. Grounded in Spinozan monism, it functions as a theoretically-based and politically-informed reading of the present that executes a dual task:

1. **Mapping *Potestas* (Power as Entrapment):** Tracking how institutional, patriarchal, capitalist, and ableist power operates to marginalize, restrict, govern, and gate bodies. It establishes a rigorous *politics of location*—assessing exactly where a subject is materially, technologically, and historically situated.
2. **Activating *Potentia* (Power as Empowerment):** Locating lines of flight, leaks, and spaces for creative resistance. It treats the map as a dynamic, performative *figuration* designed to spark alternatives, pilot transformation, and hypothesize what *could be* (the virtual) rather than merely validating what *is* (the actual).

### Comparative Breakdown: Traditional Power Mapping vs. Braidottian Cartography

* **Traditional Power Mapping:** Focuses on Critical/Analytical lenses, views the subject as a passive product of structural forces, diagnoses domination (*potestas*), and remains anchored in past/present institutional structures.
* **Braidottian Cartography:** Integrates Critical and **Affirmative** lenses, views the subject as an active, relational **nomadic subject** endowed with agency, transforms entrapment into **empowerment** (*potentia*), and is geared toward producing **new futures** and becomings.

## 2. Scaling the Nomadic Subject to the Singular Art Practice

While Braidotti frequently operates at macro-political scales (global flows, technological shifts, nations, and cultures), applying the nomadic subject to a singular, individual art practice as research is methodologically sound through three conceptual bridges:

### The Singular as a "Point of Exit"
In a monistic philosophy, the macro and micro are continuous. The singular body or studio practice is not an isolated, solipsistic ego; it is an *embodied and embedded material location*. It represents a highly localized node where broader systemic forces intersect. The individual practice becomes a micro-political laboratory where collective structures are intercepted, disrupted, and re-routed.

### Autoethnography as the "A-Personal"
To prevent individual exploration from collapsing into purely personal confession, confession must give way to the *a-personal*. The researcher uses their own body and creative trajectory as a highly sensitive diagnostic instrument. The cartography maps how systemic friction, structural traumas, and cultural discourses pass *through* a singular body, converting individual history into a transversal tool.

### Art Practice as Material Velocity
Art operates outside the immediate domesticating capture of institutional language. The material, computational, or somatic repetitions within studio practice capture intense, pre-verbal, or non-human forces. While macro-political movements shift *potestas*, the singular artist shifts *potentia* at the molecular level, testing new ways of being (becomings) before they scale up to collective cultural formations.

## 3. The Paradox of Deconstruction & Slipping the Gates of Essentialism

### The Critical Fault Line (hooks vs. Eurocentric Deconstruction)
As bell hooks, Stuart Hall, and Gayatri Spivak noted, the celebratory "dissolution of the self" championed by dominant Western male theorists often emerges from absolute structural privilege. Those who have always safely possessed a stable, institutionalized "I" can afford to dismantle it. 

For the historical Other—who has been systematically denied a stable subjectivity, locked out of institutions, and reduced to pure, unthinking flesh—the demand to "deconstruct" can function as a secondary form of essentializing exclusion. It treats the marginalized as *naturally* or *mystically* external, masking violent socio-political enforcement as an inherent state of "pure embodiment."

### Tactical Navigation of Lived Exclusion
To deploy nomadic subjectivities and *becoming-minoritarian* to slip through the gates of essentialism without erasing real histories of trauma, the research frames the minoritarian position not as a static identity but as a *velocity*:

* **From Lack to Capacity:** The research refuses to let gatekeepers define the internal capacity of the subject. The art practice does not merely document the trauma of exclusion (which replicates containment); it maps the structural terrain of the gate to find material bypasses.
* **Minoritarian Agility:** Because the minoritarian subject has been systematically kept on the margins, they possess a hyper-precise cartographic knowledge of the cracks in the fortress wall. Nomadic subjectivity becomes a specialized methodology for navigating and evading rigid institutional grids.
* **The Counter-Gate:** The combined weight of autoethnography and creative production fulfills hooks's demand for the marginalized to move from the position of *spectacle* to the position of *theorist*. The creative act generates counter-knowledge, utilizing visual, somatic, or digital language to bypass linguistic capture.

## 4. Diffractive Reading and the Visceral Reclaiming of Agency

### Adrienne Rich: Wrestling with Language
The methodology enacts a visceral, defiant refusal to remain passive, echoing the lines from Adrienne Rich's *The Demon Lover* (1966):
> *"I will not be the girl the bull rode / all Rubens flesh and happy moans / but to be wrestled like a boy / with knees, hips, tongue, / with language..."*

Historically, the marginalized, traumatized, or neurodivergent body in art and philosophy is treated like Rubens' paintings: passive, silent, over-essentialized flesh to be looked at or governed by institutional forces. Bringing theory to one's own creative archive transforms the researcher from a spectacle into the primary theorist. 

### Transposition of a 25-Year Practice
By reading a 25-year artistic archive *diffractively* through newly accessed theoretical abstractions, the past work is relocated. It ceases to be a static monument to past injury or exclusion. Instead, the historical artwork and the current theoretical tool collide to create an active interference pattern. This process of diffractive transposition forces abstract, macro-political theory to account for a specific, somatic, and lived experience—making the language work, making the theory sweat, and staging an active, nomadic exit point in the present.
