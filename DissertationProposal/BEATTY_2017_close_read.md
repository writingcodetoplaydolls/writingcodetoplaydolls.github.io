## 2. The passage you came for, in full

> "I draw on the term kinaesthetic, as the adjective of kinaesthesia, relating to the sensory awareness of position and movement. Given my performative approach, I am concerned with how direct encounter through the material environment, the body and creative practice in turn affects various senses and impulses. In this way I am attracted to the term kinaesthesia through Dee Reynolds' use of proprioceptors. Dee Reynolds defines 'proprioception' as **'the sensing of one's own position and movement stimuli from within the body, through sense receptors in the muscles, joints, tendons and inner ear as distinct from exteroception, the detection of environmental events through receptors in the eyes, ears and skin'** (Reynolds 2012: 17)."

Her source is **Dee Reynolds (2012), p. 17** — Reynolds writes on dance and kinaesthesia; the exact work is not named in this passage and needs chasing.

Note the counts: **kinaesthesia 13, proprioception only 2.** She takes the definition and then works in the adjective. Your prose runs the other way: proprioception 7, kinaesthesia 0.

What she does with it:

> "My creative practice recognises an awareness of body positioning (within the landscape) and an awareness of the senses... The experience of moving through the landscape; walking, sitting, looking have been central to my approach of gathering an embodied awareness that I then take back to the darkroom and studio. **The residue of the movements and sensory, intuitive engagement with the landscape are re-experienced through the direct handling of the negatives and prints** in the studio and darkroom. The markings on the images are led by intuitive, reflexive movement. **The markings translate the trace evidence of being in the landscape and are the material link to the body, the landscape and my experience of being there.**"

That last sentence is a definition of a trace, by a practitioner, in a Barad thesis. Your 3.5 is Traces and 3.6 is Reading the traces.

## 3. Three moves worth taking

### The refusal of digital, argued

> "An important consideration in my methodology and practice was my rejection of working digitally... It was important to work with my hands in the studio as well as the field itself. Instead I felt digital invention here would confine 'physical' practice, to take away from an unexpected, accidental (the affective) element that occurs when working in the darkroom."

Analogue 6, digital 20, darkroom 43. The refusal is made as an argument about accident and affect, not as nostalgia.

### Duration as a property of the apparatus, not the artist

> "It occurs during moments of technical processes; for example, **the durational nature of light hitting the film on initial capture** of an image and then again through inter-negative process. It occurs during moments in the darkroom when projecting the image through the enlarger and **moving my body between the light and light-sensitive paper**."

Light, film, enlarger and body all take time, and the time is the same time. That is an apparatus argument made without the word.

### The exhibition as the argument

Part 1 of her exhibition is a list of actions: **"The walking. The meandering. The sitting. The looking. The stroking. The sorting. The dancing."**

Alongside them, unedited video:

> "The videos in the first section document field work and darkroom interventions. **They are not being considered works of art in themselves, but lay bare and document my process.** These videos have not been edited... Their role in the present context is **akin to field notes**."

And her field notes are hung in the room: "placed strategically to function as a road map for the exhibition... **numbered to suggest a narrative or argument that unfolds through the space**. The framing document uses the same numbering."

**The exegesis and the exhibition are cross-numbered so the argument runs through both.** For your 3.10 on form and deposit, that is a worked solution by someone who had to satisfy a committee. Field note 20 uses to your 1; exegesis 6 to your 0.

## 4. Where it breaks, and this is the useful part

Section 4.5 turns to **Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi's five stages of creativity** — preparation, incubation, insight, evaluation, elaboration — and maps her action list onto them. Csikszentmihalyi 10 uses.

> "Insight talks to that 'aha' moment when I eventually start using the camera."

A thesis that spends chapter 2 on agential realism, intra-action and situated knowledges resolves its own practice into a **cognitive-psychology model of the creative individual**, with stages, insight moments and a sovereign maker having them.

This is the Ravisankar problem again in a different costume. Ravisankar's artist returned through phenomenology; Beatty's returns through creativity psychology. **In both cases the apparatus is Baradian and the account of the maker is not.**

That is the third independent instance this corpus has produced of the thing your first-person chapter is about, and it is the strongest argument you have that the problem is structural rather than a failure of nerve in any one writer.

## 6. Cautions

**It is landscape photography in Tasmania**, four years in the Hartz Mountains. The site is hers and the tradition she argues with — Berger, Cosgrove, Mitchell, the picturesque, the colonial gaze — is landscape theory, not yours.

**No Deleuze to speak of** (5), no assemblage, no Trinh, no Whitehead. The lineage is Barad plus Bolt plus phenomenology plus ecofeminism.
