## 2. The 2013 article: what it sets out to do

Its own keywords, printed p.261, are "Deleuze, qualitative data analysis, poststructuralism, posthumanism".

Its project, printed p.261: "Drawing on six poststructural philosophers, we “plug in” the common data set and the theorists’ philosophical concepts. We read the same data across multiple theorists by plugging the theory and the data into one another."

**The six are named, printed p.264:** "The theorists who rhizomatically emerged were Derrida, Spivak, Foucault, Butler, Deleuze, and Barad." Five of those six are poststructural. The sixth is Barad, and the article places her at the end of a genealogy rather than at the start of a different project, printed p.265: "Continuing on this genealogical bent, Barad draws heavily on the work of Foucault and Butler but theorizes the relationship between the material and the discursive to what she terms a posthumanist performativity."

Braidotti appears once in the body, printed p.262: "as Rosi Braidotti (2002) urges in this time of change, “the challenge lies in thinking about processes, rather than concepts” (p. 1)." She is in the reference list as *Metamorphoses: Towards a Materialist Theory of Becoming* (2002). She is not one of the six.

## 3. The 2013 method: plugging in, the threshold, folding and flattening

**Plugging in** is taken from Deleuze and Guattari's *A Thousand Plateaus*: the only question when one writes is which other machine the literary machine can be plugged into. Data and theory are both treated as machines, and the question is what each produces when connected to the other.

**The threshold** is the article's figure for where that happens, printed p.264: "Enter the threshold. In architecture, a threshold is in the middle of things. It exists as a passageway. A threshold has no function, purpose, or meaning until it is connected to other spaces. That is, a threshold does not become a passageway until it is attached to other things different from itself. Thresholds contain both entries and exits; they are both/and. A single threshold can be not only an entryway, but also an exit; therefore, the structure itself is not quite as linear and definitive as one might think. In other terms, thresholds can denote excess, such as in having a low threshold for pain. The excess of a threshold is the space in which something else occurs: a response, an effect. Once you exceed the threshold, something new happens."

**The three maneuvers** are set out as a numbered list at printed p.264. The third is the one that names a practice rather than a stance: "working the same “data chunks” repeatedly to “deform [them], to make [them] groan and protest” (Foucault, 1980, p. 22-23) with an overabundance of meaning, which in turn not only creates new knowledge but also shows the suppleness of each when plugged in."

**Folding and flattening** names the refusal to sit outside the data. The researcher is folded into the texts rather than placed at a distance from them.

## 4. The 2013 article on the first person, which is what the subsection asks about

This is the part that bears directly on first-person inquiry.

> If the “I” of the participant is always becoming in the process of telling, so too the “I” of the researcher is always becoming in the process of researching, listening, and writing.

Printed p.266.

> In our process of flattening and folding, we do not seek more and more reflexivity that reveals more and more about the researcher’s ways of knowing. We seek to unsettle the “I” of both the researcher and researched who is a static and singular subject.

Printed p.266. The move is explicitly away from reflexivity and toward unsettling the "I" on both sides of the interview.

> Do we still believe in the subject? Yes, and no. We do not adhere to the liberal humanist subject that is an individual person or self.

Printed p.266.

**Where the decentring pressure comes from here is partly matter.** The Barad section of the article asks a materialist question of the same data, printed p.268: "It is the work of Karen Barad and others named as “new materialists” or “material feminists” that we use to ask how our intra-action with other bodies (both human and nonhuman) produce subjectivities and performative enactments not previously thought."

And the closing formulation of the shift, printed p.269: "toward a thinking of the interview and what is “told” as discursive, as material, as discursive and material, as material<->discursive, and as constituted between the discursive and the material in a posthumanist becoming."

**The worked example is an office.** Three paragraphs at printed pp. 268 to 269 read a participant's account of students sitting on her office floor as produced by the room itself: "This office, as a force producing a materialization of bodies, creates an intimate (cramped) space that welcomes (deters) students and that invites a closeness (repulsion) of bodies “sitting on the floor” and hanging out together. This material of the office produces a social environment of refuge and intimacy for the Black students who are in the minority at Regional State University."

## 5. The 2014 article: what it sets out to do

Its abstract, printed p.742: "In this article, I use Karen Barad’s concept of diffraction as a methodological practice of reading “insights through one another” in response to the editors’ call for examples of analysis after coding in qualitative inquiry. A diffractive reading of data through multiple theoretical insights moves qualitative analysis away from habitual normative readings toward a diffractive reading that spreads thought in unpredictable patterns producing different knowledge."

**Barad's own list, quoted in the article at printed p.742**, is where quantum physics enters: "I draw on the insights of some of our best scientific and social theories including quantum physics, science studies, the philosophy of physics, feminist theory, critical race theory, postcolonial theory, (post) Marxist theory, and poststructuralist theory” (p. 25)."

**Diffraction against reflection**, printed p.742: "She stated that “whereas the metaphor of reflection reflects the themes of mirroring and sameness [e.g., coding], diffraction is marked by patterns of difference"

**The title phrase**, printed p.743: "To stop at coding, in other words, to produce an “easy sense” (Mazzei, 2007), would have allowed us to affirm our own experiences as women in the academy and to fall short in our attempts to work the limitations of such practices."

## 6. The 2014 worked example, and its first person

The article is written in the first person singular throughout. Mazzei names what she is plugging in, printed p.744: "In the data excerpt that follows, I read and plug into multiple texts: feminist poststructuralist theory, the transcript of my interview with Brenda, Barad’s concept of intra-action, Deleuze and Guattari’s concept of desire, the editors call to produce a pedagogical example of data analysis after coding, my ongoing work with Alecia Jackson, and so on."

The data excerpt is a single participant, called Brenda, on her divorce and her return to school. Two theoretical questions are put to it: Deleuze and Guattari on desire, and Barad on intra-action, printed p.744: "It is the work of Karen Barad and others named “new materialists” or “material feminists” to ask how our intra-action with other bodies (both human and nonhuman) produces subjectivities and performative enactments not previously thought."

The reading then treats words as material, printed p.745: "To make the shift from discursive to material↔discursive2 is to read these words not merely as a construction based on gender stereotypes but as material, an assault not just on her intellect but a negation of her as a woman."

**The double arrow is defined in a note**, printed p.746: "I am using the double arrow to indicate the intra-action or that which is simultaneously materially and discursively produced, reflective of a key shift in material feminist thought. I do so as a gesture toward a removal of the hyphen or slash used to indicate the relationship between the material and the discursive without privileging one over the other."

The closing sentence, printed p.745: "Such an analysis offers a way of considering the entanglement of bodies, texts, relationships, data, language, and theory that we are just beginning to understand and that presents the possibility of much productive potential for qualitative researchers after coding."

## 8. What separates the two articles

| | 2013 | 2014 |
|---|---|---|
| **Voice** | "we", 193 times. Two authors reporting a joint study | "I", 42 times in 3,163 words. One author working alone |
| **Governing figure** | the threshold, 35× | diffraction and diffractive, 32× |
| **Theorists put to work** | six: Derrida, Spivak, Foucault, Butler, Deleuze, Barad | two: Deleuze and Guattari on desire, Barad on intra-action |
| **Where the method comes from** | Deleuze and Guattari, plugging in | Barad, reading insights through one another |
| **Participants** | Cassandra and Sera, from an interview study of 10 first-generation women academics | Brenda, from the same study |
| **Relation to coding** | coding produces four themes the authors call not interesting | coding is where analysis begins and ends for many, and the article is an answer to a call for analysis after coding |

The two share a study, a book and a vocabulary. They are not the same argument: the 2013 article is a method paper built from six theorists in which Barad is the last and most materialist; the 2014 article is a diffraction paper in which Barad supplies the method itself.

## 9. What follows for the first-person subsection, stated as a difference and not adjudicated

The subsection asks how to write first-person inquiry inside posthuman theory without it reading as anthropocentrism.

| What the subsection asks | What each article does with it |
|---|---|
| **Can the researcher's "I" stay in the text?** | **2013: yes, and it is argued for.** The "I" of the researcher is put in the same position as the "I" of the participant, always becoming in the telling, and the article says plainly that the aim is not more reflexivity but an unsettled "I" on both sides |
| **2014: yes, and it is simply done.** The article is written in the first person singular from its first sentence, and the analytic questions are posed as the author's own | |
| **Where does the decentring pressure come from?** | **In both, partly from matter.** An office is a force that produces a mentor; words are an assault and not only a construction; the material and the discursive constitute one another. This is the thing the 2008 article does not do |
| **Is the word anthropocentrism used?** | **No, in neither.** It appears only in the title of Hultman and Lenz Taguchi (2010) in both reference lists, which is about visual data, not about first-person writing |

**This is recorded as a difference, not settled.** Whether an "I" decentred by intra-action answers the worry about anthropocentrism, or only relocates it, is the claim the subsection would be making, and it is not mine to make.

## 11. Their own first-person positions, for the record

The 2013 article discloses that the authors are not outside the category they studied: an endnote records that one of the two is a first-generation college graduate, and the 2014 article repeats it, adding that the other is the daughter of a first-generation graduate whose father used the G.I.
