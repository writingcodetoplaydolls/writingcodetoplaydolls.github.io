## Part 2. Are the particles in a body entangled?

Yes, no, and not in the way the word usually implies. Three tiers, which are routinely collapsed into one another in popular writing, and which a reader with physics training will separate immediately.

### Tier 1. Entanglement at the molecular scale: yes, ubiquitous, and local

| | |
|---|---|
| **Bonds as entanglement** | "Chemical bonding concepts emerge naturally from maximally entangled atomic orbitals," *Nature Communications* (2026). Treats bonding as entanglement among spatially localised orbitals. So the claim has a current peer-reviewed formulation. [Paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-73527-w) |
| **The distinction that undoes the easy version** | Entanglement arising purely from the indistinguishability of identical particles is widely treated as non-operational. A hydrogen-bond preprint argues that a covalent bond's superposition of electron locations is indistinguishable from its swapped configuration, so it "does not possess any quantum entanglement which is useful from a quantum information and thermodynamics point of view". [arXiv 1807.00647](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.00647) |
| **The counter-argument** | "Entanglement between Identical Particles Is a Useful and Consistent Resource," *Physical Review X* 10, 041012 (2020). Argues identical-particle entanglement is "precisely the resource required for distributing usable entanglement to separated parties", and that in such systems the usable entanglement is **between modes**. [arXiv 1908.11735](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.11735) |

What this amounts to: the electrons in every molecule in a body are entangled under the orbital or mode description, and that is not a fringe claim. But the entanglement is **inside the molecule**. It is not a connection between one part of a body and another, and not between one body and another. Nothing in this tier reaches across a body.

> **CORRECTED 29 Oct. Barad answers the scale question herself, with physics, on a page this folder has held all along.** *Meeting the Universe Halfway* is in `Comps\Used texts`, and **printed page 279** gives three reasons quantum effects are not evident in everyday life: the ratio of Planck's constant to the object's mass ("It is not that we live our daily lives in a classical world, rather than a quantum one; the point is that we generally don't notice quantum effects because they are very small"); the difficulty of shielding an object from an environment whose fluctuation randomises a superposition into a mixture "for all practical purposes (but not in principle)", **which is her definition of decoherence**; and not knowing where to look for the correlations, which is a methodological reason, not a physical one. She then notes that macroscopic quantum tunnelling and coherence **have** been observed, citing Tony Leggett's 1984 SQUID experiments. **Her account is more precise than the one assembled from reviews below, and her qualifier "but not in principle" is the exact form of the claim.** Also note that at **printed 270** she defines the relation the whole superposition thread needs: an entanglement is "a generalization of a superposition to the case of more than one particle." See `BARAD_pages_pulled.md`.

### Tier 2. Does entanglement do biological work? Open, and decoherence is the obstacle

| | |
|---|---|
| **The objection, stated by its own side** | A 2026 review in *Clinical and Translational Medicine* puts it plainly: living cells run at about 310 K in aqueous, ion-rich environments "that should, in principle, destroy coherence on femtosecond timescales". [Open](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13184552/) |
| **The counter** | ENAQT, environment-assisted quantum transport: some noise preserves coherence by reducing exciton localisation. This is the main technical answer to the decoherence objection, and it is in the same review |
| **The strongest candidate for entanglement doing work** | Radical-pair magnetoreception in cryptochrome, the avian compass. Spin correlations are the proposed resource. A 2024 *Nature Communications* paper adds the quantum Zeno effect for tightly bound pairs. [Paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-55124-x) |
| **Its limit, in the literature's own words** | "A demonstration that the mechanism operates in the living bird has yet to be reported." And across the reviews searched, **no direct experimental demonstration of entanglement in any living organism.** The 2024 paper itself notes the cryptochrome role "is not uncontested" |
| **Established but not entanglement** | Enzyme proton and electron tunnelling is solid quantum biology. Tunnelling is not entanglement, and the two get conflated |

### Tier 3. The brain-specific proposal: speculative

| | |
|---|---|
| **Fisher, M., Posner molecules** | Proposes that phosphorus-31 nuclear spins in Ca9(PO4)6 clusters could be entangled, formed by enzymatic hydrolysis of pyrophosphate, and that pairs might stay entangled "for a day or possibly much longer". Theory has continued, including Oxford modelling of six interacting spins, one per phosphate. [arXiv 1807.06339](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.06339), [arXiv 1711.05899](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.05899) |
| **Its status** | Reviews state that Posner cluster coherence "has not yet been studied experimentally", and that experimental proof of Posner quantum effects in living brains is still lacking. No 2025 or 2026 experiment testing it directly turned up |
| **Do not use** | A Zenodo document claiming microsecond coherence in cells and "58% decoherence suppression" in a protein is not peer reviewed and the figures could not be verified. A recent promotional post about a "nuclear spin quantum brain" framework in *Annals of Physics* could not be verified either |
| **Not searched** | Penrose and Hameroff's Orch-OR and the 2024 microtubule-anaesthetic work. Left out deliberately rather than summarised from memory. It carries a reputation cost, so it needs checking before it goes anywhere near a draft |

### The one hard limit

There is no evidence for, and no proposed mechanism for, entanglement between two people's bodies. Separately, and independent of biology: entanglement on its own cannot transmit anything. Correlation is not a channel. This is settled physics, not an open question, and it is the point at which a physics-trained reader will stop reading if a chapter implies otherwise. Recorded here as a fact about what the physics will support, not as a ruling on how the idea gets used.

## The measured permeability, which needs no quantum mechanics at all

Possibly more useful than the quantum material, because these are instruments and numbers rather than analogies.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Neutrinos** | IceCube's own fact page: about 100 trillion pass through a body each second, mostly from the Sun. Sources disagree on whether the figure is per body or per square centimetre; one derivation from solar flux times body cross-section reaches roughly 650 trillion per second, and popular sources round between 65 and 100 trillion. Expected interactions: "no more than roughly one interaction in your body, perhaps a few, over an entire lifetime." **The body is very close to perfectly transparent.** [IceCube](https://icecube.wisc.edu/about-us/facts/) |
| **Cosmic-ray muons** | Tens to hundreds through the body each second ([Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/physics-mathematics/how-many-cosmic-particles-are-passing-through-your-body-at-any-moment)); another source gives one to two per second through an average palm. These do interact |
| **Bomb-pulse radiocarbon** | Spalding, Bhardwaj, Buchholz, Druid & Frisén (2005), "Retrospective birth dating of cells in humans," *Cell* 122(1), 133-143. Carbon-14 from atmospheric nuclear tests enters the DNA of dividing cells and is locked there, so the atmosphere of a particular year is datable inside a cell decades later. Accurate to within a few years. **A measured instance of the outside world becoming tissue, with a date on it.** The 2013 follow-up modelled roughly 1,400 new hippocampal neurons a day and more than a third of them regularly replaced; that figure is from news coverage and needs checking against the paper. The pulse will have dissipated by about 2050. [Cell commentary](https://www.cell.com/fulltext/S0092-8674(05)00656-2) |
| **MRI, with a correction** | T2 is literally the decay of transverse **phase coherence** in the body's own nuclear spins, read from outside the skin. But the standard account is that quantum mechanics is needed to model a single proton while classical mechanics describes the ensemble adequately, and clinical MRI treats the signal as classical bulk magnetisation. **So MRI is not a macroscopic quantum measurement of a body, and saying it is would be wrong.** A tempting claim to avoid |

## Critiques to hold, per the citation-discipline rule

## A suggested connection, not a claim

Offered for acceptance or rejection, not written into anything:

Across three days the instruments collected here (the event camera, the magnetometer, Y-chromosome PCR, 16S sequencing, carbon-14 in DNA, and the orbital description of a bond) agree on one thing only: each one places the edge of the body somewhere different, and none of them finds the edge at the skin. That is Barad's apparatus argument with six instruments behind it rather than one thought experiment. The quantum tier may matter less to that argument than the radiocarbon does.

## ADDED 29 Oct 2026: the scale question is now answered in three held texts

Read in `BARAD_three_texts_read.md`. This file's central worry was whether quantum claims can be carried to the scale of a body. Barad answers it herself, three times, in texts that were already in `K:\OneDrive\UNM\Comps\Used texts`:

1. ***Meeting the Universe Halfway*, printed p. 279** (recorded in `BARAD_pages_pulled.md`): three reasons, her own definition of decoherence, and Leggett's SQUIDs as the empirical case for macroscopic quantum behaviour.
2. **"What Is the Measure of Nothingness?" (2012), note 2**, which is the most direct: "I don't want the reader to misunderstand and think that indeterminacy, or rather, the play of in/determinacies, is limited to the domain of the small. On the contrary, **the play of indeterminacies is ontologically prior to notions of scale** and, more generally, space and time. It's just that with current technologies they are more easily detected on relatively small scales."
3. **"Diffracting Diffraction" (2014)**, in a parenthesis: quantum physics "queers the binary type of difference at every layer of the onion (**not merely on the micro-scale as opposed to the macro-scale, as if there were a line in the sand between micro and macro** rather than an ongoing reconfiguring of spacetimemattering across and within spaces and times)."

So the scale objection, as the critiques of agential realism press it, has a stated answer in the primary texts. **It is an ontological claim, not an empirical one**, and that is the thing to be clear about: Barad is not claiming that decoherence does not happen, she is claiming that scale is itself an effect rather than a prior frame. Whether that answer satisfies the physicists who raise the objection is a separate question and this file's survey of those critiques still stands.

Two further things from those texts bear on this file directly. The nothingness essay rests its account of the vacuum on a real and citable result, Stephen Battersby, "It's Confirmed: Matter Is Merely Vacuum Fluctuations," *New Scientist*, 20 November 2008: the quarks account for roughly 1 per cent of the mass of protons and neutrons, the rest coming from virtual particles, with the Lamb shift, the Casimir effect and the anomalous magnetic moment of the electron as the measurable consequences. Barad's reading of it is the sentence closest to the permeability question in any of her work: "**even the smallest bits of matter are an enormous multitude. Each 'individual' is made up of all possible histories of virtual intra-actions with all Others.**" And the parallax essay supplies the guard against reading any of this as relativism: "**The existence of indeterminacies does not mean that there are no facts, no histories, no bleeding**, on the contrary, indeterminacies are constitutive of the very materiality of being."

Also worth recording against this file's "suggested connection" section: Barad **condemns the analogical use of quantum physics** at printed p. 18 of the book, which is a constraint on how any of this can be used.

## ADDED later on 29 Oct 2026: Barad on touch, which is this file's own question

Read in `BARAD_transmaterialities_read.md`. "TransMaterialities" (*GLQ* 21(2-3), 2015, 387-422), now held at `K:\OneDrive\UNM\Comps\Used texts\Barad_Karen_2015_TransMaterialities_GLQ_21_2-3.pdf`, contains two sections that take up the question this file was opened to ask. Both are marked in its notes 23, 24 and 30 as borrowed in part from "On Touching: The Inhuman That Therefore I Am", which is **still not held and is now the clear first priority**.

**She starts from the standard physics account and does not dispute it:**

> "The reason that the desk feels solid, or the cat's coat feels soft, or we can (even) hold coffee cups and one another's hands, is an effect of electromagnetic repulsion. **All we really ever feel is the electromagnetic field, not the other whose touch we seek.** Atoms are mostly empty space, and electrons, which lie at the farthest reaches of an atom, hinting at its perimeter, cannot bear direct contact."

**Then she goes one theory deeper rather than arguing with it**, from quantum mechanics to quantum field theory, where the electron is never an isolated thing that could fail to touch another isolated thing, because it is already touching itself through the vacuum: "the electron is always (already) intra-acting with the virtual particles of the vacuum in all possible ways . . . **Part of what an electron is, is its self-energy intra-action.**"

> "**Every level of touch, then, is itself touched by all possible others.** . . . Hence **self-touching is an encounter with the infinite alterity of the self**."

> "**All touching entails an infinite alterity, so that touching the other is touching all others, including the 'self,' and touching the 'self' entails touching the stranger within.** Even the smallest bits of matter are an unfathomable multitude."

> "touching, sensing, is what matter does, or rather, what matter is: **matter is condensations of responses, of response-ability**."

**The thing to be exact about, because this file exists to be exact about it.** Barad is not correcting the electromagnetic-repulsion account. She is saying the entities it describes are not separable to begin with, so "did they make contact" has no determinate answer. **That is an ontological claim of the same kind as the scale answer above, not an empirical result**, and the critiques this file surveys apply to it in the same way. The empirical anchor underneath it is the vacuum-fluctuation mass result already recorded here from the nothingness essay, not anything in this article.

**A fifth and sixth statement of the scale answer**, and the most compact one yet, from note 65: "I have tried to make the point over and over again that quantum phenomena are not restricted to the so-called micro scale. **Scale does not precede phenomena; scale is only materialized/defined within particular phenomena.**" Note 29 says the same thing again. That is six statements across four held texts.

**One empirical finding worth this file's attention**, because it is a bioelectric claim about bodies with laboratory papers under it rather than a physics analogy: the Levin and Adams lab at Tufts showed that manipulating the electric field around a severed *Xenopus* tadpole tail extends the window in which it regenerates, that the same technique regrew a complete frog leg, and that a bioelectric "face" pattern flashes across the embryo and vanishes **before cell differentiation**, with the relevant genes not yet switched on. Disrupting the pump that generates the potential produced abnormal development, which is the causal test. The primary papers are **Adams, Masi and Levin, *Development* 134 (2007): 1323-35** and **Tseng et al., *Journal of Neuroscience* 30(39) (2010): 13192-200**. **Barad's further claim that this is a quantum field theoretic effect is marked by her as conjecture** ("If my conjecture is correct, it places the Levin-Adams regeneration investigations within the emerging field of quantum biology"), and it should be quoted with that hedge. The bioelectric findings do not depend on it.
