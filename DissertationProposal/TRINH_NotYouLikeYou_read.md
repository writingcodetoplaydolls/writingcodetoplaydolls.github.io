## 1. The subjectivity argument, which answers two standing objections at once

**This is the find.** The last two paragraphs of the essay are a defence of subjective, first-person, practice-based work, made by a filmmaker about her own practice, and they are stronger and more specific than anything in either of the two first-person files.

The two objections the folder has been carrying are **Joan Scott's** (via Siebers, *Material Feminisms* ch. 10): experience used as evidence "reproduces rather than contests given ideological systems", and "It is not individuals who have experience, but subjects who are constituted through experience". And **Barad's**, found earlier today in *Meeting the Universe Halfway*: reflexivity is no answer, because "the methodology of reflexivity mirrors the geometrical optics of reflection" and "remains caught up in geometries of sameness".

Trinh's answer is not to drop the I and not to be reflexive about it. It is to refuse to naturalise it:

> "**By refusing to naturalize the I, subjectivity uncovers the myth of essential core, of spontaneity and depth as inner vision.** Subjectivity, therefore, **does not merely consist of talking about oneself**, be this talking indulgent or critical."

She disposes of the self-absorption charge directly, and she does it by reclassifying what subjectivity is:

> "a grasp of subjectivity as, let's say, **the science of the subject** or merely as related to the subject, **makes the fear of self-absorption look absurd**."

> "**Awareness of the limits in which one works need not lead to any form of indulgence in personal partiality, nor to the narrow conclusion that it is impossible to understand anything about other peoples** since the difference is one of essence."

> "Acute political subject awareness cannot be reduced to a question of self-criticism toward self-improvement, nor of self-praise toward greater self-confidence."

And then she names what actually goes wrong, which is the usable part, because it is **a test with three clauses rather than a position**. What is at stake is

> "a practice of subjectivity that is still **unaware of its own constituted nature**, hence, the difficulty to exceed the simplistic pair of subjectivity and objectivity; a practice of subjectivity that is **unaware of its continuous role in the production of meaning**, as if things can make sense by themselves, so that the interpreter's function consists of only choosing among the many existing readings; **unaware of representation as representation**, that is to say, the cultural, sexual, political inter-reality of the filmmaker as subject, the reality of the subject film and **the reality of the cinematic apparatus**. And finally unaware of **the inappropriate other within every I**."

Four awarenesses, and a practice that has them is not what Scott's objection is about. **This is the same shape as the criterion found in "Nature's Queer Performativity" yesterday** (anthropomorphising is legitimate where it "put[s] oneself at risk and do[es] the work it takes to truly enable a response"): a move that is usually defended or attacked in the abstract is given a test a passage can be held against.

**And note what the third clause contains.** "The reality of the **cinematic apparatus**" puts Trinh's own word for her equipment in the same place as this project's central term, which runs at 118 in the prose and 243 in Barad's book. **Trinh uses "apparatus" of a camera**, in the essay Barad builds her diffraction methodology on, in a list of the things a subjective practice must be aware of. That is a join between 1.1 and 2.2 that needed neither construction nor a secondary source.

## 2. The threshold passage, verified, with its surroundings

The passage already in the folder at second hand, through Barad, is verbatim correct. **What was missing is the paragraph after it**, which is the part that turns the figure into a claim about writing.

> "The moment the insider steps out from the inside she's no longer a mere insider. She necessarily looks in from the outside while also looking out from the inside. Not quite the same, not quite the other, she stands in that **undetermined threshold place where she constantly drifts in and out**. Undercutting the inside/outside opposition, her intervention is necessarily that of both not quite an insider and not quite an outsider. She is, in other words, **this inappropriate other or same** who moves about with always at least two gestures: that of affirming 'I am like you' while persisting in her difference and that of reminding 'I am different' while unsettling every definition of otherness arrived at."

Then, immediately:

> "**This is not to say that the historical I can be obscured and ignored** and that differentiation cannot be made, but that **I is not unitary**, culture has never been monolithic and is always more or less in relation to a judging subject. Differences do not only exist between outsider and insider, two entities. They are also **at work within the outsider herself or the insider, herself, a single entity**. **She who knows she cannot speak of them without speaking of herself, of history without involving her story, also knows that she cannot make a gesture without activating the to and fro movement of life.**"

So the threshold is not a position between two groups; the difference runs inside the single person. And the guard against the obvious misreading is in her own next sentence: the historical I is not to be obscured.

## 3. An artist answering a criticism of her own work with a method

For practice-as-research this is a precedent worth having, because the criticism is formal and the answer is the argument:

> "**I have often been asked about what some viewers call the lack of conflicts in my films.** Psychological conflict is often equated with substance and depth. Conflicts in Western contexts often serve to define identities. My suggestion to the 'lack' is: **let difference replace conflict.** Difference as understood in many feminist and non-Western contexts, difference as foreground in my film work is not opposed to sameness, nor synonymous with separateness."

The shape is: a property of the work that viewers read as a deficiency is the thing being proposed. **That is the same operation as Yergeau building stims into the argument and Jonker building echolalia into the form of the writing**, and it is an earlier and differently sourced instance of it. It also pairs with Chiew's etymology of **deficiency** (*de* + *facere*, an unmaking rather than a lack) in the 2017 Kirby collection, where the plan already files it: Trinh performs the same reversal on the word "lack" without the etymology.

Her ending to that paragraph is the sentence Barad quotes: "Many of us still hold on to the concept of difference not as a tool of creativity to question multiple forms of repression and dominance, but as a tool of segregation, to exert power on the basis of racial and sexual essences. **The apartheid type of difference.**"

## 4. Three examples, and the first and second both bear on standing threads

Trinh offers three practices of difference. All three are short and quotable.

**The veil, "as reality and metaphor".** Her phrase, and it is a both/and:

> "First of all I would take the example of **the veil as reality and metaphor**. **If the act of unveiling has a liberating potential, so does the act of veiling.** It all depends on the context in which such an act is carried out, or more precisely, on how and where women see dominance. Difference should neither be defined by the dominant sex nor by the dominant culture."

Two things follow. **"Reality and metaphor" is a fifth position in the metaphor cluster** assembled earlier today, where Braidotti says "these are no metaphors", Hayles says "it is an artifact, a created object", Barad says "more than a metaphor", and Haraway says "an optical metaphor for the effort to make a difference in the world". Trinh says both at once, and says it in three words. **And the structure of the veiling claim is Bohr's complementarity as Barad defines it**, two accounts that are mutually exclusive and mutually necessary: unveiling liberates, veiling liberates, and which one obtains depends on the apparatus, here "on how and where women see dominance". Four authors on metaphor and two on complementarity, recorded side by side and left there.

**Silence**, which is a third vocabulary for the absence findings this folder keeps producing:

> "Within the context of women's speech silence has many faces. Like the veiling of women just mentioned, **silence can only be subversive when it frees itself from the male-defined context of absence, lack, and fear as feminine territories.** On the one hand, we face the danger of **inscribing femininity as absence, as lack and blank** in rejecting the importance of the act of enunciation. On the other hand, we understand the necessity to place women on the side of negativity and to work in undertones . . . **Silence is so commonly set in opposition with speech. Silence as a will not to say or a will to unsay and as a language of its own has barely been explored.**"

The folder already has two vocabularies for absence: **Law's** manifest absence and Otherness, with four styles of Othering, and **Mills's** epistemology of ignorance by way of Tuana. Trinh's is a third and it is the only one of the three that treats the absence as **a practice someone is carrying out** rather than a gap in a record or a structure of not-knowing: "a will not to say or a will to unsay". Set that beside the nothingness essay's blank page "teeming with the desires of would-be traces" and there are two held texts on the blank.

**Subjectivity as the third example** leads into section 1 above, and carries the Lévy-Bruhl quotation on primitive mentality having "no clear idea or even no idea at all of matter and soul" (printed "Levi-Bruhl" on the archived page, so check the spelling before quoting).

## 5. Two things that join Trinh to lanes she was not in

**A disability and neurodivergence sentence, in 1988.** Early in the essay, on what happens to people who will not hold still as an identity:

> "This means that at heart X must be X, Y must be Y, and X cannot be Y. **Those running around yelling X is not X and X can be Y, usually land in a hospital, a rehabilitation center, a concentration camp, or a reservation.** All deviations from the dominant stream of thought, that is to say, the belief in a permanent essence of woman and in an invariant but fragile identity whose loss is considered to be a specifically human danger, **can easily fit into the categories of the mentally ill or the mentally underdeveloped.**"

Section 1.4 is autism as method, and its nearest existing statement is Manning and Bozalek on neurotypicality as "a systemic baseline according to which modes of self-presentation and modes of knowing are policed". **Trinh says the policing mechanism is psychiatric classification and names four institutions**, and she says it as a consequence of the logic of identity rather than as a separate topic. Nothing in the plan joins the Trinh lane to 1.4; this sentence does, and it is in a text that is now held.

**A seventh instance of measurement producing its object, and it is an administrative table.** Trinh quotes a *Time* report, "The Crazy Game of Musical Chairs", on South African racial reclassification:

> "people in South Africa who are classified by race and place into one of the nine racial categories that determine where they can live and work, can have their classification changed if they can prove they were put in a wrong group. Thus, in an announcement of racial reclassifications by the Home Affairs Ministers one learns that **9 whites became colored, 506 coloreds became white, 2 whites became Malay, 14 Malay became white, 40 coloreds became Black, 666 Blacks became colored**, and the list goes on. However, says the minister, **no Blacks apply to become whites. And no whites became Black.**"

The plan's 2.5 cluster has six instances of measurement producing its object (the skin prick test, Butler on signification, Bohr via Barad, Wigner, reproducibility, and Molderings's *Stoppages*), plus Barad's sonogram and the two *Pfiesteria* laboratory protocols found yesterday. **This is a different kind again**: an official classification system, its own published correction table, and a pattern in the corrections that the table does not comment on and Trinh does. It is also counting used as evidence inside an artist's talk, which is what this project does with its own corpora.
