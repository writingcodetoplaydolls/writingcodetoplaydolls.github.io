## 1. Not the metaphor book

It scored 33 hits on the phrase set because of "literalis-", "is the reality" and similar strings. **All six of its metaphor passages are about Biblical literalism versus allegorism in ontotheology**, not about the standing of figures in general. The nearest thing to a position:

> "Christian ontotheology has had a tendency to represent the divinity through literalist (or, at best, strongly analogical) ontic anthropomorphism. **Such mythological tendency is the result of interpreting poetic metaphors that come from a variety of human cultural spheres in a word-for-word way** . . . **the literal interpretation of these metaphors transforms the depth of these ideas into a more-than-often childish mythological product** incompatible with a critical philosophy supported on scientific findings."

That is the **inverse** of the claim being looked for: here the error is reading a metaphor literally, and "the depth of these ideas" is lost by literalising rather than preserved by it. It is worth one line as a counter-position and nothing more. The volume's own method on the mythological, stated for the sake of clarity, is to "**reduce here mythological thinking to the literalist interpretation of anthropomorphic and zoomorphic metaphors and allegories**", which is a stipulation rather than an argument about metaphor.

## 2. What it is instead, and why it matters to 1.5

**It is a mainstream analytic-philosophy volume on materialism**, in Springer's Synthese Library, and it contains a sustained critique of new materialism. The plan's row on "the published critiques of agential realism" is the place for it.

Its contents: 1 What is Materialism? History and Concepts; 2 Systemic Materialism; 3 Discontinuous Materialism; **4 Quantum Matters**; 5 Spacetime Is Material; 6 Systemic Materialism in Biology; 7 Mind and Matter; 8 Materialism and the History of Sciences; 9 Materialism, Logic, and Mathematics; 10 The Material Nature of Software; and **three paired debate chapters**, 11 "Mathematics Refer to Material Entities / Mathematics Do Not", 12 "Emergent Materialism Implies Continuism / Emergent Materialism Does Not", 13 "**Materialism Is False / Materialism Is Not False**".

**The paired-chapter form is worth noticing on its own account**, because it is a published instance of the standing rule in this project about divergent readings: the volume prints the two positions as two chapters and does not adjudicate between them in the chapter titles.

**Its critique of new materialism**, which is the usable part:

> "the so-called '**new materialisms**', although critical of physicalism, are equally paradoxical. **They have a strong tendency towards reducing everything to socio-cultural and political practices and dynamisms.** Harman (2016) has correctly seen that these 'materialisms' are supported on a reductionism that '**overmines**' objects. In our own terms: **they very often practice an upwards reductionism that ends up in a form of metaphysical holism.**"

> "If Meillassoux's 'speculative materialism' implicitly introduced animism, **Jane Bennett's 'new materialism' explicitly embraces it.** Bennett correctly critiques outdated views of matter as a passive and dull reality, in contrast to active 'life'. But, following Bruno Latour's Actor-Network-Theory (2005), she considers edibles, commodities, storms and metals as '**quasi agents**' . . . **these approaches confuse the necessary critique of matter as negativity and passivity with a neo-vitalist or neo-animist understanding.**"

> "'New materialism', of course, comes in much more varieties than just Bennett's. **But the emphasis on the agential character of matter is common to all** (Coole and Frost 2010)."

> "**a flat ontology which avoids distinctions, classifications, and decisions on whether scientific results are true beyond a specific setting and in what way is hardly an ontology at all.**"

> "Talk of materiality and materialism pervades a number of disciplines, but it does so in so heterogeneous and undefined ways as to encompass both physical reductionism and a number of 'new materialisms' that **actually defend a social constructionism** . . . '**Materiality' talk faces the same ontological ambiguities as Bridgman's operationalism before it** (Bunge 1988)."

**And the list, which is useful in itself** because it shows what the word carries in this literature: "corporeist materialism, physicalist materialism, dialectical materialism, mechanistic materialism, holistic materialism, systemic materialism, eliminative materialism, Australian materialism, transcendental materialism, idealist materialism, speculative materialism, discontinuous materialism, **feminist materialism**, 'new materialisms' . . . And the list goes on."

**So the volume attacks new materialism fourteen times and never mentions Barad, Braidotti or Haraway.** Its targets are Bennett, Latour, Meillassoux and Harman, and its own positive position is Mario Bunge's systemic and emergent materialism, which is what the 435 is.

Three things follow. **The critique does not land on agential realism**, whatever its force against Bennett, so it cannot be cited as a critique of Barad and should not be put in the same row as Faye and Jaksland, who argue with Barad directly. **The word "new materialism" is doing different work here** than in this project's sources: in this volume it means Bennett-and-after, read as neo-vitalism, and the word's instability is itself the volume's complaint. And **the operationalism charge recurs**: Bridgman's operationalism is raised here as the precedent for "materiality talk", and Fitje's chapter, read earlier today, independently describes physics as providing only "**operational definitions**". Two unrelated sources, the same term, opposite valuations.

**Chapter 4, "Quantum Matters", and chapter 5, "Spacetime Is Material", were not read.** Given that this is a physics-literate volume with Bohr at 5 and decoherence at 2, chapter 4 is the one to read if the quantum-critique row is ever worked up properly, since it would say what a philosopher of physics takes the ontology of quantum theory to be, without reference to Barad.
