## 1. Horse-girl assemblages was there all along

**`EBSCO-FullText-10_27_2026.pdf`**, in the top-level folder since 13:46 today. The filename says nothing, which is why three searches missed it.

> **Renold, E. & Ivinson, G. (2014). "Horse-girl assemblages: towards a post-human cartography of girls' desire in an ex-mining valleys community." *Discourse: Studies in the Cultural Politics of Education* 35(3), 361-376.** DOI 10.1080/01596306.2014.888841. 8,971 words.

Worth renaming, because every one of the thirteen other phematerialism files cites it, most of them pp. 364-365.

**And here is what they are all citing, on p. 364:**

> "Feminist and queer scholarship on neomaterialism is encouraging social scientists to think with new ontologies of the decentred subject: this is a post-human version of a **non-unitary, impersonal and post-identitarian subject that foregrounds the more-than-human** (Alaimo & Hekman, 2008; Barad, 2007; Braidotti, 2002/2005, 2006, 2013; Grosz, 2011; Probyn, 1996)."

> "practices that carry affective traces of the past that leap between any number of what Deleuze and Guattari call 'existential territories'; that is, those **'transversal flashes' (Guattari, 1995, p. 93)** of affective space, created by experience in assemblages."

**Note the attribution.** "Transversal flashes" is Guattari's, from *Chaosmosis* p. 93. Renold and Ivinson are quoting him. A dozen papers cite *them* for it. If you use the term, cite Guattari and note the route.

The paper's own move: the **pit-pony-girl assemblage**, reading the Welsh Mountain Pony and the pit-lasses harnessed to coal wagons into contemporary girls' horse riding, and refusing the empowerment frame: "Instead of viewing girls as needing to be empowered, transformed or rerouted, we emphasise the potential of what girls already do and feel."

## 2. Quantum: Manning uses "superposition", and it is not Barad's

Across the 37-file Manning library:

| *Always More Than One* | 1 | 2 | 0 |
| **Everything else** | 0-1 | 0-1 | **0** |

**Bohr appears zero times in roughly 895,000 words of Manning.** Her superposition is not the physics.

What it actually is, from *Thought in the Act*:

> "In this shared activation contour, movement and vision dynamically coincide. They are **in a state of dynamic superposition across their difference from each other**, each in its own manner, on its own register, according to its own order."

> "Waves collect. Overbalance. And fall. The form of movement, in and of itself, is not a straight line. It is a pulse. It is a waveform. Forsythe: 'Waves on the seashore, one coming in as the last is going out, overtaking, receding. **Don't be where you are. Make yourself tidal**'. In the tidal interval, the waves cancel each other out for an uncountable beat. They collapse into each other in dynamic superposition. **At this any-point of superposition, there is no visible movement.** The body, movement, is in suspense."

> "**Movement always starts from a superposition, a mutual inclusion of sequential forms.**"

So: superposition as the interval where a wave is neither arriving nor receding, borrowed from the seashore and from Forsythe's instruction to a dancer, and glossed as "mutual inclusion". Whitehead's term, not Bohr's.

### What this is worth to you

Your own prose uses **quantum 29, Bohr 10, superposition 1**, and your 1.5 states it carefully:

> "In quantum physics, before measurement, before the agential cut that produces a phenomenon, there is superposition: not multiple things coexisting but a state of ontological indeterminacy in which the boundaries and properties of the thing have not yet been enacted."

That is Barad's. Manning's is a different concept wearing the same word.

**Two ways to use that, and they pull opposite directions.** Either it is a second, non-physics lineage for the same term, which lets you show the concept doing work in movement and art without the physics load-bearing. Or it is a collision to flag, because a reader who knows Manning will hear "superposition" differently from a reader who knows Barad, and your 1.5 depends on the Baradian sense being precise. Given that your 1.5 already insists on indeterminacy-not-uncertainty, I would flag it rather than absorb it.

### Molderings

> **Molderings, Herbert (2010). *Duchamp and the Aesthetics of Chance: Art as Experiment.* New York: Columbia University Press.** 69,880 words. **In your folder twice.**

Chance 159 mentions, **Poincaré 187**. Its subject is the **3 Standard Stoppages** (1913-14), the work where Duchamp dropped three one-metre threads from one metre and fixed them where they fell, producing three new "standard" metres from chance.

> "It is a well established fact in Duchamp scholarship that the scientific theories of the mathematician Henri Poincaré were one of Duchamp's most important sources of inspiration. This also applies, and particularly so, in the case of the 3 Standard Stoppages."

> "Duchamp's art sides against the rationalism of Henri Poincaré and with the nominalistic theory of Édouard Le Roy, **according to which all laws, axioms, and standard measures are the arbitrary constructs of scientists**. From 1913 onward, the subversion of scientism became the main intent of Duchamp's art."

> "the scien[tific] and art, artwork and experiment, art and non-art no longer existed... ultimately led to the **experimentalization of art**."

**Why this is the bridge you are looking for.** The 3 Standard Stoppages is a work about **measurement producing its object**. A standard metre is not found, it is enacted, and Duchamp makes three of them by chance and keeps them. Your 1.5 says: "before the agential cut that produces a phenomenon, there is superposition... entities do not determinately exist apart from the particular, physical measuring apparatuses that constitute them."

That is the same claim, made in 1913, as an artwork, about the metre. And Molderings routes it through Poincaré and the early-twentieth-century crisis in the philosophy of measurement, not through quantum mechanics, which is why "quantum" is only 9 and "Bohr" only 3 in 69,880 words.

### Your 2.5 is already doing the adjacent work

Your prose has nine blocks on this, all in **2.5 Assemblage**, including:

> "**The frame as apparatus.** A urinal becomes art through the frame that receives it: the exhibition, the signature, the title. In Barad's terms the frame is the apparatus, and the readymade makes its cut visible. Assemblage folds the cut into the arrangement."

> "**One chooser or many relations.** The readymade puts the act in a single choice and the frame that receives it ('He CHOSE it'). Assemblage puts it in the relations among many found things... **Does the readymade keep the sovereign chooser that assemblage gives up?**"

So the readymade-as-apparatus move is already yours. **The 3 Standard Stoppages is the stronger case for it than Fountain**, because the apparatus in it is literally a measuring instrument, and the work is the measurement. Fountain makes the institutional frame visible; the Stoppages make the metrological one visible. For a chapter that runs on Barad's apparatus, the second is the better example, and the book is on your disk.

### Manning on the infrathin

> **Manning, E. (2017). "For a Pragmatics of the Useless, or the Value of the Infrathin." *Political Theory* 45(1), 97-115.** DOI 10.1177/0090591715625877. In your folder, twice (as the journal article and as a chapter of the Duke book).

Keywords, hers: **"infrathin, Marcel Duchamp, Alfred North Whitehead, speculative pragmatics, value/valuation, art/artfulness, politics/the political, time/duration."**

> "Marcel Duchamp describes the infrathin as 'the most minute of intervals, or the slightest of differences.' Working through Duchamp's proposition, and taking him at his word that the infrathin **cannot be defined as such, 'One can only give examples of it'**, this article explores how the infrathin comes to expression."

*Infrathin* (*inframince*) is Duchamp's own coinage, from his late notes. Manning uses it 83 times.

**This is a Duchamp-and-Whitehead article by the author your 2.6 was already looking for.** Your section 2.6 is "Black Mountain College: Albers, Rauschenberg, Motherwell, and Whitehead's process," with a to-search bullet naming *Relationscapes*. Duchamp is not currently in 2.6 at all; he is in 2.5. This article is the hinge between them, and it is a hinge made by Manning.

A term that "cannot be defined as such, one can only give examples of it" is also a direct precedent for how you might handle transposition, making strange and speaking nearby, which have resisted definition throughout this corpus.
