# Santa Cruz as a place: mapping people and place

29 Oct 2026. Opened because of what the two Barad texts read today put in the same room. Companion to `BARAD_three_texts_read.md`.

## The claim, in Amy's words

> "add a note about mapping people and place, that Barad and Anzaldua together gives us a key insight into Santa Cruz and UC Santa Cruz as a place. Adrienne Rich, Gregory Bateson, Donna Haraway were all there. Leonard Shlain and perhaps Fritzof Capra were nearby. I have found references to Trinh Minh Ha as a visiting lecturer. Add to this Anzaldua, who writes in a completely other research topic, then we enlarge the scale of the interconnection"

Everything below is evidence for that claim or a limit on it. Nothing below is a reading of it.

---

## 1. What the held texts say, which is the strongest part of this

Two sentences from texts in `K:\OneDrive\UNM\Comps\Used texts`, both read today, do the work that no biography can, because they are first-hand and they are dated.

**Barad dates and places herself with Anzaldúa.** From "Diffracting Diffraction: Cutting Together-Apart," *parallax* 20(3), 2014, p. 176:

> "Crossroads/Diffraction: Santa Cruz and Claremont, CA 1991. I am sitting outdoors with Gloria Anzaldúa talking about quantum physics and mestiza consciousness."

With the institutional detail around it: Barad was teaching in the Physics Department at Pomona College, sitting in on **Deena González's** Latina Feminist Traditions class, and co-organising a seminar reading *Borderlands*. "Gloria and I talk about quantum physics, the two-slit diffraction experiment, waves and particles and *mita' y mita'*. We are happily making diffraction patterns."

**Barad dates and places Trinh at Santa Cruz.** The same essay opens on Trinh T. Minh-ha presenting **"Not You/Like You" at Santa Cruz in 1988**, and quotes her at length.

**Barad's own author line**, in "Nature's Queer Performativity," *Kvinder, Køn & Forskning* nr. 1-2, 2012, p. 53: "Karen Barad, Professor of Feminist Studies, Philosophy, and History of Consciousness, University of California, Santa Cruz."

**One precision that matters.** Barad's own unit in that scene is **not one campus**. It is "Santa Cruz and Claremont", which are about 350 miles apart, and the Anzaldúa conversation happened at the Claremont end, where Barad was employed. So the primary text supports a two-place network with Santa Cruz as one node, not a single-campus story. That is a more defensible version of the claim and it costs nothing.

**And a second precision, in the other direction.** In "Nature's Queer Performativity" Barad calls Kirby and Schrader her "co-workers" and says their work and hers "have been in conversation with one another since before we met." **Kirby is in Sydney** (the lightning passage is written from "an aerie whose panorama includes a significant sweep of the Sydney harbor"). So Barad's own figure for intellectual proximity is explicitly **not** geographic. A place argument has to be made alongside that, not against it.

## 3. The fact that complicates the claim, and makes it sharper

The single most interesting thing found in this pass is not an affiliation. It is an exclusion.

**Anzaldúa was reportedly denied entry to the History of Consciousness program**, and enrolled in UCSC's Literature doctorate instead. One source states it directly: in 1986 she moved to Santa Cruz, and "in 1988, after being denied entry to the History of Consciousness program at the University of California at Santa Cruz, she enrolled in their doctorate program in literature."

**This rests on one source.** Two other accounts describe the same 1988 step with no denial in it, one saying she "was accepted into the Ph.D. program in literature", another calling it "the American literature program". No primary record of a History of Consciousness decision was found. **It needs verifying before it is used**, and the place to verify it is the UCSC records above or the Anzaldúa papers.

If it holds, the place argument changes shape. It would mean that in the same years, on the same campus, the program that held Haraway and would later hold Barad **turned Anzaldúa away**, and that the degree she eventually got came after her death. The campus would then be a site of exclusion and of connection at the same time, and the connection Barad records in 1991 happened in Claremont, outside it, between a physicist employed somewhere else and a writer the program had declined.

That is a harder and more specific claim than proximity, and it is the one the evidence is pointing at.

---

## 4. What enlarges the scale, and what does not

**What does.** Anzaldúa is the load-bearing case precisely because she is in a different field. A list of people in one program is a disciplinary story. A poet, a biologist-turned-theorist, an anthropologist of mind, a physicist and a filmmaker in one small city over roughly three decades is a different kind of claim, and Barad makes the cross-field join herself, in print, with a date on it: quantum physics and *mestiza* consciousness, *mita' y mita'* and the electron. **That join is already in a held text.** It does not have to be constructed.

**What does not.** Proximity on its own. Bateson died in 1980, the year Haraway arrived. Rich's Santa Cruz years and Anzaldúa's overlap, but no connection between them was found in this pass. Shlain was in San Francisco. Capra is not established anywhere. A map that puts all of these in one frame is a map of a region and a few decades, which is a weaker thing than the dated 1991 conversation.

## ADDED later on 29 Oct: the essay is built out of seven dated Santa Cruz coordinates

**This section corrects the shape of section 1 above.** Section 1 recorded the 1991 scene and the 1988 Trinh presentation as two primary items. Rereading "Diffracting Diffraction" against the reading list (`REREAD_against_reading_list_2026-10-29.md`) shows that **those are two of seven dated coordinates in one place, and that the sequence is the essay's organising device**. Barad sets it out on her first page and gives her reason for it:

> "we re-turn to a thicker 'moment' of spacetimemattering, which we might designate by **the spacetime coordinates Santa Cruz, CA late 1980s/early 1990s**, when, **thanks to the enormous labours and persistence of women of colour**, questions of differences broke through the breakwater of Universal Sisterhood, built on the foundations of sameness and shared commonalities, to become vital to, if not the lifeblood of, feminist theorizing. **This moment is dispersed/diffracted throughout the paper**, and this moment, like all moments, is itself a diffracted condensation, a threading through of an infinity of moments-places-matterings, a superposition/entanglement, never closed, never finished." (p. 169)

So the place-and-period is Barad's own analytic unit, not something to be assembled from scattered mentions, and she attributes the moment to the labour of women of colour. All seven are PRIMARY.

| Coordinate, in the order the essay gives them | What is at it |
|---|---|
| **"Santa Cruz, CA late 1980s/early 1990s"** | The frame, quoted above |
| **"Santa Cruz 1988"** | "Trinh Minh-ha is presenting her paper 'Not You/Like You' **for a UC Santa Cruz Cultural Studies gathering**", then two pages of Trinh quoted |
| **"Santa Cruz (the un/holy cross roads), sometime before 1987"** | "**Gloria Anzaldua is busy at work writing *Borderlands***, a foundational text in feminist studies. She is explaining the Coatlicue state, the prelude to crossing over." A second, earlier Anzaldua-in-Santa-Cruz coordinate, consistent with the secondary report that she moved there in 1986 |
| **"Santa Cruz 1992"** | "**Donna Haraway**, feminist theorist, master of the art of figuration, reads Trinh's account of difference through the figure of diffraction" |
| **"Crossroads/Diffraction: Santa Cruz and Claremont, CA 1991"** | The conversation with Anzaldua, recorded in section 1 |
| **"1982 - 2000 Elsewhere / 2010 Santa Cruz. Experimental meta/physics."** | The quantum eraser. **The three dates are citations**: note 59 gives Scully and Druhl, *Physical Review* A25 (1982), and Kim, Yu, Kulik, Shih and Scully, *Physical Review Letters* 84 (2000). A reference list written as a place and a date |
| **"Walking by the ocean in Santa Cruz"** | The closing section, first person, undated: "I re-turn again and again to thoughts of diffraction and entanglement. The conversation is ongoing. **The redwoods, the ocean, the paths taken and those which may yet have been taken hold the memory of these explorations by foot and by mind.**" |

**Barad dates her own arrival, which the certificate failure had blocked.** At the 1991 coordinate: "At the time Anzaldua was living in Santa Cruz. **I am already at the crossroads, but not yet in Santa Cruz.**" Note 21 is a full career note: "at this 'moment in time' that I'm inviting us to re-member, **when I had not yet moved to Santa Cruz** but was already entangled with conversations at the crossroads, I note one reference point. **By the late winter (early spring) of 1991, I had finished writing 'Meeting the Universe Halfway: Realism and Social Constructivism without Contradiction'** (in *Feminism, Science, and the Philosophy of Science*, eds Lynn Hankinson Nelson and Jack Nelson, Kluwer, 1996, pp. 161-194) **and had already received a couple of journal rejections** . . . In any case, **it did not find a home for another five years, six total**." So: Pomona physics in 1991, not UCSC; the book's title comes from a 1991 paper; and the paper was rejected for five years. That is three dates from Barad's own hand, and it means **the UNVERIFIED row for her career sequence can be narrowed without her CV**.

**The 1988 Trinh paper has a venue and a URL.** Note 8 gives it as *Inscriptions*, special issue "Feminism and the Critique of Colonial Discourse", 3-4 (1988), at `http://culturalstudies.ucsc.edu/PUBS/Inscriptions/vol_3-4/minh-ha.html`. ***Inscriptions* was the publication of UCSC's Center for Cultural Studies**, so the gathering and the text are both institutional artefacts of this campus, and the Trinh text the plan lists as not held appears to be free on the university's own server.

**Two more named people, both tying Barad to UCSC.** The acknowledgements thank "**Irene Reti** for all the many walks by the ocean and conversations about Gloria Anzaldua and her writings". A person of that name directs the UCSC Library's Regional History Project, the oral history programme that sits beside the History of Consciousness Board records this file wants to request; **that identification is not in Barad's text and needs checking before it is used.** And note 63 cites "**Martha Kenney**, *Fables of Attention: Wonder in Feminist Theory and Scientific Practice* (UCSC dissertation, June 2013)", the same Martha Kenney who co-organised the 2009 conference in note 1 of "TransMaterialities". **One named UCSC graduate student appears in both held texts**, once as an organiser alongside Haraway and Barad, once as a cited dissertation.

**And a citation-practice note of Barad's own**, which bears on `TRINH_reference_form_and_clarity.md`. Note 22: "This was one of the most sacred conversations I have had in academia. I treasure this memory, not because Gloria Anzaldua has moved on to other worlds, but because of the extraordinary quality of our conversation, the joy of it, the recognition of common passions in different languages and the gift of her generosity, kindness and focused presence. **I use her first name here because my sense is that using her surname would be too formal, stiff and artificial, and I didn't want to show disrespect in this way.**"

## ADDED later on 29 Oct: two notes from "TransMaterialities", and the Sydney node

Barad's "TransMaterialities" (*GLQ* 21(2-3), 2015, 387-422) was obtained and read after this file was written. Two of its notes bear on it directly, and both are PRIMARY. Close read in `BARAD_transmaterialities_read.md`.

**Note 1, in full.** This is the strongest single piece of evidence in the file, because it is institutional, dated, and names the room:

> "TransMaterialities is a term that arose in the planning of UCSC's 2009 'Trans Materialities: Relating across Difference' Science Studies Cluster graduate student conference, co-organized by Harlan Weaver and Martha Kenney, with faculty sponsors **Donna Haraway and Karen Barad**. The first time I saw the playful term matterealities was at a conference run by Monika Buscher at Lancaster University in 2007."

So **Barad and Haraway were co-sponsoring faculty of the same UCSC graduate student event in 2009**, run by the Science Studies Cluster and organised by two named graduate students, and **the title of Barad's 2015 article came out of that conference**. Before this, the Barad-Haraway link in this file was a reading relation (Haraway's 1992 essay read through Trinh in a 2014 article). This is a working relation in one place, with a year on it, and it adds two more names to the map, Harlan Weaver and Martha Kenney, who were graduate students there at the time.

**Note 36 dates Barad in Sydney:**

> "This material was presented during my talk, 'Multispecies Intra-actions: Queerness and Virtuality,' Distinguished Lecturer for Environmental Humanities, **University of New South Wales, Sydney, Australia, July 11, 2013**."

Vicki Kirby is at UNSW. Section 1 of this file already recorded that Barad's own figure for intellectual proximity is not geographic, because she places Kirby in an aerie above Sydney harbour. Now there is a date on which Barad was in Kirby's city, at Kirby's university, as a distinguished lecturer, presenting the material of this very article. **Both ends of that point are documented**, which makes the shape of the claim clearer rather than weaker: the network has a Santa Cruz node and a Sydney node, and the person who connects them travels. Notes 3 and 28 of the same article credit Kirby's *Quantum Anthropologies* (Duke, 2011) for the lightning material, note 28 specifically "for her attention to the untimely nature of lightning's connective engagement".

**Nothing in "TransMaterialities" mentions Anzaldua.** The 1991 scene remains in "Diffracting Diffraction" alone, and the Trinh question is not touched by it either.

**The honest position:** two documented working relations (the 1991 encounter across fields, the 2009 UCSC conference co-sponsored with Haraway), one documented 1988 presentation, one dated visit to Kirby's university in 2013, one program with an archive, one reported exclusion to verify, and a surrounding set of people who were in the same place without any established contact. The first five are the argument. The last is context.
