# Trinh T. Minh-ha: which name is which, how she is referenced, and the four texts held

Written 29 Oct 2026 in answer to two questions asked the same day: "which is her first and which is her last name. How is she referenced?"

---

## 1. The short answer

**Trinh is the family name. Minh-ha is the given name. The T. is a middle name, Thi.**

Her name is written in Vietnamese order, family name first, so "Trinh T. Minh-ha" is **not** first name plus middle initial plus surname. It is already surname first, and nothing needs reversing.

| element | what it is |
|---|---|
| **Trinh** | family name |
| **T.** | middle name, Thi |
| **Minh-ha** | given name |

So:

| context | correct form |
|---|---|
| first mention in prose | **Trinh T. Minh-ha** |
| every later mention in prose | **Trinh** |
| in-text citation | **(Trinh, 1989)**, **(Trinh, 1990, p. 77)**, **(Trinh, 1992, p. 86)** |
| possessive | **Trinh's** |
| reference list, written as she writes it | **Trinh T. Minh-ha. 1989. *Woman, Native, Other*...** |
| alphabetised under | **T**, for Trinh |
| never | Minh-ha, T. T. / Minh-ha, Trinh T. / (Minh-ha, 1989) / "Minh-ha argues" |

The complication worth knowing: because the name is already surname first, a reference list that mechanically inverts "Author, Initials" produces **"Minh-ha, Trinh T."** or **"Minh-ha, T. T."**, which moves her to the M section under her given name. That is how the wrong form spreads, and it is in the literature in quantity (section 3).

Two things supporting this, both from texts held in these folders rather than from an authority file. **Her own self-citations in the endnotes of *Woman, Native, Other* do not invert.** Where she cites everyone else surname-first ("30. Virginia Woolf, *A Room of One's Own*..."), she cites herself as: "33. Both the images of the Japanese boxes and the Taoist empty mirrors as related to writing have been discussed in **Trinh T. Minh-ha**, 'The Plural Void: Barthes and Asia,' *Sub-Stance* 36, vol. XI, no. 3 (1982), pp. 41-50." Also notes 46 and 22, both "Trinh T. Minh-ha." **The middle name is given in full as Thi in one held source**, a German dissertation that writes "Trinh Thi Minh-ha" twice (`todo` corpus, *Diffraktionsereignisse der Gegenwart*). The library authority form should be checked once and logged in the citation check before this is relied on in print (section 5, item 1).

## 2. How the authors in her own citational chain do it

Counts over the held full texts, so these are what the sources actually print.

| Source | "Trinh T. Minh-ha" | "Trinh Minh-ha" | "Minh-ha, T. T." | bare "Trinh" | bare "Minh-ha" |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Barad, "Diffracting Diffraction" (2014)** | 14 | 3 | 0 | 14 | 0 |
| **Barad, "Posthumanist Performativity" (2003)** | 0 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| **Barad, *Meeting the Universe Halfway* (2007)** | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| **Haraway, "The Promises of Monsters" (1992)** | 1 | 3 | 0 | 2 | 0 |
| **Posthuman Glossary (2018)** | 0 | 1 | **3** | 0 | 1 |
| **Fuser, "Nomadism in the Cinema of Trinh T. Minh-ha" (2019)** | 30 | 11 | 0 | 309 | 2 |

So the chain the proposal runs on (Trinh, then Haraway, then Barad) is consistent and it is consistent with Trinh's own practice: full name on introduction, **Trinh** thereafter, never Minh-ha alone. Barad does it 14 times each way in one essay. Haraway uses the shorter "Trinh Minh-ha" and then "Trinh." The *Posthuman Glossary* reference list is the one that inverts.

Across all 60 documents in the three corpora that mention her at all, out of 1,117 documents searched:

| form | occurrences |
|---|---|
| bare surname **Trinh** | 707 |
| **Trinh T. Minh-ha** | 153 |
| **(Trinh, YEAR)** | 122 |
| **Trinh Minh-ha** | 35 |
| **(Minh-ha, YEAR)** | 80 |
| **Minh-ha, T. T.** | 20 |
| **Minh-ha, Trinh T.** | 14 |
| **Trinh, T. M.** | 13 |
| **Trinh Thi Minh-ha** | 2 |

Five distinct bibliographic forms are in circulation. Three of them (Minh-ha first, and "Trinh, T. M.") file her under the wrong name.

**"Not You/Like You" is now held and read in full**, at `K:\OneDrive\UNM\Comps\Used texts\Trinh_T_Minh-ha_1988_Not_You_Like_You_Inscriptions_3-4.txt`, retrieved from the one Internet Archive capture that still returns the page. **A title correction for the citation check.** The archived page gives the title as "Not You/Like You: Post-Colonial Women and the Interlocking **Questions** of Identity and Difference", **plural**. Barad's note 8 in "Diffracting Diffraction" gives "Question", singular, and so do several reference lists in these corpora. The archived page is the better of the two authorities available here; the print *Inscriptions* 3-4 (1988) would settle it.

**Two things in it bear on this file's subject.** The essay carries **Trinh's own practice of the first person** at length, which is the form the name question arises in. And it has no page numbers, because the held copy is the Center for Cultural Studies web edition rather than a scan, so **citations to it can name the essay and not a page**, which is also what Barad does.
