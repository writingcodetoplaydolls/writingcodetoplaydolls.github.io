## 3. What he does, and you should know it before writing chapter 2

Chapter 2, "Cripqueering Method in Posthuman Educational Research: Diffractive writing-with A/autisms," opens with its own glossary, defining:

> **Neuroqueer (adjective):** The posthuman disidentificatory dimension of neurodiverse life.
>
> **A/autisms (abstract noun):** The deeply entangled contingencies and potentials produced at the intersection of 'autism' as a pathologising label, the lived reality of being marked as such, and the disidentificatory middling, with and against, the hegemonic identitarian assumptions produced by compulsory able-bodymindedness.

And then the sentence that places him:

> "**Writing as a queer, autistic educational practitioner**, I am aided by atypical forms of data (at least in the conventional qualitative sense). I write-with theoretical, **autie-biographical**, and personal journal writing as my data-points."

### The formal device, which is the thing

The chapter is built as a **montage of script-format extracts**, each tagged with a first name. His own journal entries appear as **FRANCOIS:**; others are **MEL:** (Mel Baggs), **TITO:** (Mukhopadhyay), **REMI:** (Yergeau), **JULIA:**, **KIMBERLY:**.

> "These extracts are presented in a script format, that is, as observational, experiential and affective accounts that are **to be activated through the reading**, rather than being considered as quotes referring to texts solidified in time and space. These textual interruptions are intended to be **rehearsed and re-performed** as a mode of practice."

And the justification for the repetition, in a footnote, which is the single most important sentence in the dissertation for you:

> "Re-turning, in this context serves an additional purpose by also **making use of repetition in alliance with the phenomena of echolalia** (the seemingly involuntary repetition of words or phrases typically associated as a 'symptom' of autism)."

**He has built an involuntary autistic repetition into the form of the writing and named it as method.** Some extracts recur, "repeated several times in echo-modulation," so that they land differently at different points.

That is the structural equivalent of what your research questions propose with stimming: an involuntary repetitive autistic act, reclaimed as the method rather than removed from it. He does it with echolalia in language. **He never uses the word stimming, and never works with movement.**

A sample of what the layout does, from the autistic perception section:

> **MEL:** The world is thick with sensation ... there is also the feel of it, the way the space between everything flows and moves and changes colour.
>
> **FRANCOIS:** The classroom never pauses for long enough to reveal its full composition.
>
> **MEL:** Objects are alive to us and interact with us as much as we interact with each other.
>
> **FRANCOIS:** Collectivities form and dissolve. ChairStudentPencil leans forward to meld with Paper/Table/Figureshadow.
>
> **MEL:** Objects ... us ... we ... each other.

The third Baggs extract is the first one, cut down. That is the echo-modulation working.

### He also answers a reviewer about plain language, in a footnote

> "During the peer review process, the writing that follows brought into question the notion of the value of 'plain language'. I appreciate the reviewer's suggestion that simple (or perhaps more didactic) language should be considered as a matter of accessibility, yet, the question comes to mind: **what is being accessed and how?**"

Worth having if anyone asks you the same question.

## 5. His *agencement* is not yours

Chapter 3.3 is "Reading Agency through Performativity as '*Agencement*'", and your section 2.5 is *agencement* in translation. His, in full:

> "The Deleuzoguattarian concept of *agencement* (**translated by Massumi as 'assemblage'**) delinks agency from the assumption of discrete subjects and objects to attend to the relational 'force of distributed directionality in the event' (Manning, 2016, p. 137)."

He takes it from **Manning's *The Minor Gesture***, notes the Massumi translation in a parenthesis, and moves on to use it. **He does not do the etymological argument.** No Nail, no reading of the French against the art-historical term, no claim about what the translation lost.

Your 2.5 is intact. It is also now a point you can make *against* a specific recent example: here is a 2024 dissertation using *agencement* 16 times and treating the translation as a footnote.

His **cartography** is Braidotti's ("propositional cartographies", Braidotti 2019), which is also yours. That one does overlap.

## 6. Where his project is not yours, in one paragraph each

**His site is the institution, not a practice.** He is a founding partner and the academic head of the Cape Town Creative Academy, a private art and design school in South Africa. The dissertation's question is "how notions of response-ability might be engaged to reconfigure conceptions of value in higher education research, pedagogy and assessment." The chapters are about classrooms, study and assessment. **"Art practice" appears twice in 67,796 words.** He is not making work; he is teaching, assessing and running a school.

**His first person is positional, not practice-based.** He writes as a queer autistic practitioner, and his data are journals and autie-biography. He does not produce artworks as part of the inquiry. The anthropocentrism problem you have been working on, where the researcher is also the maker and the "I" risks becoming the author of the work, does not arise for him in that form.

**His political ground is coloniality and neoliberalism.** 18 uses of coloniality, 12 of decolonial, sustained engagement with the South African private higher education sector, SAQA, CHE, DHET. Yours is a different politics with a different object.

**Braidotti is his background, not his spine.** 24 against your 94, and no transposition, no nomadic subject as a structuring device.
