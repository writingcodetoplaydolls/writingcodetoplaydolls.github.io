## 1. Citation audit

### Hinton's Barad 2012a, in the 2017 Kirby collection

All six quotations **verified verbatim** against the nothingness essay: "an unending dynamism"; "the un/doings of no/thingness"; indeterminacy as "key not only to the existence of matter but also to its non-existence, or rather, it is the key to the play of non/existence"; "no matter"; "Ontological indeterminacy, a radical openness, an infinity of possibilities, is at the core of mattering"; and the closing pair, "Infinity and nothingness are infinitely threaded through one another so that every infinitesimal bit of one always already contains the other. The possibilities for justice-to-come reside in every morsel of finitude."

**But the page numbers do not match this printing.** Hinton cites 11, 12, 15, 18 and 19. **The English section of this edition ends at page 17**, and the final two quotations are on EN 16-17. Her 18 and 19 fall in the German section. So she is working from a different pagination, possibly the e-book. **Verify the pages before citing; the words are right.**

### Davis's Barad 2012a and 2012b, in the same collection

A trap worth knowing: **Hinton and Davis use "Barad 2012b" for two different texts.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Hinton's 2012b** | "On Touching, The Inhuman That Therefore I Am", *differences* (pages 206-23). **Not held** |
| **Davis's 2012b** | Pages 32, 41 and 46, which fall inside **"Nature's Queer Performativity"** (25-53). **Held** |
| **Both authors' 2012a** | Pages 11-19, which is the **nothingness** essay. **Held** |

So in one 2017 book, two contributors cite "Barad 2012b" for different articles. Anyone building a bibliography from this collection will merge them.

### Dalziell's two citations

Her note 2, that Barad (2012) discusses cellular slime moulds and social amoebas and "the moral unease often aroused by ontologically indeterminate organisms": **correct, and understated.** The essay is **built** on social amoebas; it opens on them.

Her note 9, on the animate/inanimate distinction: **the substance is right.** Barad writes that "the very practices of differentiating the 'human' from the 'nonhuman', the 'animate' from the 'inanimate', and the 'cultural' from the 'natural' produce crucial materializing effects that are unaccounted for by starting an analysis after these boundaries are in place." I did **not** locate Dalziell's phrasing that this is "perhaps the most routinely undisputed distinction within the humanities", so treat that as her gloss unless it turns up elsewhere in the essay.

### Two printings of "Nature's Queer Performativity", and Barad disowns one

**This corrects what I wrote above the table.** The essay's own **notes 1 and 15** explain the 2011 in the filename, and the explanation matters for citing it:

> "There is a previously unauthorized version of this paper that appears in the graduate student run journal Qui Parle (2011/19/2 University of Nebraska Press). I am grateful to Hilda Rømer Christensen and the editors of Women, Gender, & Research for allowing me the opportunity to bring the authorized version of the paper to light."

And note 15: the remainder of the article, aside from the first paragraph of one section, "is the final version of the article . . . as submitted to Qui Parle. Unfortunately, this is not the version Qui Parle sent to the publisher, and attempts to have the error corrected failed."

So there are **two printings**, 2011 in *Qui Parle* 19(2) and 2012 in *Kvinder, Køn & Forskning* nr. 1-2, and **Barad repudiates the 2011 one**. The 2012 printing is the one to cite, and the 2011 in the held filename is presumably the year of the printing she disowns, not an error in the file name. Note 16 records one further difference between them: a footnote describing the *New York Times* article was in the original and was removed.

### The 2014 misdating

Davis's epigenetics chapter runs on "cutting together-apart" and cites it to 2012. **The article is *parallax* 2014.** If "cutting together-apart" is being cited to a 2012 text it is being cited to the wrong one, although Barad does use the phrase in the 2012 nothingness essay ("Intra-actions are practices of making a difference, of cutting together-apart, entangling-differentiating (one move) in the making of phenomena"), so both are defensible for the phrase and only the 2014 article is about it.

## 2. *What Is the Measure of Nothingness?* (2012)

A seventeen-page essay for a documenta catalogue, which means it is written for an art audience, and it is the most quotable Barad in the folder.

### The sentence for 1.5, and for the camera

Setting up the experiment of measuring a vacuum: to confirm there is nothing there you must measure it, but a flashlight "would introduce at least one photon (quantum of light) onto the scene, thereby destroying the very conditions we seek."

> "**Like turning up the light to see the darkness.**"

That is the measurement problem in seven words, and it describes the event camera (which cannot see a still body), the skin prick test (which can cause what it measures), and the sonogram, all at once.

### The measurement claims

- "Measurements are agential practices, which are not simply revelatory but performative: they help constitute and are a constitutive part of what is being measured."
- "measurements are intra-actions (not interactions): the agencies of observation are inseparable from that which is observed. **Measurements are world-making**: matter and meaning do not pre-exist, but rather are co-constituted via measurement intra-actions."
- "If the measurement intra-action plays a constitutive role in what is measured, then **it matters how something is explored**."
- And the distinction that matters: measured one way electrons are waves, measured another they are particles, and "what we're talking about here is not simply some object reacting differently to different probings but **being differently**."

### Two footnotes that answer standing questions

**Note 4, on complementarity**: "Bohr's notion of complementarity does not follow the colloquial usage. By 'complementarity' he means simultaneously '**mutually exclusive**' and '**mutually necessary**'."

That is a definition the superposition row needs. Two accounts can be mutually exclusive and mutually necessary at once, which is a stronger version of the standing rule about divergent readings than "they need not agree."

**Note 2, on scale**, which answers the 28 October question directly:

> "I don't want the reader to misunderstand and think that indeterminacy, or rather, the play of in/determinacies, is limited to the domain of the small. On the contrary, **the play of indeterminacies is ontologically prior to notions of scale** and, more generally, space and time. It's just that with current technologies they are more easily detected on relatively small scales."

With `BARAD_pages_pulled.md` section 5 and the parenthesis in *Diffracting Diffraction* below, **that is three places in held texts where Barad answers the micro-to-macro objection.**

Note 5 adds: "'zooming in' is not a uniquely human activity. For example, the larvae of sunburst diving beetles come equipped with bifocal lenses."

### The physics, and the figure for a blank page

Quantum field theory, built up through a drumhead analogy: a field assigns a value to every point in spacetime; quantising it means only discrete vibrational states exist; with special relativity, a field vibrating at a particular energy is equivalent to particles of a particular mass. So "vacuum fluctuations are the indeterminate vibrations of the vacuum or zero-energy state", and "**virtual particles are quantized indeterminacies-in-action**".

She rejects the usual story: "Virtuality is not a speedy return, a popping into and out of existence with great rapidity, but rather the indeterminacy of being/nonbeing, a ghostly non/existence." And the banker image that goes with it, "like a banker playing fast and loose with accounts, taking money out and paying it back before anyone notices", is "of questionable validity. The void is not a financial wheeler-dealer." Instead: "**Not even nothing can be free of ghosts.**"

> "Virtual particles are not in the void but of the void. They are on the razor edge of non/being. **The void is a lively tension, a desiring orientation toward being/becoming. The vacuum is flush with yearning, bursting with innumerable imaginings of what could be.**"

Then the passage for anyone who works on marks:

> "**The blank page teeming with the desires of would-be traces of every symbol, equation, word, book, library, punctuation mark, vowel, diagram, scribble, inscription, graphic, letter, inkblot, as they yearn toward expression. A jubilation of emptiness.**"

### The empirical hook

"It's Confirmed: Matter Is Merely Vacuum Fluctuations" (Stephen Battersby, *New Scientist*, 20 November 2008): most of the mass of protons and neutrons comes not from the quarks, which account for about 1 per cent, but from virtual particles. "an electron is not just 'itself' but includes a 'cloud' of an indeterminate number of virtual particles", and the measurable consequences are the Lamb shift, the Casimir effect and the anomalous magnetic moment of the electron.

> "**So even the smallest bits of matter are an enormous multitude. Each 'individual' is made up of all possible histories of virtual intra-actions with all Others.**"

With Derrida, *Aporias* (1993: 10): identity "can only affirm itself as identity to itself by opening itself to the hospitality of a difference from itself . . . the stranger at home."

### The close

"Nothingness is not absence, but the infinite plentitude of openness." "Matter is never a settled matter. It is always already radically open." "**The idea of finitude as lack is lacking.**" And an etymology of her own: infinities as "a constitutive part of all material 'finities,' or perhaps more aptly, '**af/finities**' (affinities, from the Latin, 'related to or bordering on; connection, relationship')."

Note 11 cites her own "Quantum Entanglements and Hauntological Relations of Inheritance" (*Derrida Today* 3(2), 2010, 240-68), **Kirby's *Quantum Anthropologies*** (which is held), and a new lead: **Astrid Schrader, "Responding to *Pfiesteria piscicida* (the Fish Killer): Phantomatic Ontologies, Indeterminacy, and Responsibility in Toxic Microbiology", *Social Studies of Science* 40(2), April 2010, 275-306.**

## 3. "Diffracting Diffraction: Cutting Together-Apart" (2014)

**The most important of the three for this project, and the reason is 2.2.**

### It opens on Trinh Minh-ha

`READING_PLAN_2026-10-27.md` has Trinh at 60 uses in the proposal's prose, lists Chen's 1992 "Speaking Nearby" interview as "the source of your third method term", and marks it not held, with no connective tissue between Trinh and diffraction. **This essay is that connective tissue**, and it has been in `Comps\Used texts` the whole time.

Barad structures the essay as a set of dated, placed scenes read through one another: Trinh at UC Santa Cruz in 1988 presenting "Not You/Like You"; Grimaldi in Bologna in the mid-seventeenth century; Anzaldúa in Santa Cruz before 1987 writing *Borderlands*; Young in England in the early 1800s; Haraway in Santa Cruz in 1992 reading Trinh through diffraction; Bohr in Copenhagen around 1927.

**Trinh, quoted:**

> "Many of us still hold on to the concept of difference not as a tool of creativity to question multiple forms of repression and dominance, but as a tool of segregation . . . The apartheid type of difference." And: difference "is not opposed to sameness, nor synonymous with separateness. [ . . . ] **There are differences as well as similarities within the concept of difference.**"

> "The moment the insider steps out from the inside she's no longer a mere insider. She necessarily looks in from the outside while also looking out from the inside. Not quite the same, not quite the other, she stands in that undetermined threshold place where she constantly drifts in and out . . . always at least two gestures: that of affirming 'I am like you' while persisting in her difference and that of reminding 'I am different' while unsettling every definition of otherness arrived at."

> "Differences do not only exist between outsider and insider, two entities. They are also at work **within** the outsider herself or the insider, herself, a single entity."

> "not only that we live in many worlds at the same time, but also that these worlds are, in fact, all in the same place, the place each one of us is here and now."

**That threshold passage is the first-person methodological position** the gap files are looking for, and it comes from the author already at 60 in the draft.

**Haraway, quoted (1992):** "Diffraction does not produce 'the same' displaced, as reflection and refraction do. Diffraction is a mapping of interference, not of replication, reflection, or reproduction. **A diffraction pattern does not map where differences appear, but rather maps where the effects of difference appear.**" And on the "inappropriate/d other": "To be inappropriate/d is not to fit in the taxon, to be dislocated from the available maps specifying kinds of actors and kinds of narratives, not to be originally fixed by difference."

### The etymologies, and there are three

Her own epigraph: "**Diffract, dif-frangere, to break apart, in different directions**". Grimaldi's coinage, "citing the Latin verb *diffringere*, *dis* (apart) and *frangere* (break)". And in passing, that diffraction "troubles the very notion of **dicho-tomy, cutting into two**, as a singular act of absolute differentiation."

### Re-turning, and the earthworms

> "re-turning, not by returning as in reflecting on or going back to a past that was, but **re-turning as in turning it over and over again**, iteratively intra-acting, re-diffracting, diffracting anew."

Her figure for it is compost: "the kinds earthworms revel in while helping to make compost . . . turning the soil over and over, ingesting and excreting it, tunnelling through it, burrowing, all means of aerating the soil, allowing oxygen in, opening it up and breathing new life into it."

And: "There is no moving beyond, no leaving the 'old' behind. There is no absolute boundary between here-now and there-then. **There is nothing that is new; there is nothing that is not new.**" Plus "Matter is a sedimented intra-acting, an open field. Sedimenting does not entail closure. (Mountain ranges in their liveliness attest to this fact.)"

### Grimaldi's experiment, and darkness as abundance

Sunlight through a pinhole into a dark room, a thin rod in its path, the shadow on a screen. "the boundary of the shadow is not sharply defined"; "the shadow is larger than the projected geometrical area; geometrical optics doesn't cut it." Then with a rectangular blade, diffraction fringes: "Bands of light appear inside the shadow region, the region of would-be total darkness; and bands of darkness appear outside the shadow region. **There is no sharp boundary separating the light from the darkness: light appears within the darkness within the light within . . .**"

Grimaldi's own observation, quoted: "**That a body actually enlightened may become obscure by adding new light to that which it has already received.**"

Barad's gloss: "**Darkness is not a lack.** Darkness can be produced by 'adding new light' to existing light . . . Darkness is not mere absence, but rather an abundance. Indeed, darkness is not light's expelled other, for it haunts its own interior."

Set against Anzaldúa, quoted on the same page: "There is darkness and there is darkness . . . Darkness, my night, is identified with the negative, base and evil forces, the masculine order casting its dual shadow, and all these are identified with dark skinned people."

### The line that reframes the superposition thread

> "Superpositions, not oppositions." And: "**Superpositions aren't inherent; they are the effects of agential cuts, material enactments of differentiating/entangling.**"

**That changes the question.** The folder now holds seven readings of superposition, and this says a superposition is not a property anything has but an effect of a cut. So the question for each of the seven becomes: what cut does this reading enact, and what does it exclude. That is a better organising question than comparing definitions.

With her own account of the cat: "it is not simply that the cat is both dead and alive, nor neither dead nor alive, nor part alive and part dead, nor somewhere between dead and alive; [rather, there] is not a determinate fact of the matter concerning the cat's state of being alive or dead. **It is a ghostly matter!**"

And on entanglement: "Entanglements are not unities. They do not erase differences; on the contrary, **entanglings entail differentiatings, differentiatings entail entanglings. One move, cutting together-apart.**" Plus "**One is too few, two is too many**", and the call for "a different sense of a-count-ability, a different arithmetic, a different calculus of response-ability."

### The scale answer, a third time

In a parenthesis: quantum physics "queers the binary type of difference at every layer of the onion (**not merely on the micro-scale as opposed to the macro-scale, as if there were a line in the sand between micro and macro** rather than an ongoing reconfiguring of spacetimemattering across and within spaces and times)."

### Anzaldúa, and a first-person scene from Barad

**Barad writes herself into the essay, dated and placed:**

> "Crossroads/Diffraction: Santa Cruz and Claremont, CA 1991. I am sitting outdoors with Gloria Anzaldúa talking about quantum physics and mestiza consciousness."

She was teaching in the Physics Department at Pomona, sitting in on **Deena González's** Latina Feminist Traditions class (the same Deena González that Bost cites in *Material Feminisms*), and co-organising a seminar reading *Borderlands*. "Gloria and I talk about quantum physics, the two-slit diffraction experiment, waves and particles and *mita' y mita'*. We are happily making diffraction patterns."

**A first-person precedent from Barad**, with a date, a place, an institutional position and a named interlocutor. For the first-person files this is worth more than another theory of the first person.

Then the pairing: Anzaldúa's *mita' y mita'*, "half and half . . . neither one nor the other . . . but a strange doubling, a deviation of nature that horrified", read against the electron. "Electrons are queer particles, *mita' y mita'*. They are particles. They are waves. Neither one nor the other. A strange doubling." And Anzaldúa: "What we are suffering from is an absolute despot duality that says we are able to be only one or the other."

**And the passage for an art practice.** Anzaldúa, quoted:

> "[B]ones often do not exist prior to the flesh [ . . . ] I like to think of them as **performances and not as inert and 'dead' objects** [ . . . ] the object/event [ . . . ] is 'enacted', it is both a physical thing and the power that infuses it."

Barad places that beside Bohr. An artist's account of her objects, set against a physicist's account of apparatuses.

### Other things to keep

Feynman on the two-slit experiment: "a phenomenon which is impossible, absolutely impossible, to explain in any classical way [...it] has in it the heart of quantum mechanics. In reality, it contains the only mystery." Barad's historical aside: "**Physics has always been spooked**", since Newton, "the chap who would 'feign no hypothesis'", was accused of mysticism when he banished the spirits and opted instead for action at a distance. The two-slit apparatus as "the ultimate ontological sorting machine". "There is no absolute outside; the outside is always already inside." "Agential cuts never sit still . . . An uncanny topology: no smooth surfaces, willies everywhere."

And the sentence that guards against reading all this as relativism: "**The existence of indeterminacies does not mean that there are no facts, no histories, no bleeding**, on the contrary, indeterminacies are constitutive of the very materiality of being."

The essay also points at **"Transmaterialities"**, which is on the get-hold-of list, and at **Marrano consciousness**.

## 4. "Nature's Queer Performativity" (2012)

Built on **social amoebas and slime moulds**, which makes it the text behind Dalziell's chapter.

### How it opens

A *New York Times* article, "Oozing through Texas Soil, a Team of Amoebas Billions Strong" (Yoon 2009), about a clonal colony forty feet across in a cow pasture outside Houston. Barad reads the fear in the prose, and names its ghost: *The Blob* (1958), a Cold War film about creeping communism. She then tracks that fear mutating through "the AIDS epidemic, the mad cow disease epidemic, and the avian flu epidemic", Islamophobia, antisemitism, "and the latest round of unabashedly racist anti-immigration legislation in Arizona".

On the organism: John Tyler Bonner's description, that slime moulds are "no more than a bag of amoebae encased in a thin slime sheath, yet they manage to have various behaviours that are equal to those of animals who possess muscles and nerves with ganglia, that is, simple brains". And Barad: "Social amoebas queer the nature of identity, calling into question the individual/group binary . . . its species-being defying not only classification by phylum but also by kingdom."

### The anthropomorphism position, which answers the first-person worry from another side

> "it would be a serious error to conclude that I am out to recount the sins of anthropomorphizing. On the contrary, I am deeply interested in 'anthropomorphizing' as an intervention for shaking loose the crusty toxic scales of anthropocentricism, where the human in its exceptional way of being gets to hold all the 'goodies' like agency, intentionality, rationality, feeling, pain, empathy, language, consciousness, imagination."

> "What I am suggesting is not strategic anthropomorphism per se, but **using the anthropomorphic moment to call the question**, not in order to reiterate the habits of projection, but rather, in fracturing the presumptions of the 'anthropos' of 'anthropocentrism', and in so doing opening up a space for response, that is, making an invitation to the other to respond by **putting oneself at risk and doing the work it takes to truly enable a response**."

`Posthuman_FirstPerson_Anthropocentrism_Notes.md` concludes that the defensible position is to keep the first person and change what it is for. **This is the same conclusion reached from the other direction**, and it adds a criterion: the anthropomorphic moment is permissible if it puts the writer at risk and does the work of enabling a response.

### The rest of the argument

Her critters are "amoebas, neuronal receptor cells in stingrays, lightning, a phantom species of dinoflagellates, academics (a strange companion species), and atoms". And the method is stated: "The approach used here is **not to invite nonhuman others into the fold of queerness, but to interrogate the binaries that support the divisions** that are at stake."

On the boundary: "the very practices of differentiating the 'human' from the 'nonhuman', the 'animate' from the 'inanimate', and the 'cultural' from the 'natural' produce crucial materializing effects that are unaccounted for by starting an analysis after these boundaries are in place." And: "what is needed is an account not only of the materialization of 'human' bodies but of all matter(ings)/materializations."

An etymological move on **critter**, which is "already internally queer", defined both in contrast to humans and in relation to them, "as a term of reprobation or contempt, but also sometimes as term of affection or tenderness".

And a line on using organisms as examples: they are "not merely as tools to think with, but in thinking with them to face our ethical obligations to them, for they are not merely tools for our use but real living beings (and I include in this category 'inanimate' as well as 'animate' beings)."

On moralism: "**Moralism, which feeds off of human exceptionalism**"; the nature/culture divide is "not just any old boundary but the very air it breathes"; and "to the degree that fear and moralism are whipped up into a fevered state aimed at safe-guarding the nature/culture divide, a compulsive breeching of that very divide inevitably breaks through, like some uncontrollable tic." Her legal case is "Crimes against Nature", via Harry Jaffa's 1989 *Los Angeles Times* piece and a Claremont Institute paper, whose logic is that "if homosexuality is not criminalized, the morality floor drops out". Her observation: the gravest crime against nature "is not mass killings of nonhumans or those deemed 'less-than-human' human Others, but 'unnatural sexual acts'". And "the unquestioned killability of nonhumans."

The essay is organised as a series of cases, and the sequence is the argument: each one is a phenomenon at a larger scale than the last, ending at the atom, which is the smallest. Barad calls them her "co-workers", which is not a decoration: she means that Kirby's and Schrader's readings and her own "have been in conversation with one another since before we met."

### The quantum eraser, which is the section the traces chapters want

The last case is the **quantum eraser experiment**, and Barad gives it in full and plainly: a two-slit apparatus with a which-slit detector made of a laser and two micromaser cavities, which marks which slit the atom passed by making an electron drop an energy level and leave a photon behind in the cavity. Run it without the detector and the pattern is a diffraction pattern; run it with the detector and the pattern is a scatter pattern; **erase the which-slit information and the diffraction pattern comes back, even if the erasure happens after the atom has already gone through and made its mark.** "This is direct empirical evidence that identity is not fixed and inherent, but performative."

Her disagreement with the physicists who built it is the part that matters here. They call it erasure and recovery; she says neither word holds:

> "**The trace of all measurements remains even when information is erased; it takes work to make the ghostly entanglements visible.** The past is not closed (it never was), but erasure (of all traces) is not what is at issue."

> "**Memory, the pattern of sedimented enfoldings of iterative intra-activity, is written into the fabric of the world. The world 'holds' the memory of all traces; or rather, the world is its memory** (enfolded materialization)."

> "**Changes to the past don't erase marks on bodies**; the sedimenting material effects of these very reconfigurings, memories/re-member-ings, are written into the flesh of the world."

Three sentences about traces that survive their own erasure, with an actual experiment under them. Two epigraphs sit over the section: Derrida's "a 'past' that has never been present, and which never will be" (*Margins of Philosophy*), and Schrödinger on entanglement as "the characteristic trait of quantum mechanics, the one that enforces its entire departure from classical lines of thought."

### The scale answer, a fourth time, and this is the irritated one

The conclusion returns to it, and the tone is worth noting because she is plainly tired of saying it:

> "Elsewhere, I have repeatedly emphasized that the ontological shift suggested by these findings is not necessarily circumscribed by the scale of the microscopic realm. **But somehow my exhaustive repetition and development of this point does not ward off panicked attempts to contain, tame, or normalize nature's queerness**, which will not be quarantined and is always threatening to leak out and contaminate 'life as we know it'."

That is why the essay is built on lightning and *Pfiesteria*: both are macroscopic, and lightning in particular "poses a set of quandaries, displaying an array of queer/quantum happenings, **from within the very domain of 'classical' physics**." Her closing question: "**What closet indeterminacies might be lurking in the presumably straightforward classification of micro and macro?**"

**Note 56** is the full statement, and it separates the two questions the critics run together:

> "There are two distinct issues here. One is how entities and events are understood. These are questions of ontology. A separate issue is whether the behavior of the entity in question can be accounted for in terms of the laws of classical physics or quantum physics. Surely one can consider lightning, for example, to be a phenomenon (in the technical sense of the proposed quantum ontology) and still be able to explain certain regular features of its behavior using the laws of classical physics. Also, interestingly enough, **those who challenge the importance of a quantum ontology for the macroscopic domain are happy to assume that it unquestioningly applies to submicroscopic domains all the way down to the smallest length scales.** Furthermore . . . scale is also not a straightforward concept, and notions of 'macro' and 'micro', like 'past' and 'future', are not nested or ordered in simple ways."

**Of the four places Barad answers the scale objection, this is the one to cite**, because it concedes what the physicists are right about (classical laws still describe lightning's regularities) and still refuses the conclusion they draw from it.

### The ending

"Ironically, in an important sense, **there are no 'acts against nature'**, not if they entail the sense of absolute exteriority that is usually assumed . . . there is no outside of nature from which to act; there are only 'acts of nature' (including thinking and language use)". With Kirby's question, which Barad poses as her own: "**What if we were to understand culture as something nature does?**"

Then the account of accountability: "Ac/counting, a taking into account of what materializes and of what is excluded from materializing, cannot be a straightforward calculation . . . **Accountability cannot be based on a mathematics of identity.**" And "all ac/countings are from within, not without." Closing line: "What if we were to recognize that **differentiating is a material act that is not about radical separation, but on the contrary, about making connections and commitments**?"

One further thing to know before using the moralism material, because it is heavy. Her argument that moralism traffics in equivalence relations rather than analogies runs through **note 7**, which quotes Hugh Raffles on Himmler's statement that antisemitism "is exactly the same as delousing", and Raffles's point that this is not a simile but an identity claim. **Note 20** uses the Stanton stages of genocide. Barad's own abstract puts it as moralism being "a genetic carrier of genocidal hatred". That is the register the essay ends in, and it is not a register this project needs to enter in order to use the lightning or the *Pfiesteria*.

**Note 57** names the companion piece: **Barad 2010, "Quantum Entanglements and Hauntological Relations of Inheritance", *Derrida Today* 3(2), 240-68**, which "might usefully be read as a companion piece to this article". Not held.
