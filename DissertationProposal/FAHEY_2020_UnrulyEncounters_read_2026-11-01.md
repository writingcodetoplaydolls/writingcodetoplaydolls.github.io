## 1. What the research is on, and the question

> "This practice-based research centres upon **censored operative images uploaded to YouTube by the UK and US militaries. These images present drone warfare as disembodied, clean and precise.** Through theoretical research and a fine art practice of **writing, video, sculpture and installation**, the project develops a response to the central question of the thesis: **how can art practice create an embodied encounter with these military drone images?**"

Her sources are a Ministry of Defence YouTube playlist, and the first entry in her list of images records that the material has moved: a screen grab from the RAF playlist "Operations", with the note that "**this playlist has now been deleted and the content has been absorbed into the MoD's YouTube playlist '#DefeatingDaesh'**", citing an individual video uploaded 25 November 2010. **A practice-based thesis whose primary material is state video that the state has since reorganised**, which is a provenance problem of exactly the kind this project knows.

**The iterative method is stated as a sequence, not a design:**

> "The generation of this research methodology has been **iterative; each of the four bodies of practice-based research assimilated appropriate and useful concerns from the previous one. These decisions were reached through critical reflection enabled by the installation and public exhibition of the work**, theoretical research and an ongoing dialogue with other artists working in the field."

**The finding, which is about vulnerability and resistance rather than about representation:**

> "In these encounters, **the vulnerability of human and non-human bodies, materials, spaces, technical processes and sounds also emerge as markers of their agency, interrupting the ideology of technical precision and bodies as calculative entities.** These vulnerabilities registered through **subtle human and non-human frictions and resistances** in the practice-based research generate **unruly experiential registers with bodies resisting categorisation**."

**The contribution claimed:** "an **embodied, decentred feminist methodology** and a body of artistic practice that intervenes in techno-scientific modes of visualising employed by the military and which have become prominent in art practice concerned with drone warfare."

## 2. The methodology: Haraway's situated knowledges, recalibrated

Her method chapter is built on one essay, **Haraway, "Situated Knowledges: The Science Question in Feminism and the Privilege of Partial Perspective", *Feminist Studies* 14(3), 1988, 575-599**, and she uses it with page numbers.

The **"god trick"** (581, 583): "a metaphor for how science furnishes a disembodied, objective form of knowledge through technically mediated positions. **Machine vision for example, promises 'vision from everywhere and nowhere, equally and fully'**", while Haraway also argues "against a position that claims to see from the vantage point of the subjugated and against appropriating this perspective."

The **split and contradictory self** (586): the one "who can interrogate positionings and be accountable", where splitting refers to "heterogeneous multiplicities" that are "within and among subjects".

**Then the move that makes this thesis useful here**, which is the reason the research sits in her own body:

> "**Employing situated practice and partial perspectives locates the research in my own body, rather than in or through the body of another**, with the acknowledgement that this position is split and contradictory, always partial and multi-dimensional."

> "As the research progressed, this position recognised **an openness and permeability to the bodies relevant to the research**; the body of the spectator of the videos on YouTube, the body of the drone operator, the targeted body, non-human and technological bodies, the body of the viewer of the practice-based research and **the body of the phantom data-double** among others. Yet it acknowledges that **this can only ever be a partial and constructed momentary inhabitancy**, one that is explored through and **perpetually returns to my own unstable body**, 'able to join with another, **to see together without claiming to be another**'."

> "Thus, **the research is located primarily in and through my body, permeable and porous to multiple other bodies.**"

**That is the answer to the question the first-person files keep circling**: why your own body. Because the alternative is working through someone else's, and Haraway's phrase "to see together without claiming to be another" is doing the work.

**Porosity is then named as the ontology, not the theme**, and it comes with a source the permeability notes do not have:

> "Political geographer **Marijn Nieuwenhuis** argues that **the notion of holes and pores destabilise ideas of authority, containment, control and binary relations** in what he terms '**porous politics**'. I explore porosity further in the forthcoming chapters as **a vulnerability that is also relational**, drawing upon **feminist reclamations of porous and leaky bodies**. In practice, **it is this destabilised ontology of porosity that I apply to Haraway's theorisation of the science question.**"

The citation is **Nieuwenhuis, M. (2019), "Porous Skin: Breathing through the Prism of the Holey Body", *Emotion, Space and Society* 33: 2.** **A paper titled "Porous Skin" that nothing in this folder's permeability material cites.**

**And the data-double**, which is the surveillance counterpart to the permeable body, via **Haggerty, K. D. and Ericson, R. V. (2000), "The Surveillant Assemblage", *British Journal of Sociology* 51(4): 614**: "disembodied flows of information, generated through the surveillance of an 'observed' and 'monitored body' abstracted and then reassembled into a '**decorporealised body**' of '**pure virtuality**'", "a 'new type of individual, one comprised of pure information', differentiated according to '**how useful they are in allowing institutions to make discriminations among populations**'."

## 3. She states the harm her own method risks

This is the passage to keep. It is not a hedge and it is not a disclaimer at the end; it is in the methodology, immediately after the position is established.

> "This approach, while attempting to situate a materially orientated conceptualisation of bodies at the core of the research, **acknowledges its own limitations in that this position is always a partial perspective. Yet at its most problematic, it places my white western body (operating in the realm of knowledge production in academic and cultural institutions), at the centre of the research, thus potentially disavowing the precariousness of the lives targeted by the operative image and those living and dying under drones.**"

She then works through **Spivak** on the impossibility of recovering the subaltern voice: western intellectuals and institutions "desire to re-present and give voice to a pure subaltern subject", but these are subjects "**whose consciousness we cannot grasp**", in "an oppressed position without agency, identity, social mobility or ability to speak which does not 'permit the formation of a recognisable basis of action'", and "**attempts to speak for but also listen to the subaltern can silence them**".

**Set against Kotzé**, who cites Spivak for the injunction that truthfulness is a necessary quality for an educator, the two theses use Spivak for two different jobs on the same page of method: one for honesty about the researcher's state, one for the limits of the researcher's reach. Both are held and read, and the pairing is worth keeping as it is.

## 4. Hybridity, and a term used with its history stated

Her definition of the key term is short and usable:

> "By '**feminist embodiment**', I mean **a relational condition in which nature and culture, interior and exterior, mind and body are entangled, contingent and mutable, and productive of each other**" (citing **Lisa Blackman, *The Body: The Key Concepts*, 2008: 34-35**).

> "**An embodied perspective is one that considers the notion of bodies as intertwined material, lived, sensing, inscribed, relational and agential entities.**"

> "**Art practice is a site where hybridity and interdisciplinarity can exist and thrive in the production of new forms of understanding contingent upon reasoning that is sometimes speculative and illogical.**"

**Two things in this section are models rather than content.**

**She names the racial history of her own term and keeps the term anyway, with the use specified:** "While I acknowledge both the **problematic racialised historical roots of the term 'hybridity'** and its associations with binary logic, in this thesis 'hybridity' is used in a specific relational way to describe the array of theories, methods, materials, methodologies, practices and bodies and **how they are intertwined** . . . My use of the term concerns **an attention to where they differ as well as align** and explores the provocative and dynamic tensions raised by these relations."

**And she gives a reasoned exclusion**, which is the thing Barnwell's chapter asks any method for: affect is deliberately de-prioritised, and not because it is unimportant. "my research **gives primacy to these other aspects of embodiment, because of the contentious relationship between affect and the media and the mobilisation of support for military warfare**, along with the complex issue of multiple and conflicting theoretical positions on affect."

**On the cyborg, with a critique of it she reports rather than makes.** Haraway's cyborg, "theorised and fabricated hybrids of machine and organism" (*Manifestly Haraway*, 2016: 7), born of "militarism and patriarchal capitalism, not to mention state socialism", though "**illegitimate offspring are often exceedingly unfaithful to their origins**" (9-10). And then: "**The figure of the cyborg has been criticised for reinstating ontological dichotomies through its reliance on 'connection,' as in the joining of two pre-existent parts, rather than 'entanglement'.**" That is a precise objection to a figure this folder uses, and it is the same distinction Barad draws when she insists relata do not precede relations.

With **Braidotti, *Metamorphoses* (2002: 104)** on relations as "the virtual links that a body can form with other bodies", a body as "an assemblage of forces, [. . .] a multiple phenomenon".

## 5. The glitch and the stutter, which is the chapter to read next

Chapter 5, "I Could Feel That My Eyes Were Open…", is a multi-channel video installation, and its material is the one the plan tracks through Braidotti, Bolt and Deleuze:

> "I explore **the logic of the glitch, both visually as artefacts and bodily, as vocal stutters**. The writing of **Simon O'Sullivan**, who draws upon . . . **Deleuze and Guattari's theorisation of a minor language**, is particularly relevant. In the work, **a fictional Skype conversation over a poor connection generates a mode of intimate attention, rather than representation or interpretation.** The juxtaposition of close bodily imagery and the pixellated operative image, along with the sonic registers of the technical and the vocal, generate a **multi-scalar, multi-perspective and multi-sensory vibratory encounter** with the images."

**The stutter as a vocal glitch in an installation, with Deleuze's minor language behind it**, which is the same stutter the plan has from Braidotti's four writers' tactics and from Bolt on handling, now carried out in practice. **Barad's "theorisation of touching, alterity and ontological indeterminacy"** informs the chapter, alongside Bennett on the agency of matter, so this is also where "On Touching" is being used by someone else.

Chapter 4, *Fuzzy Logic*: "**My body is also increasingly understood as unstable and porous through this work.** The installation . . . establishes **the posthuman assemblage as a methodological position** which accounts for the multiplicity of bodies acknowledged through my own **situated, yet destabilised, decentred and porous body**."

And the claim the whole thesis ends on: "a practice-based research methodology that generates an embodied art practice and **non-corrective body of work** based on a (new) **materially adjusted Butlerian vulnerability**", with "a more affirmative and responsible **politics of vulnerability**, one that does not deny how discursive practices inscribe and mark certain bodies, but also understands bodies as agential, meaning that they are **unpredictable, active and incalculable**."

## 6. What the two theses give, read together

They were requested together and they answer different halves of one problem, so this is worth stating once.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Kotzé** | How to analyse without coding, and how to account for your own selection as an agential cut. **The method's mechanics.** A master's dissertation, in an art classroom, with children |
| **Fahey** | Why the research sits in your own body, what that risks, and how to say so. **The method's position.** A doctorate, in a gallery, with state video |
| **Both** | Use **Spivak** in their methodology, for different jobs. Treat **porosity** as ontology rather than theme. Write in the first person throughout without arguing for it. And **name the limits of their own method inside the method, not in a concluding caveat** |
| **Neither** | Codes, themes, or a claim to have resolved anything |
