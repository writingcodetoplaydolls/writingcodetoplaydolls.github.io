## 2. Yergeau, "Practicing Plasticity"

> Asked: skip chapter 1 for now and read Yergeau's Practicing Plasticity section closely instead.

**K:\OneDrive\UNM\Dissertation\todo October 26\YERGEAU_PracticingPlasticity_closeread.md**

The section is pp. 128-134, the end of chapter two. The hinge is on p. 129:

> "The plastic, Catherine Malabou tells us, both gives and receives shape. As with rhetoricity, plasticity in aba discourse is halved, focused almost exclusively on how the neuroqueer can be shaped rather than how the neuroqueer shapes (and fucks, and defies, and writhes)."

Plasticity is halved the same way rhetoricity is halved. That is the whole move. Then Skinner's bowling pigeons as the mechanism of shaping, which Yergeau reads as Zeno's paradox (approximations halving the distance), and Thornton's neoliberal brain as "a kind of reverse Zeno's paradox" (improvability that can always double and never exceed).

The two existing approaches she sets against each other: Thornton does **the rhetoric of plasticity**, Skolnik does **the plasticity of rhetoric**. Skolnik is an unpublished 2012 manuscript, so that half is not obtainable.

Three things worth your attention.

**The "oo" is not decoration.** The last line of the chapter is "Oo. Oooo. Let us queerly practice." Earlier in the same chapter "oo" is the clinical abbreviation for **optimal outcomes**, from Fein et al.'s thirty-four children who no longer met diagnostic criteria, and Yergeau tells readers to sound it out as a self-stimulatory ooooo in public. So "compel the neuroqueer to drop the nq in favor of a nonphonetic oo" is trading neuroqueer for cured, and the final line takes the abbreviation back as a stim. "nq" appears exactly once in the whole book, in that sentence.

**The refusal to make it pretty**, p. 133: "I do not mean to suggest that autistic plastique is always of necessity positive, shiny, or cheery." Self-injury that can end in retinal detachment or death is in the same paragraph as the stim.

**The Manning citation leads somewhere else than expected.** Yergeau quotes Manning for "pure plastic rhythm is dynamic form in potential." In *Relationscapes* (p. 16) that phrase is **Umberto Boccioni's**, from Futurist sculpture, and Boccioni appears 45 times in Manning's book. So on p. 133 two unrelated plasticities sit side by side: Malabou's nitroglycerine and a Futurist sculptor's dynamic form. Neither is introduced to the other.

Three citation items flagged, none fixed: Yergeau dates *Relationscapes* 2012, your copy is 2009 (pp. 6 and 16 both check out against your copy); she writes "in potential" where Manning writes "in potentia" and "pre-definition" where Manning writes "predefinition"; and Manning's own Boccioni page ref (1964, 48) falls outside the page range her bibliography gives (50-57).

Malabou's *What Should We Do with Our Brain?* is not in any of your folders. Both Yergeau quotes come off p. 5 of it.

Schwab's own paragraph gives two Malabou page refs: p. 4 ("we run into this word in every neurology department of every medical school and of every university hospital") and p. 7 ("the brain is not already made"). His enemy term is **flexibility**, not rigidity.

**The best substitute for the missing book** is the *Posthuman Glossary*, which you have twice. Tom Giesbers, "Plasticity," pp. 320-323, entirely on Malabou. It gives the Greek *plassein* and the three senses (receive form, give form, annihilate form), the Hegel ground, where she parts from Derrida, the flexible-labour critique, "our brain is what we do with it," the brain-damage argument, and "if we didn't destroy ourselves a bit, we could not live." **Two more routes in the folder.** Louise Mazet's Rotterdam thesis on "the brain is the screen" (34 mentions, a diffraction grating on its cover and a Diffraction chapter) summarises the missing book and notes that Malabou's own examples in it are Deleuze, Resnais and Kubrick, so the book runs through artworks. Incidental: Schwab's reference list gives Kirkkopelto 2015 as pp. **48-53**, which settles the page range that three citing works disagreed about. His chapter in *Transpositions* is pp. 33-40.

## 4. Mazet read closely, and the Boccioni note

> Asked: read Rotterdam more closely. Also add a note to look for Boccioni in other texts. Leonard Shlain's *Art and Physics* book might have mentioned him, it certainly mentioned the Futurists.

**K:\OneDrive\UNM\Dissertation\todo October 26\MAZET_BrainIsTheScreen_closeread.md**

The thesis is 13,256 words, 39 pages, Erasmus Rotterdam, supervisor Sjoerd van Tuinen. It is an MA piece, so nothing in it is authority. But it cites Malabou 34 times with page numbers, which between them reconstruct the book that is not on the drives.

**The four-term distinction**, all in one paragraph, with pages:

| | | Malabou p. |
|---|---|---|
| rigidity | fixed, cannot change form. The opposite of plasticity | 4-5 |
| elasticity | changes form but always returns to the original | 15 |
| flexibility | changes to adapt instead of creating. A passive state, and only a small part of plasticity | 9, 12 |
| plasticity | gives and receives form, keeps the new form, cannot necessarily go back | 5, 15 |

So the enemy term is flexibility, not rigidity, and elasticity is the one that snaps back.

Other pages worth having: p. 1, "The brain is a work and we do not know it. We are its subjects, authors and products at once, and we do not know it." p. 39, the plasticity of the brain is the "real image of the world." pp. 72-77, "form and deform into the world." p. 78, the book's last line, "Not to replicate the caricature of the world: this is what we should do with our brain." And pp. 38-40, where Malabou's own examples are films, Resnais and Kubrick, so the book already runs through artworks.

**The section that matters most is Diffraction.** It makes the join Yergeau's p. 133 leaves unmade:

> "Reflections are representations; they do not make a difference, they do not create and thus they are not 'plastic'."

Reflection is to diffraction as flexibility is to plasticity. Both pairs separate adapting from creating, which is the same halving Yergeau finds in ABA. It also claims, sourced to Barad p. 83, that **crystals are diffraction gratings**, which is why Deleuze's crystal-image reads as diffractive cinema and why the thesis has a grating on its cover. And Patricia Pisters is a second route to Malabou; her *Cultural Studies Review* article is open access.

So Sullivan does not carry the Futurist material forward. Whether Shlain names Boccioni is still open.

## 5. Braidotti, "Writing as a Nomadic Subject"

> Asked: begin a deep read of the latest Braidotti nomadic subject text placed in the folder yesterday.

**K:\OneDrive\UNM\Dissertation\todo October 26\BRAIDOTTI_WritingAsANomadicSubject_deepread.md**

The file you put in yesterday is "Writing as a Nomadic Subject," *Comparative Critical Studies* 11.2-3 (2014): 163-184. 22 pages, 8,925 words, 39 endnotes. Not a research article: a retrospective on her own writing practice, twenty years after *Nomadic Subjects* and three after she rewrote it.

Five things in it that reach the rest of the work.

**Style as a navigational tool.** Her words, in the Nomadic Becoming section: "style is a navigational tool. It negotiates our path across sets of material ('matter-real') coordinates that, assembled and composed in a sustainable and enduring manner, allow for the qualitative transformation of the affects and the forces involved." The same paragraph says artistic genres are "variables co-existing along a continuum" and that writing practice installs "a sort of parallelism between the arts, sciences and conceptual thinking."

**The Deleuze stutter.** She sets out four tactics as a matched set: Deleuze tricks and makes language stutter, Foucault decodes and makes it falter, Derrida unveils and exposes its drive to mastery, Barthes seduces and reveals its affective core. That is the same stutter Bolt uses for handling, where it becomes "the body becomes language." Neither cites the other.

**The fold gets a citation.** "Conceptualized by Deleuze as the folding in and out of perception," and the self as "the internalized score of cosmic vibrations" is footnoted to Deleuze's *The Fold: Leibniz and the Baroque*. That reference was missing from the project.

**Flexibility.** This is the one I did not expect. Malabou is not in this essay at all, nor Barad, nor the word plastic, nor diffraction. Zero of each. Yet she makes Malabou's argument: "the state of so-called 'flexibility' of a large proportion of the working force... the universities and the research world are far from immune," and "advanced capitalism is a surveillance society, a system of a centre-less but constant security." Braidotti gets there from political economy, Malabou from neurobiology, and nothing connects them.

**Figurations are not metaphors**, stated flatly: "A figuration is a living map, a transformative account of the self; it's no metaphor." Then the long list of positions ending "these are no metaphors... it's history and belonging tattooed on your body." The conclusion then opens "The nomadic subject is a myth, or a political fiction." Both are hers; the essay does not reconcile them, and I left it that way.

Also worth having: "a translation without originals," "nomadism for me equals multilingualism," the creolisation effects that are acoustic ("I'm always writing with an accent"), and the future perfect, "I will have been free."

Two things to flag. **`319.-Nomadic-Ethics.pdf` in the same folder has no text layer at all**, a ten-page Xerox scan, so it is not searchable and I have not read it. And "latest" is ambiguous: this is the most recent nomadic-subject text in the folder, but `ScholarDownloads_Braidotti\Braidotti - 2024 - Foreword A Becoming-world of Collective Knowledge.pdf` is a decade newer on a different subject.

One caution recorded in the file: en and em dashes were flattened to hyphens during extraction, so any quote from my file needs checking against the PDF for its dashes before it goes into prose.

- **K:\OneDrive\UNM\Dissertation\todo October 26\319.-Nomadic-Ethics.searchable.pdf** (text layer attached, 48,658 characters, 7.6 MB)

The original PDF is untouched. The searchable copy is a 300 dpi re-render, not the original bitstream.

What it gives that the 2014 essay does not: **zoe defined against Agamben**, "the opposite of Agamben's 'bare life', in that it is a creative force that constructs possible futures." The 2014 essay uses zoe once and cites Agamben once and never joins them. It also has whole sections on becoming-woman and on Deleuze's critique of feminism, which the 2014 essay defers to "elsewhere." And its reference list names *Nomadic Theory: The Portable Rosi Braidotti* (2011), which you do not have.

**On art in the 2014 essay: there is almost nothing, and no visual artist, composer or filmmaker is named anywhere.** Sections 12 and 13 of the deep read have it in full.

The substring "art" returns 41, but those are *part*, *particular*, *Descartes*, *cartography*. With word boundaries, in 52,591 characters of body text: **art 2, arts 2, artist 1, artistic 1, aesthetic 1, painter 1, composer 1.** Zero for film, cinema, sculpture, dance, theatre, poetry, artists. Against writer 12, style 11, creativity 18. Roughly nine to one in favour of writing over art.

Six sentences mention art, and in all six it is an item in a list of parallel knowledge practices: philosophy, the arts, science. The only time a specific art operation is described (painters making visible forces that were not, composers making us hear the unheard) she is quoting Deleuze and Guattari. The one use of "artist" means Woolf. The one use of "aesthetic" is about Deleuze.

The only makers named are **three women novelists**: Woolf (16 mentions), Stein (one epigraph), George Eliot as Mary Evans (four, including that she translated Spinoza), plus Humpty Dumpty, with Carroll unnamed. Everyone else is a philosopher or theorist.

The sensory vocabulary that is hers is acoustic, not visual: accent, creolisation as acoustic, pitching a musical tone, the roar on the other side of silence, hearing the grass grow and the squirrel's heartbeat. Sight appears as the cartographer's accuracy and as the artist's eye meaning receptivity.

"Nomadic Ethics" is thinner still: art 0, artist 0, aesthetic 0, metaphor 0. Only "artistic and scientific practices have their role to play as well" and "new arts of existence," the second being Foucauldian usage. The only creative work in it is Adrienne Rich's *Blood, Bread, and Poetry* in the references.

Two unresolved. **Colebrook's page range disagrees between Braidotti's own two citations** of the same chapter: pp. 279-96 in the 2013 reference list, pp. 110-127 in the 2014 note 26. The OCR may have garbled the first. And **the OCR is uncorrected**; the reference list is the noisy part ("Nonsadic," "Sssians," "Spittoza," "Le Detexteme Sexe").
