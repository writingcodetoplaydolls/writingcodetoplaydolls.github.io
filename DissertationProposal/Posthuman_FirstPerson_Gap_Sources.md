**Why this lane matters more than the anthropocentrism one.** The anthropocentrism worry is answerable in a paragraph, because refusing the first person is the more anthropocentric move. Todd's piece is specifically about who gets cited. A chapter that leans on Braidotti, Barad, Deleuze and Guattari while citing no Indigenous thinkers is vulnerable on exactly the ground Todd names, regardless of what the prose says about decentering.

## 5. Resolutions of the seven flagged items

Checked 2026-10-26 against primary sources: the books' own front matter, journal tables of contents, Crossref metadata, and the full text of the TallBear article.

### Two of the seven changed something

**Plumwood: an error in the earlier version of this file, now corrected.** The essay's first publication is Plumwood, V. (1996). Being prey. *Terra Nova: Nature and Culture*, 1(3), Summer 1996, pp. 32-44. This comes from the ANU book's own acknowledgements page, which credits permission to reuse "previously published material," and from its works cited, which lists the same details. So the 1996 date is right and the 2000 date circulating online is presumably a reprint, which was not chased down.

The earlier version of this file said "Being Prey" is collected in *The Eye of the Crocodile*. That is wrong. The book has no chapter under that title. Its chapter one is "Meeting the predator," and its own footnote to that chapter says it "is a revised version of an article published by the journal Terra Nova titled 'Being Prey'." So the book holds a revised version under a different title, which is not the same thing as a reprint, and a chapter citation would not be interchangeable with the 1996 article.

One trap worth knowing about: **there are two journals called *Terra Nova*.** The one needed here is *Terra Nova: Nature and Culture*, a quarterly founded in 1996 by David Rothenberg at the New Jersey Institute of Technology, later associated with MIT Press, anthologised as *The New Earth Reader: The Best of Terra Nova* (MIT Press, 1999). The other is a Wiley geoscience journal, ISSN 1365-3121, which is what Crossref returns for a "Terra Nova" search and what a library search will surface first.

**TallBear: the citation held, the characterisation needs narrowing.** The full text confirms everything bibliographic, and the substance is stronger than the secondary summaries suggested. But the earlier version of this file called it "a positive first-person research ethic," which overstates the fit. The piece is written in the first person, and it is a positive ethic, but its object is the **relation between researcher and researched**, not the writing of the self. TallBear is working on who counts as a colleague rather than a subject, on studying up and across, and on being willing to be altered by the people one inquires among. She says nothing about autoethnography or about narration.

That narrows the use but does not remove it. The problem it solves is the one Pin-Ru Su's abstract raises via Jackson and Mazzei: that reflexivity does little to disrupt researcher hierarchy and may just re-privilege the ethnographer's voice. TallBear's answer is to change the relation rather than to confess harder in the prose. That is a structural answer, which puts it in the Warfield column rather than the Wilde column.

Two further findings from the full text. She grounds the approach in Haraway's situated knowledges (citing Haraway 1991, chap. 8) and Harding's standpoint theory, which means it connects directly to Haraway 1988, already in the comps bibliography. And her model of what "standing with" looks like in practice is Haraway herself: that Haraway does not merely study dog worlds but lives in them, participates in everyday technical conversations, does agility with her own dogs. That is a bridge between the decolonial lane and the multispecies lane that was not visible before reading the text.

## 5b. The "Being Prey" publication chain, resolved 2026-10-26

Chasing the 1996 printing turned up something the earlier entries did not anticipate: **1996 is not the first publication.** The essay appeared in *Quadrant* in March 1995, under a different title. That copy is now in hand, and the Quadrant masthead and page numbers are legible on the scan, so this is confirmed from the artefact and not from a database record.

| Year | Where | Title | Length | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1995 | *Quadrant*, March 1995, pp. 29-34 | "Human Vulnerability and the Experience of Being Prey" | 5,571 words | **Earliest known printing. In hand.** Scan confirms "QUADRANT MARCH 1995" running heads on pp. 29-34 |
| 1996 | *Terra Nova: Nature and Culture* 1(3), Summer 1996, pp. 32-44 | "Being Prey" | not seen | The version ANU credits. **Not obtained**, see below |
| 1999 | *The New Earth Reader: The Best of Terra Nova*, MIT Press, ed. Rothenberg and Ulvaeus | "Being prey" | not seen | Reprints the Terra Nova text. Confirmed in the book's contents via the East Carolina University catalogue record (xiii + 238 pp.). Page range cited elsewhere as 76-91 or 76-92, not confirmed |
| 1999 | *The Ultimate Journey*, Travelers' Tales | "Being Prey" | 4,240 words | **Abridged and edited. In hand.** |
| 2000 | *Utne Reader*, July-August 2000 | "Being Prey" | same abridged text | This is the source of the "2000" date |
| 2012 | *The Eye of the Crocodile*, ANU, ch. 1 | "Meeting the predator" | revised | In hand |

**So the 2000 date is a reprint, and that answers the question, but the more useful finding is that it is a reprint of an abridgement.** The widely circulating text with the strapline about a crocodile attack revealing the truth about nature carries its own credit line: "Adapted from *The Ultimate Journey* (Travelers' Tales, 1999)." It runs roughly 1,300 words shorter than the Quadrant text and has been edited, including into American measures and idiom. Comparing the two in hand: Quadrant has "after twelve years of protection," the abridgement has "after a decade and more of protection." Anyone quoting "Being Prey (2000)" may be quoting a magazine edit of Plumwood's prose rather than Plumwood's prose. That matters for a chapter about whose words get to stand.

**Can the 1996 Terra Nova printing be obtained?** Not online. *Terra Nova: Nature and Culture* was a quarterly founded in 1996 by David Rothenberg at the New Jersey Institute of Technology and published by MIT Press; it ceased as a quarterly and became an annual book series in 2000. No evidence it was ever digitised: it is not on JSTOR and has no MIT Press Direct archive. Three routes, in order of effort:
1. *The New Earth Reader* (MIT Press, 1999) reprints it. Second-hand copies are cheap and this is the realistic way to read the Terra Nova text.
2. Interlibrary loan of *Terra Nova* 1(3) through UNM, as a print article scan.
3. Treat the 1995 Quadrant text, now in hand, as the citable original. For most purposes it supersedes the question, since it predates Terra Nova and is the fuller text.

**One trap.** A search for "Being Prey" returns a 2013 article in *The Trumpeter* 29(1), "Being Prey: Dismantling the Emplacement/Displacement Dualism." That is **not** Plumwood. It is a different author writing about her. Checked and set aside.

**One loose end.** The Quadrant volume number is not printed on the scanned pages, only "QUADRANT MARCH 1995." Sources give it as volume 39 and as volume 29; volume 39 fits the journal's run from 1956. Confirm before citing a volume, or cite by month and year, which the scan does support.

**ADDED later on 29 Oct: Trinh answers the objection in section 4, and she does it in a text now held.** "Not You/Like You" (*Inscriptions* 3-4, 1988) was retrieved and read in full the same day; close read in `TRINH_NotYouLikeYou_read.md`, text at `K:\OneDrive\UNM\Comps\Used texts\Trinh_T_Minh-ha_1988_Not_You_Like_You_Inscriptions_3-4.txt`.

**This file has been carrying two objections.** Joan Scott's, by way of Siebers: experience used as evidence "reproduces rather than contests given ideological systems", and "It is not individuals who have experience, but subjects who are constituted through experience". And, as of earlier today, **Barad's rejection of reflexivity**, from *Meeting the Universe Halfway*: "the methodology of reflexivity mirrors the geometrical optics of reflection", and for all the emphasis on reflexivity as critical self-positioning "it remains caught up in geometries of sameness". That second one is the harder of the two for this lane, because reflexivity is the standard name for writing oneself into the research and it is ruled out by the apparatus this project borrows.

**Trinh's answer is neither to drop the I nor to be reflexive about it:**

> "**By refusing to naturalize the I, subjectivity uncovers the myth of essential core, of spontaneity and depth as inner vision.** Subjectivity, therefore, **does not merely consist of talking about oneself**, be this talking indulgent or critical."

> "a grasp of subjectivity as, let's say, **the science of the subject** or merely as related to the subject, **makes the fear of self-absorption look absurd**."

> "**Awareness of the limits in which one works need not lead to any form of indulgence in personal partiality, nor to the narrow conclusion that it is impossible to understand anything about other peoples** since the difference is one of essence."

And the argument ends in a test, not a position. What is at stake is a practice of subjectivity "**unaware of its own constituted nature**"; "**unaware of its continuous role in the production of meaning**, as if things can make sense by themselves"; "**unaware of representation as representation**, that is to say, the cultural, sexual, political inter-reality of the filmmaker as subject, the reality of the subject film and **the reality of the cinematic apparatus**"; and "finally unaware of **the inappropriate other within every I**."

**Four clauses, each of which a passage can be held against.** That is the same shape as the criterion this lane took from "Nature's Queer Performativity" (anthropomorphising is legitimate where it puts the writer at risk and does the work of enabling a response), and it comes from **a filmmaker writing about her own practice**, which is nearer to this project than any of the theorists already in this file. Note also that the third clause names the **apparatus** of her own camera, which is this project's central term.

**One sentence to keep beside it**, because it is the guard against the obvious misreading: "**This is not to say that the historical I can be obscured and ignored** and that differentiation cannot be made, but that **I is not unitary**."
