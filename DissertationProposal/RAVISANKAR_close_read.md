## 1. What it is

A Barad dissertation in art education. The research question: "How can artmaking and philosophical inquiry expand current understandings of material and materiality?"

Structure:

| Ch | |
|---|---|
| 1 | Introduction, questions, the braid/wave figure, limitations |
| 2 | Heidegger and Merleau-Ponty, against Cartesian dualism |
| 3 | Artmaking, material, materiality, new materialism, Barad |
| 4 | Methodology: diffraction, post-qualitative, post-coding |
| 5 | **Material (Jasmine)**: her own practice with jasmine garlands, turmeric, pressed flowers |
| 6 | **Discourse (Textual Provocation)**: "Heaps of Language," "Barad Poems" |
| 7 | Conclusions and new beginnings |

Chapters 5 and 6 are the "findings," split by Barad's material-discursive pair: chapter 5 takes the material side through her making, chapter 6 takes the discursive side through experimental writing on Barad's own sentences.

The organising figure is a **braid**: strands (biographical, theoretical) twisted together, with "waves" for the diffraction. She reconstructs an actual braid as an artwork (Figures 4.1, 4.2).

## 3. The three places your project is not hers

### 3.1 She names your central problem and does not solve it

This is the most important paragraph in her dissertation, and it is buried in the Limitations section of Chapter 1:

> "The work of this dissertation is done through my own artmaking process, which seems to reify the centrality of the artist that I discuss a desire to overthrow. However, artmaking is the practice through which the understandings and conceptualizations about materiality emerge, and as such, it is an intrinsic part of the research process."

That is the anthropocentrism problem, stated by her, about her own work, with no answer offered beyond necessity. Her defence is that she could not have done the study otherwise. Everything in the Wilde/Herbrechter/Warfield/Butler material is an answer she did not have.

**Why she could not solve it is structural, not personal.** Her route out of Cartesian dualism runs through Heidegger and Merleau-Ponty: 392 combined mentions, a whole chapter. Phenomenology dissolves the subject-object split by deepening the account of a *perceiving subject*. It keeps someone at the centre doing the perceiving. So the artist comes back in through the method she used to remove the artist.

Your route runs through Braidotti, Trinh and Indigenous thought, which does not have that return built into it. Braidotti's nomadic subject is a positive account of *what the subject is instead*, not just a refusal of the Cartesian one. Ravisankar decentres the artist and puts nothing in the vacated position. You have something to put there.

**That is the difference to lead with**, and it is defensible precisely because she identified the problem first. Citing her limitation and answering it is stronger than not citing her.

### 3.2 She drops the feminism, and says so

> "Although Barad, and the other new materialists and posthumanist scholars referenced in this study are inherently working through a feminist perspective, I do not address it directly and engage with the queer feminist side of this theoretical sphere... the feminist side of these theories does not emerge as I had hoped. I do intend to engage with the implications of new materialism's feminist basis in the future."

Braidotti appears twice in 69,799 words. Trinh does not appear at all.

A project whose organising structure is situated nomadic subjectivities (mother, artist, programmer, Appalachian born, educator, autist, AuDHD) is doing the thing she set aside, not a variant of what she did.

### 3.3 No situated subject, no genealogy argument

She has no cartography, no account of position, and no engagement with the decolonial critique of new materialism's origin story. Watts, Todd, Indigenous: zero. Your section 1.5 argument about an omitted genealogy has no precedent in her work.

Worth noting she *does* cite Kaiser and Thiele's 2014 diffraction genealogy, the same three pages I flagged as omitting Trinh. So she inherits the standard origin story intact.

## 4. Where the overlap is real, and what to do about it

**Diffractive methodology applied to one's own art practice is genuinely her territory.** Chapter 4 is a careful, thorough account: Barad's reflection/diffraction table reproduced, post-coding, a critique of reflexivity, a refusal of Barone and Eisner's and Sullivan's arts-based strategies on the grounds that they "focus on the artist/maker as a primary agent." She cites jagodzinski and Wallin's 2013 critique of arts-based research as needing to move past humanist reflective practice.

Two consequences.

First, **your section 2.2 cannot present diffractive methodology in art practice as new.** It has a 2019 doctoral precedent with a 30-page methodology chapter. Read her Chapter 4 before writing yours.

Second, and more usefully: **she positions in arts-based research (39 mentions) and you position in art practice as research (19 mentions in your draft, 2 in hers).** Those are different fields with different literatures, Sullivan and Barrett and Bolt on your side. The fields are close enough that a committee will ask, and far enough apart that the answer is real.

## 6. One honest risk

Her Chapter 6 experiments on Barad's own sentences ("Heaps of Language," "Barad Poems," "On Touching Heaps of Language"). Your section 2.2 plans writing-as-praxis moves in a similar register. Hers is the weaker half of her dissertation by her own framing, which makes it both less of a threat and less of a model. Read it to see what did not work.
