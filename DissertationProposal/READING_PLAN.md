## 1. Assemblage cluster: start here, feeds 2.5

| Priority | Item | Pull this | Length |

| **1** | **Nail, T. (2017). What is an Assemblage? *SubStance* 46(1), 21-37** | The whole Strand 1 problem, already argued. Nail has a section headed "Agencement" that does the etymology: *agencement* comes from *agencer*, to arrange or lay out, meaning "a construction, an arrangement, or a layout," while English "assemblage" comes from French *assemblage* and means "the joining or union of two things." ### The Nail complication, which is worth keeping rather than resolving

Nail's advice is to dissociate the two words. Section 2.5 plans to read them through each other. Those pull in opposite directions, and that is the useful part.

The art term traces to French *assemblage*, the word Nail says already means the English thing, which means Strand 2 and Strand 1 are not two readings of one word. They are two different words that English collapsed. That is a stronger version of the premise, not a problem for it: a diffraction needs two things that do not resolve into one, and Nail supplies the best available argument that they never were one. Synthesis is what diffraction is defined against.

So the thing to pull from Nail is not a definition. It is the non-identity itself, as the grating.

## 2. Disability and neurodivergence: not a side cluster

The research questions name "autism as method: stimming, perseveration, proprioceptive attunement." That is a methodological commitment, so these are core sources, not background.

| Item | Pull this |
|---|---|
| **Yergeau, M. R. (2018). *Authoring Autism*. Duke** | The closest match in the folder to a stated method. Yergeau works on stimming and perseveration directly, and on whether autistic people are granted the capacity to author, intend and mean. The book's own method is storying. If "autism as method" is to be defended rather than asserted, this is where the defence comes from. **Note: this file is in the folder twice, byte-identical. Delete one and recover 5.7 MB.** |
| Shildrick, M. (2002). Monstering the (M)Other, ch. 2 of *Embodying the Monster* | Already inside Herbrechter's reference list, so it was the cheapest bridge available and you found it.
