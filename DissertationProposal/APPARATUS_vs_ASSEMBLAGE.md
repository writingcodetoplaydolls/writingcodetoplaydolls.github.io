**This is a Heidegger book, not a Barad book**, and it predates *Meeting the Universe Halfway* by three years. Worth knowing, because Beatty runs Bolt and Barad together as though they were one lineage.

### Her move: handling

> "this Chapter offers a counter-representational model based on notions of **handling and handlability**... **Handlability involves our concrete dealings with things in the world, rather than our abstract thinking about the world.** It is concerned with the logic of practice. In handling, as I will show, Dionysian blood comes to pulse through Apollo's veins."

Built from Heidegger's ready-to-hand, de Certeau's tactics, and Deleuze. Via de Certeau: "Decisions are made, not according to logical thought, but as a **direct and felt response to handling** elements."

### And the thing you will want from chapter 5

"Working Hot" is Mary Fallon's 1989 novel, read through Dianne Chisholm. Bolt uses it to get to **Deleuze on stuttering**:

> "The stutter no longer affects pre-existing words, but, rather itself ushers in the words that it affects; in this case, **the words do not exist independently of the stutter**, which selects and links them together. It is no longer the individual who stutters in his speech, it is the writer who stutters in the language system: **he causes language as such to stutter.**"

> "The vibration and stuttering of the language occur in the interaction of the matter of a body with the language system. This different valency shifts the notion of performativity from one in which **the body is inscribed by language** to one where **the body becomes language**."

That is a Deleuzian account of **productive disfluency**: repetition and breakage in language as what generates, not what fails. It sits directly beside Jonker's echolalia move and your stimming material, from a different direction and twenty years earlier.

# Part 2: Apparatus against assemblage

## 1. Barad draws the distinction herself

In *Meeting the Universe Halfway*, which is in your Comps folder, **apparatus appears 456 times and assemblage 23**. And she separates them explicitly:

> "**apparatuses are not mere instruments or devices** that can be deployed as neutral probes of the natural world, or determining structures of a social nature, but neither are they merely laboratory instruments or social forces that function in a performative mode. Apparatuses are not merely about us. **And they are not merely assemblages that include nonhumans as well as humans.** Rather, **apparatuses are specific material reconfigurings of the world** that do not merely emerge in time but iteratively reconfigure spacetimematter as part of the ongoing dynamism of becoming."

So the comparison is not one you have to construct. **Barad makes it, as a refusal.** An assemblage, for her, is the weaker thing an apparatus is not: a gathering that happens to include nonhumans.

And the function she gives it:

> "the **measurement apparatus is the condition of possibility for determinate meaning** for the concept in question, as well as the condition of possibility for the existence of determinately bounded and propertied (sub)systems... apparatuses provide the conditions for the possibility of determinate boundaries and properties."

## 4. What each term actually does

Set out from the sources, not from me.

| | **Apparatus** | **Assemblage** |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Barad, after Bohr. Also Foucault's *dispositif* (63 and 30 hits; a different lineage) | Deleuze and Guattari's *agencement*, after Nail; and DeLanda's separate systems version |
| What it is | "specific material reconfigurings of the world" | a gathering of heterogeneous components |
| What it does | **cuts**: produces determinate boundaries and properties that did not exist before it | **holds together**: components retain exteriority and can leave and join others |
| Where agency sits | in the intra-action; the cut is enacted, not made by someone | distributed across the components |
| Its characteristic verb | to enact, to cut, to resolve indeterminacy | to compose, to territorialise, to co-function |
| Scale | the apparatus defines the phenomenon's boundary | **DeLanda: all assemblages have the same ontological status, so "entities operating at different scales can directly interact with one another, individual to individual"** |
| In your draft | 118 | 104 |

## 5. Where this bites on your work

**The scale point is the one.** DeLanda's claim that assemblages at different scales interact directly, "a possibility that does not exist in a hierarchical ontology," is the ontological permission for moving in and out of scales. An apparatus does not give you that: it gives you one cut, locally, and a boundary that holds for that phenomenon.

**Your 2.5 and your 3.3-3.4 are currently doing different jobs with the two words.** 2.5 is "Assemblage: *agencement* in translation, read against the art term" and 3.3-3.4 is "Apparatus, site, and the artifact." On the counts, 118 to 104, the draft treats them as roughly equal partners. Barad does not.

**The readymade question you already wrote sits exactly on the seam.** Your own block: "A urinal becomes art through the frame that receives it... In Barad's terms the frame is the apparatus, and the readymade makes its cut visible. **Assemblage folds the cut into the arrangement.**" That sentence is the comparison in miniature, and it is yours.

**And Duchamp's 3 Standard Stoppages is the better case for it** than *Fountain*, because its apparatus is a measuring instrument and the work is the measurement.

## 6. One caution on the counts

In Leonardo, and in parts of the art literature generally, "apparatus" often means a piece of equipment rather than Barad's concept. The 1,229 Leonardo hits are not 1,229 Baradian uses. The Barad-sense count in Leonardo is closer to its Barad count, which is 154 across 46 articles.
