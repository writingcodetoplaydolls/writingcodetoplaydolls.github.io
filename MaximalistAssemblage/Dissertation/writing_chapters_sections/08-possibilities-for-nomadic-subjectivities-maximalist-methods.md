A note on how theorists are situated across these three questions. New materialism is a specific movement within the posthumanist theoretical scenario (Ferrando, 2013, p. 30; Ibrisim, 2020, p. 11, citing Ferrando, 2013), not a parallel or competing framework. This means the three questions are not sequenced as three separate fields but as three levels of resolution of the same theoretical and methodological project: from the broad ontological commitments shared across the field (Q1), to their operationalization as diffractive practice (Q2), to their instantiation in art practice as research (Q3). Because new materialism is nested within posthumanism rather than external to it, theorists appear across all three questions. Barad is the central figure of Q2 even though she identifies primarily as a new materialist, because NM is part of the posthumanist theoretical scenario. Braidotti, Haraway, and Bennett appear across all three, because their work operates at every level of resolution simultaneously. Practitioners in the APAR field — Bolt, Borgdorff, Hill, Lambert, Hillary, Andersen — are included under Q3 not because they self-identify as new materialist theorists but because their accounts of art practice as research are grounded in or accountable to NM ontological commitments, whether or not they deploy NM vocabulary. The question structure comes from the committee; the answer recognizes that the field does not partition along those lines, and says so explicitly here rather than negotiating the classification at every turn.

## 1. Design of the search

1. Design of the search

### 1.1 Rationale

1.1 Rationale

A maximalist tool was created to collect every return for every given query. I wanted all the results so that I could search them myself for patterns. I created tools that poked at the data from multiple angles, looking for stray citations, never dependent on a research repository's opaque non-deterministic ranking system, unstable result count, or undocumented retrieval ceiling.

The primary literature search read through the comprehensive exam questions:

What are the theoretical and methodological distinctions between posthumanism and new materialism? How have other posthumanist theorists conceptualized diffractive practice? How have new materialist theorists conceptualized art practice as research?

Google Scholar was chosen as the primary index because the theoretical ground, new materialism and posthumanism, are transdisciplinary, distributed across a multitude of research areas, which are published in a mix of journals, edited collections, monographs, theses, and institutional repositories that no single disciplinary database covers well.

### Instrument three: text analysis

Instrument three: text analysis

A Processing sketch reads the extracted text of each source, normalizes it, writes out its reference list as a separate file, and counts every word against a minimum-occurrence threshold. `CleanText.pde` flattens smart quotes and strips anything outside printable ASCII. `Word.pde` holds the word and its count. A second mode, `Compare2Texts.pde`, counts a word across two texts at once and surfaces only the words that appear in one and not the other.

Where instruments one and two ask what literature exists and who has taken it up, this one asks what a text says and what it says that another does not.

### Instrument four: full-text concordance

Instrument four: full-text concordance

Instruments one and two read what Scholar says about a text. This one reads inside the text. A Processing sketch walks the retrieved corpus, opens every PDF, text file and JSON it finds, and records each occurrence of a keyword with a thousand characters of context on either side. Each hit is bracketed between the printed page numbers found above and below it, so a phrase arrives already carrying a page.

Keywords are given as stems and expanded before the pass runs, which is why `femini` catches feminist and feminism in one sweep, `reflecti` catches reflection, reflective and reflexive, `transpos` catches transpose and transposition, and `diffract` catches diffraction and diffractive.

The sketch also reports on the corpus as it goes. It measures the mean and spread of characters per page, and a PDF below that threshold is judged image-only, skipped, and written to a `needs_ocr` log. Eighteen of those logs survive. Every concordance pass was therefore also an audit of which sources had no readable text layer.

### Instrument five: concept co-occurrence

Instrument five: concept co-occurrence

The examination turned on whether posthumanism and new materialism are the same project. Rather than settle that from the secondary literature, the corpus was measured for it. Every occurrence of each concept was recorded in context, 31,504 for posthumanism and 40,001 for new materialism, then reduced to one row per source file carrying the count of each, whether both appear, and the smallest character distance between them.

That distance is the measure. A source where the two terms come within a few dozen characters of each other is holding them together; one where the nearest approach is thousands of characters apart mentions both without relating them. An earlier run makes the window explicit, testing co-occurrence within 200 characters across 7,376 matches.

765 source files carry the measure. A further 3,372 are marked as less than full text and excluded from it, because a proximity measure cannot be taken on an abstract. Three successive runs survive, over 940, then 1,172, then 1,172 files, as duplicates were removed and partial texts identified.

### Instrument seven: coded close reading

Instrument seven: coded close reading

The instruments above locate and measure. None of them reads. Dense sources were read aloud, transcribed, and coded line by line, with columns for the code, the tag, the page, and the quotation.

Bennett's Vibrant Matter runs to 3,705 transcript rows, 2,013 of them carrying a note and 947 a tag, across 222 distinct tags, with the book partitioned into eleven sections. The most-used tags name arguments rather than topics: introduction and anthropomorphizing 108, postulate 39, negative dialectics 38, life is beyond material 32.

The same schema runs per examination question. Braidotti for question one, 48 coded lines. Artist case studies for question two, 27 rows with the artist, the work, the page and the quotation. Question three carries an outstanding list headed keep looking for text.

Every one of these sheets has a page column and a quoting column. A quotation was never recorded without the page it came from.

### Instrument eight: paragraph provenance

Instrument eight: paragraph provenance

The join between the coded reading and the draft. 111 quotations are numbered in a bank, each with its code, tag, page and text. 301 transcript chunks are numbered separately. A paragraph is then declared before it is written: a heading, a core claim, a summary of its sources, and two lists of identifiers naming the chunks and the quotations that support it.

A later sheet states the same logic as a query rather than a list, holding expressions like `Code contains: "Postulate-"` alongside page anchors for 63 paragraphs.

This runs the opposite way from an audit. An audit takes finished prose and asks which paragraphs lack support. This specifies the support first and writes the paragraph from it.

### Instrument nine: knowledge-graph visualization

Instrument nine: knowledge-graph visualization

The examination document itself becomes the corpus. People, works, concepts, movements, institutions, places, events and artworks are extracted from the text and joined by typed relations, then published as an interactive map with an index back into the source pages.

Ten raw extraction passes produced 975 node records and 923 edge records. Merged and reconciled, these become 650 nodes, of which 212 are people, 134 works and 170 concepts, joined by 817 typed edges.

The index runs the other way, from graph back to page. 614 of 648 indexable entities locate to a page in draft version 40, and 419 locate in the submitted examination. 22 texts in the corpus match no graph entity at all.

The full procedure is documented separately in the Knowledge-Graph Visualization Protocol.
