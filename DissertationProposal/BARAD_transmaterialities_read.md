## 1. Touch, which is the section this project needed

This is the part of Barad that speaks directly to the permeability question, and it is the reason this text was worth chasing. Two sections do the work: "Electric Interlude: Virtual Touch" (pp. 396-97) and "Back to Quantum Field Theory: A Touchy Subject" (pp. 398-402). Both are marked in notes 23, 24 and 30 as **borrowed in part from "On Touching: The Inhuman That Therefore I Am"**, which is still not held.

She begins from the standard physics account and lets it stand:

> "A common explanation for the physics of touching is that one thing it does not involve is . . . well, touching. That is, there is no actual contact involved."

> "The reason that the desk feels solid, or the cat's coat feels soft, or we can (even) hold coffee cups and one another's hands, is an effect of electromagnetic repulsion. **All we really ever feel is the electromagnetic field, not the other whose touch we seek.** Atoms are mostly empty space, and electrons, which lie at the farthest reaches of an atom, hinting at its perimeter, cannot bear direct contact."

And then refuses its conclusion, not by denying the physics but by going one theory deeper, from quantum mechanics to quantum field theory. The move is: the electron is never an isolated thing that could fail to touch another isolated thing, because it is already touching itself through the vacuum.

> "the electron does not exist as an isolated particle but is always already inseparable from the wild activities of the vacuum. In other words, the electron is always (already) intra-acting with the virtual particles of the vacuum in all possible ways. For example, the electron will emit a virtual photon and then reabsorb it. This possibility is understood as the electron electromagnetically intra-acting with itself. **Part of what an electron is, is its self-energy intra-action.**"

> "**Every level of touch, then, is itself touched by all possible others.** Particle self-intra-actions entail particle transitions from one kind to another in a radical undoing of kinds--queer/trans*formations. Hence **self-touching is an encounter with the infinite alterity of the self**. Matter is an enfolding, an involution, it cannot help touching itself, and in this self-touching it comes in contact with the infinite alterity that it is."

> "**All touching entails an infinite alterity, so that touching the other is touching all others, including the 'self,' and touching the 'self' entails touching the stranger within.** Even the smallest bits of matter are an unfathomable multitude. Each 'individual' always already includes all possible intra-actions with 'itself' through all possible virtual others, including those (and itself) that are noncontemporaneous with itself."

> "In an important sense, in a breathtakingly intimate sense, touching, sensing, is what matter does, or rather, what matter is: **matter is condensations of responses, of response-ability**. Each bit of matter is constituted in response-ability; each is constituted as responsible for the other, as being in touch with the other."

Note what is and is not being claimed here, because it is the thing to be careful about. **She is not saying that the electromagnetic-repulsion account of touch is wrong.** She is saying that the entities it describes are not separable to begin with, so the question "did they make contact" has no determinate answer. That is the same structure as the scale answer: an ontological claim, not an empirical correction. The *New Scientist* vacuum-fluctuation result in the nothingness essay (quarks accounting for about 1 per cent of proton and neutron mass) is the empirical anchor under it, and that result is in the held dOCUMENTA text, not in this one.

**Feynman on the same term, quoted by Barad at p. 399:**

> "Instead of going directly from one point to another, the electron goes along for a while and suddenly emits a photon; then (horrors!) it absorbs its own photon. Perhaps there's something 'immoral' about that, but the electron does it!"

She then reads **renormalization** as a disciplinary response to that: two infinities, one from self-touching and one from the "bare" particle, subtracted from one another.

> "the infinity of the 'bare' point particle cancels the infinity associated with the 'cloud' of virtual particles; in this way, the 'bare' point particle is 'dressed' by the vacuum contribution (that is, the cloud of virtual particles). The 'dressed' electron--the electron in drag--that is, the physical electron, is thereby renormalized, that is, made 'normal' (finite). (I am using technical language here!)"

> "It shows that all of matter, matter in its 'essence' . . . is a massive overlaying of perversities: an infinity of infinities."

The physics in that passage is accurate as a description of what renormalization does. The reading of it is hers, and she marks the joke as a joke.

## 2. Lightning, reworked, and what is new in it

Note 25 says plainly that parts of the lightning section are **borrowed from "Nature's Queer Performativity"**. So this is the same material read yesterday, and the confirmation is worth having: the queer performativity essay is the precursor, as predicted. The stepped leader and upward streamer sequence, the Uman quotations, and the "communication that has neither sender nor recipient until transmission has already occurred" question are all carried over.

Three things are new.

**The slow-motion video and what it actually shows.** Barad watches the Discovery Channel footage of Geoff McHarg's camera at the US Air Force Atmospheric Research Center in Colorado Springs and corrects the narrator:

> "What the Discovery program narrator does not mention, but the viewer is witnessing in the video, is a stunning feature of the not-yet-lightning flashes: the flashes of light do not just head downward for fifty yards and then change direction and head out again (much like a child's drawing of lightning). Rather, one sees erratic, disjointed sets of flashes tentatively testing out different pathways. The trace of each trial gesture vanishing as quickly as it appears."

**The second Uman quotation**, which is the one where the physics source itself runs out of explanation:

> "Why is the lightning channel so tortuous? The answer is not known, but some reasonable guesses may be made. . . . **More likely, the leader just doesn't know exactly where it wants to go, except that ultimately it wants to move downward.**" (Uman 1986: 83, 90; the emphasis is Barad's)

That is a working lightning physicist writing "doesn't know exactly where it wants to go" in a Dover popular book. It is the strongest instance in any of the four texts of Barad's recurring move, which is to find the agential language already inside the science writing rather than importing it.

**The electrifying-origins history**, which is new material entirely: the Miller-Urey experiment of 1953 with its sparking device standing in for lightning; the posthumous 2008 reanalysis of Miller's unopened vials yielding not five but twenty-three amino acids; Galvani's frog legs on the balcony; Giovanni Aldini's 1803 electroshock of the executed body of George Foster; and the 1816 textbook recommending electric shock for a stopped heart. The Aldini passage is quoted from Anne K. Mellor's essay, not from Aldini.

## 3. The electric face, which is a traces finding with a laboratory behind it

This is the second empirical spine, and for the traces chapters it is the most useful thing in the article, because the phenomenon is a pattern that appears, does the work, and then **vanishes before the thing it patterns exists**.

The lab is Michael Levin's and Dany Spencer Adams's at Tufts. Their primary papers are named in the notes and are real and checkable: **Adams, Masi and Levin, "H+ pump-dependent changes in membrane voltage are an early mechanism necessary and sufficient to induce Xenopus tail regeneration", *Development* 134 (2007): 1323-35**, and **Ai-Sun Tseng et al., "Induction of Vertebrate Regeneration by a Transient Sodium Current", *Journal of Neuroscience* 30(39) (2010): 13192-200**.

What the lab established, in Barad's account: *Xenopus* tadpoles can regenerate a tail only in their first seven days, and manipulating the electric field around the missing tail extends that window; a tail is "a complex organ containing multiple cell types: muscle, peripheral nerves, spinal cord, notochord, skin, and vasculature", so this is not skin closing over a wound; the same technique grew a complete frog leg, which frogs do not regenerate; and changing ion channels produced four-headed planaria, six-legged frogs, two-tailed worms, and tadpoles with working eyes on their backs.

Then the video, which Adams made by accident by leaving the camera running overnight:

> "When a frog embryo is just developing, before it gets a face, a pattern for that face lights up on the surface of the embryo. . . . We believe this is the first time such patterning has been reported for an entire structure, not just for a single organ. I would never have predicted anything like it"

> "A slash where the mouth will form shimmers into view, only to quickly fade away. A dot, signifying an eye, appears briefly on the left side of the embryo; a moment later, a matching dot flashes on the right. . . . **These features--the mouth, the eyes--didn't actually exist. In fact, many of the genes that are linked to their development hadn't even been turned on. It's only after the patterns fade, the ghost of features yet to come, that all the necessary proteins are activated.**" (Adams, quoted at p. 409; the emphasis is Barad's)

And Barad's own gloss, with the ordering stated exactly:

> "It is important to take in the fact that the 'electric face' appears and disappears before any actual features develop, that is, **prior to cell differentiation!** For example, the 'eye field' electrically paints out the location and structure of the eye and vanishes prior to differentiation."

The causal test is in the article too, and it is the part that makes this evidence rather than imagery: disrupting the pump that generates the potential produced abnormal facial development, which is why the pattern is read as a switch rather than a by-product.

**The one thing to get right when using this.** Barad's claim that this is a *quantum field theoretic* effect is explicitly marked by her as **conjecture**, in her own words:

> "**If my conjecture is correct**, it places the Levin-Adams regeneration investigations within the emerging field of quantum biology."

That is the clearest instance across the four texts of Barad flagging her own claim as speculative, and it should be quoted with the hedge intact. The bioelectric findings stand on the Tufts papers. The quantum reading of them does not, and she says so.

## 4. Stryker, and the diffractive poem

Susan Stryker, "My Words to Victor Frankenstein above the Village of Chamounix", *GLQ* 1 (1994): 237-54, is the other text this article is built on, and the one Barad works through most closely. Barad quotes it at pp. 238, 240-41, 242, 248 and 251.

> "The transsexual body is an unnatural body. It is the product of medical science. It is a technological construction. It is flesh torn apart and sewn together again in a shape other than that in which it was born." (Stryker 1994: 238)

> "the Nature you bedevil me with is a lie. Do not trust it to protect you from what I represent, for it is a fabrication that cloaks the groundlessness of the privilege you seek to maintain for yourself at my expense. **You are as constructed as me; the same anarchic womb has birthed us both.** I call upon you to investigate your nature as I have been compelled to confront mine." (Stryker 1994: 240-41)

Barad's reading of that second passage, which is the hinge of the whole article: "examine your own nature, stretch your own body out on the examining table, do the work that needs to be done on yourself . . . and discover the seams and sutures that make up the matter of your own body. **Materiality in its entangled psychic and physical manifestations is always already a patchwork, a suturing of disparate parts.**"

**The second Stryker text is the one about boundary and surface**, and it is directly on the permeability question. From "Dungeon Intimacies: The Poetics of Transsexual Sadomasochism", *Parallax* 14(1), 2008, 36-47, quoted at pp. 413-14:

> "From my forward-facing perspective I look back on my body as a psychically bounded space or container that becomes energetically open through the break of its surface--a rupture experienced as interior movement, a movement that becomes generative as it encloses and invests in a new space, through a perpetually reiterative process of **growing new boundaries and shedding abandoned materialities**: a mobile, membranous, temporally fleeting and provisional sense of enfolding and enclosure. This is the utopian space of my ongoing poesis."

**And the ending is a formal experiment that has a permission note attached to it.** The last four pages set Stryker's "Frankenstein" poem in italics and intercut it, line by line, with the voice of a virtual electron in roman type, speaking in the first person. Barad's acknowledgement records that she asked and Stryker agreed: "I would like to thank Susan Stryker for graciously accepting my proposal to have some of her poetics diffractively read through mine and, especially, her willingness to have her powerful poetry interrupted by the murmurings of the void". Note 71 repeats it: "With apologies to Susan Stryker for disrupting her powerful poem".

The electron's lines include: "I am inseparable from the darkness, the void." "There is no myself that is separable from it." "**Why am I not dead if there is no difference between me and what I am in?**" "While I struggle to come into being I am virtually annihilated and re(sub)merge into the nothingness, over and over again." "I will try out every im/possibility, every virtual intra-action with all beings, all times." "I am not myself. I am becoming multiple, a dispersion of disparate kinds."

For the first-person lane this is the strongest held precedent by some distance, because it is not a theorist writing about the first person: it is **a theorist writing in the first person as something other than herself, inside another author's poem, with that author's recorded consent.** The permission is part of the method and should be cited with it.

## 5. Scale, answered a fifth and sixth time, and more compactly than before

Three notes in this article take up the scale objection again, and note 65 is the most compact formulation in any of the four texts.

| Note | What it says |
|---|---|
| **29** | "I have repeatedly made the point that quantum phenomena are not restricted to some alleged 'micro' domain. Perhaps a(nother) large scale example like this one will help to defeat that misconception." |
| **60** | "this is further evidence that quantum effects, falsely believed to exist only at micro scales, are being detected at larger and larger spatial scales." |
| **65** | "I have tried to make the point over and over again that quantum phenomena are not restricted to the so-called micro scale. **Scale does not precede phenomena; scale is only materialized/defined within particular phenomena.**" |

That last sentence is the one to cite. It states the position in eleven words and it makes clear that the claim is about the status of scale, not about how big a system can be kept coherent. With note 2 of the nothingness essay ("the play of indeterminacies is ontologically prior to notions of scale"), the parenthesis in *Diffracting Diffraction*, and note 56 of "Nature's Queer Performativity", there are now **six statements of it across four held texts**, which is enough to say that the objection has a stated answer and to quote the strongest version rather than paraphrasing.

## 6. Place: two notes that bear directly on the Santa Cruz file

**Note 1, in full, which is the single most useful fact this text adds to the place argument:**

> "TransMaterialities is a term that arose in the planning of UCSC's 2009 'Trans Materialities: Relating across Difference' Science Studies Cluster graduate student conference, co-organized by Harlan Weaver and Martha Kenney, with faculty sponsors **Donna Haraway and Karen Barad**. The first time I saw the playful term matterealities was at a conference run by Monika Buscher at Lancaster University in 2007."

This is PRIMARY, dated, and institutional. It establishes that **Barad and Haraway were co-sponsoring faculty of the same UCSC graduate student event in 2009**, that the event was run by the Science Studies Cluster, that two named graduate students organised it, and that **the title of this article is a UCSC artefact**: the word came out of that conference, not out of Barad's desk. Before today the Barad-Haraway link in the place file was Haraway's 1992 essay read in a 2014 article, which is a reading relation. This is a working relation in one building.

**Note 36 adds the Sydney node, dated:**

> "This material was presented during my talk, 'Multispecies Intra-actions: Queerness and Virtuality,' Distinguished Lecturer for Environmental Humanities, **University of New South Wales, Sydney, Australia, July 11, 2013**."

Vicki Kirby is at UNSW. The place file already recorded that Barad's own figure for intellectual proximity is not geographic, because she locates Kirby in an aerie above Sydney harbour. Now there is a date on which Barad was in Kirby's city as a distinguished lecturer, presenting this article's material. So the Sydney connection is not a figure of speech in one essay; it is an institutional relation with a day attached. **Both ends of the "not geographic" point are now documented**, which makes the argument stronger: the network has a Santa Cruz node and a Sydney node, and Barad moves between them.

Notes 3 and 28 both credit Kirby's *Quantum Anthropologies: Life at Large* (Duke, 2011) for the lightning material, note 28 specifically "for her attention to the untimely nature of lightning's connective engagement".

**Nothing in this article mentions Anzaldúa.** The 1991 scene is in *Diffracting Diffraction* only.

## 7. Superposition, an eighth reading, and Barad on collapse

Two passages extend the superposition row, and the second one is a position statement rather than a definition.

The eighth reading, at p. 410: "**The virtual is a superposition of im/possibilities**, energetic throbs of the nothingness, material forces of creativity and generativity." With the exclusion stated plainly just before it: "The virtual is not a set of individual possibilities, one of which might yet be realized or actualized. . . . They are not the roads not taken or some yet unrealized potential future, the other to actual lived reality."

And note 63, which is where she states her own position on the measurement problem:

> "Although a common story of measurement in quantum theory is that the 'wavefunction,' which represents a superposition of possibilities, is collapsed on measurement and one of the possibilities is realized, **I argue that there is no collapse, that measurement intra-actions reconfigure possibilities**."

Put beside "superpositions aren't inherent; they are the effects of agential cuts" from *Diffracting Diffraction*, that completes the position: a superposition is not a property a system has, and it is not destroyed by measurement either. Measurement reconfigures. Both halves are needed to state her reading accurately, and the second half is in a note.

Note 63 also disclaims the obvious neighbour: "The notion of the virtual discussed here is based on my interpretation of quantum field theory. **It is not the same as Gilles Deleuze's notion of the virtual**, although there are some interesting resonances."
