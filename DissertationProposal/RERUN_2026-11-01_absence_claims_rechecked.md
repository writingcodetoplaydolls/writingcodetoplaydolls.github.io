### 1.4 Frayling. I was wrong yesterday, and the correction matters because it was my headline.

Yesterday I wrote that Frayling (1993) is "the founding reference of the whole field, and this plan did not have it". **That is true of the plan and false of the folder and false of the draft.**

**It is held twice**, and one copy was already searchable: `K:\OneDrive\UNM\Comps\compsnotused\Frayling_1993_ArtResearch.pdf` (the scan I OCR'd) and **`K:\OneDrive\UNM\Comps\Used texts\frayling_research_in_art_and_design_1993_OCR.pdf`, which had already been OCR'd by someone and carries "_OCR" in its filename.**

**And it is already in `Draft v37`, cited seven times with page numbers**, including the whole triad at p. 5 with "materials research", "development work" and "action research", and "where the thinking is, so to speak, embodied in the artefact". The draft also has **two Frayling quotations my read did not surface**: the big-R definition, "work directed towards the innovation, introduction, and improvement of products and processes" (p. 1), and "**Research is a practice, writing is a practice, doing science is a practice, doing design is a practice, making art is a practice**" (p. 4).

**The draft's artistic-research apparatus is already substantial and the plan does not reflect any of it**: alongside Frayling it cites **Borgdorff (2012), *The Conflict of the Faculties*** at pp. 87, 160, 161 (held, and with 13 Frayling references of its own), **Sullivan (2006), "Research Acts in Art Practice"** at pp. 19-20, **de Assis and D'Errico (2019), *Artistic Research: Charting a Field in Expansion*** at p. 3, and **Kaounas (2024)**. **What is new from the OCR is not the triad but Frayling's reservations**, which the draft does not have: the OED etymology of *research*, the Picasso interview, "research has become a status issue . . . and that, I must confess, worries me", and the RCA's refusal of degrees for work said to "speak for itself".

## 4. Caveats, including one false positive of my own

**One count above is wrong and I am leaving it out of the tables.** I searched for **Hausman**, the philosopher who reviewed Turbayne, and got 23 files and 191 occurrences, 120 of them in Benson's Berlin Dada article. Those are **Raoul Hausmann**, the Dada artist. **Carl Hausman the philosopher is not in this corpus**, and that absence claim survives; my regex did not distinguish the two names.

**What the counts include.** Case-insensitive matches over the extracted text of each PDF, including notes, reference lists, figure captions and front matter, so a count of 1 or 2 is usually a citation and not an engagement. Duplicate copies of the same text are counted as separate files, which is why several rows above list the same work twice. Word-boundary matching was used only where the table says so.

**What is still not searched.** **7 of the 1,264 PDFs**: the four blank one-page files, the portfolio wrapper, and two others. Nothing else is outstanding.

**The 29 Oct OCR output is uncorrected machine reading.** Counts drawn from the nineteen OCR'd scans are reliable enough for finding things and not reliable for quoting; every quotation from them needs checking against the page image.

**One thing this re-run does not touch.** The absence findings that were measured inside a single held book are unaffected, because they never depended on the corpus: Whitehead 0 across the five Barad texts, Barad 0 in the Romero volume, permeab\* 0 in Kirby 2011 and Colman 2023, the Hayles term counts, and the TOCE audit of 339,583 words. **Those all still stand.**
