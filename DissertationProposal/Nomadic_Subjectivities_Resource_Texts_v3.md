## 1. Texts that hit three or more axes (read these first)

1. **Jack, Jordynn. *Autism and Gender: From Refrigerator Mothers to Computer Geeks*. U of Illinois P, 2014.** · W M C N
   Rhetorical study of four gendered "characters" in autism discourse: refrigerator mother, mother warrior, computer geek, autism dad. Argues the clinical picture of autism was built around boys so autistic women go unseen. The one text that puts mother, computing and autism in a single frame. RSA Book Award 2015. Review: Ellen Herman, *Women's Review of Books*, Mar/Apr 2015.

2. **hooks, bell. *Belonging: A Culture of Place*. Routledge, 2009.** · W P A
   Essay-memoir of leaving and returning to the Kentucky hills ("Kentucky Is My Fate"). The country-to-city-to-country loop is itself a nomadic cartography, and hooks is already in your frame. Pair with *Appalachian Elegy: Poetry and Place* (UP of Kentucky, 2012) and *Where We Stand: Class Matters* (Routledge, 2000).

3. **Allison, Dorothy. "A Question of Class." In *Skin: Talking About Sex, Class & Literature*. Firebrand, 1994.** · W P A
   Poor white South Carolina; refuses to reduce her life to patriarchy, incest, or class alone. Also *Two or Three Things I Know for Sure* (1995). A model for autoethnography that stays a-personal in Braidotti's sense: the body as diagnostic instrument, not confession.

4. **Baraitser, Lisa. *Maternal Encounters: The Ethics of Interruption*. Routledge, 2009.** · W M
   Maternal subjectivity as "called into being" through relation to the child; interruption as the mother's constitutive mode. Each chapter opens with an anecdote and is itself interrupted by more anecdotes. The closest thing to a nomadic theory of mothering: subjectivity as discontinuous becoming. FWSA Book Award 2009.

5. **Rosner, Daniela K. *Critical Fabulations: Reworking the Methods and Margins of Design*. MIT Press, 2018.** · W C P
   Feminist technoscience plus craft history of computing: NASA's "Little Old Ladies" who wove Apollo core memory by hand. Mixes history, theory, personal experience and case studies. Gives a method name (critical fabulation, after Saidiya Hartman) for art practice as research that reworks technical history from the margins.

6. **Yergeau, M. Remi. *Authoring Autism: On Rhetoric and Neurological Queerness*. Duke UP, 2018.** · W C N
   "Autie-ethnography"; where the word neuroqueer first appears in print. Yergeau is in Digital Studies, so the autistic subject is already a networked, rhetorical, technological one. MLA First Book Prize.

7. **Nakamura, Lisa. "Indigenous Circuits: Navajo Women and the Racialization of Early Electronic Manufacture." *American Quarterly* 66.4 (2014): 919–941.** · W C P
   Fairchild Semiconductor's Shiprock, NM plant (1965–75): Navajo women's weaving skill recruited as "natural" circuit-assembly labor. Local to UNM, and the sharpest critique of the "women weave therefore women compute" romance in Plant.

8. **Plant, Sadie. *Zeros + Ones: Digital Women and the New Technoculture*. Fourth Estate, 1997.** · W C
   First sustained cyberfeminist treatise: Lovelace, the Jacquard loom, women as the "zeros." Read with Nakamura as corrective.

## 2. Mother + machine (M C)

- **Hayles, N. Katherine. *My Mother Was a Computer: Digital Subjects and Literary Texts*. U of Chicago P, 2005.** Title marks Mother Nature's displacement by the Universal Computer; also recalls when "computer" was a woman's job title. (*How We Became Posthuman* is already in your comps bib.) **Held** in `todo October 26`. Rein (section 10) builds a whole thesis on its "Computational Universe."
- **Braidotti, Rosi. "Mothers, Monsters, and Machines." In *Nomadic Subjects*, Columbia UP, 1994; 2nd ed. 2011.** The essay in her own corpus that ties the maternal body to teratology and technology. Italian expansion: *Madri, mostri e macchine* (2005; new ed. 2021).
- **Haraway, Donna. "A Cyborg Manifesto" (1985/1991)** and *Staying with the Trouble: Making Kin in the Chthulucene* (Duke UP, 2016). You have *Situated Knowledges* and *Modest_Witness* in the bib; "Make kin, not babies" speaks to mother-as-position.
- **Rich, Adrienne. *Of Woman Born: Motherhood as Experience and Institution*. Norton, 1976.** The experience/institution split is still the cleanest tool for separating mothering from Motherhood.
- **O'Reilly, Andrea. *Matricentric Feminism: Theory, Activism, and Practice*. Demeter, 2016.** Gives mothering its own standpoint rather than folding it into "women"; used by the autistic-motherhood thesis in section 5.
- **Buller, Rachel Epp & Charles Reeve, eds. *Inappropriate Bodies: Art, Design, and Maternity*. Demeter, 2019.** (unverified) Maternal art practice anthology.
- **Nelson, Maggie. *The Argonauts*. Graywolf, 2015.** Autotheory of queer pregnancy and becoming; a form model for a-personal autoethnography.
- **Joy, Angelique. "The Mother Image: Soft-Cyborgs & Neuroqueer, Postmother Speculations." Artist talk, ISEA2024 (Brisbane), session "Eco/Techno Feminism," subtheme Speculative Practices.** · W M C N. RMIT photography/digital-image artist, self-described neuroqueer, autistic, queer "mother/child." Autoethnographic creative-practice research with a crip/queer/feminist manifesto-as-method; soft sculptures moving toward virtual outcomes; includes "The Postmother Manifesto" (2023). Full paper PDF: isea-symposium-archives.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/2024_Angelique_THE_MOTHER_IMAGESoft_Cyborgs_Neuroqueer_Postmother_Speculations.pdf. Program page: isea-symposium-archives.org/?p=81155. Closest thing found to your exact position. **Now held** in `todo October 26`.
- **Lautenschlaeger, Graziele. "Post-Human Motherhoods: Reflections on mother-offspring bonding as symbiotic individuation in Contemporary Art." ISEA2023 (Paris), full paper, session "Non-human - Post-human Agency."** PDF: isea-archives.siggraph.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/2023_Lautenschlaeger_Post-Human_Motherhoods.pdf. The ISEA2023 catalogue that lists it is held in `October 31`. Not yet read.

## 3. Working-class women in the academy (W P, some A)

- **Tokarczyk, Michelle M. & Elizabeth A. Fay, eds. *Working-Class Women in the Academy: Laborers in the Knowledge Factory*. U of Massachusetts P, 1993.** Twenty explicitly feminist essays on academic socialization as student and teacher.
- **Dews, C. L. Barney & Carolyn Leste Law, eds. *This Fine Place So Far from Home: Voices of Academics from the Working Class*. Temple UP, 1995.** Mostly autobiographical; see Nancy LaPaglia, "Working-Class Women as Academics: Seeing in Two Directions, Awkwardly." Sherry Linkon (LAWCHA, 2016) calls it the founding text of the genre.
- **Sohn, Katherine Kelleher. *Whistlin' and Crowin' Women of Appalachia: Literacy Practices Since College*. Southern Illinois UP, 2006.** Pairs her own doctoral experience with eight nontraditional working-class Appalachian women. W P A.
- **Chavira-Prado, Alicia, ed. *The Feminist Alliance Project in Appalachia*. Peter Lang, 2018.** Autobiographical and autoethnographic narratives of women as students and professionals inside Appalachian academia. W P A.
- **Linkon, Sherry. "Working-Class Academics, Working-Class Studies: Still Far from Home." LAWCHA LaborOnline, 2016.** Short state-of-the-field piece.

## 4. Appalachia and the poor white South as theory, not elegy (P A)

- **Catte, Elizabeth. *What You Are Getting Wrong About Appalachia*. Belt, 2018.** Short rebuttal of the culture-of-poverty story in *Hillbilly Elegy*.
- **Harkins, Anthony & Meredith McCarroll, eds. *Appalachian Reckoning: A Region Responds to Hillbilly Elegy*. WVU Press, 2019.** First-person chorus; many pieces are themselves autoethnographic.
- **McNeill, Z. Zane, ed. *Y'all Means All: The Emerging Voices Queering Appalachia*. PM Press, 2022.** Section one is on the tension between "queer" and "Appalachian"; a template for holding minoritarian positions together. Sister volume *Deviant Hollers* (UP of Kentucky).
- **Engelhardt, Elizabeth S. D. *The Tangled Roots of Feminism, Environmentalism, and Appalachian Literature*. Ohio UP, 2003.** Emma Bell Miles as early Appalachian feminist; a bridge to new materialism.
- **Rice, Connie Park & Marie Tedesco, eds. *Women of the Mountain South: Identity, Work, and Activism*. Ohio UP, 2015.** Embodiment, maternalism, gendered space; contests the "Appalachian woman" stereotype.
- **Chambers, Cassie. *Hill Women*. Ballantine, 2020.** Food stamps in eastern Kentucky to Harvard Law; the matriarchal anti-Elegy. Memoir, so use as data not theory.
- **Wray, Matt & Annalee Newitz, eds. *White Trash: Race and Class in America*. Routledge, 1997**, and **Wray, *Not Quite White*. Duke UP, 2006.** Founding "white trash studies" texts: the slur as both class and racial marker.
- **Isenberg, Nancy. *White Trash: The 400-Year Untold History of Class in America*. Viking, 2016.** Notes the stereotype was coded masculine, so poor white women fall out of it.
- **Yaeger, Patricia. *Dirt and Desire: Reconstructing Southern Women's Writing, 1930–1990*. U of Chicago P, 2000.** Southern women's writing, the grotesque, underpaid labor.
- **Hubbs, Jolene. *Class, Whiteness, and Southern Literature*. Cambridge UP, 2022.** Recent synthesis; also her "Tainted by (White) Trash" (*Miscelanea*) on Allison and Bonnie Jo Campbell.
- **Dunbar, K., M. Coenraad & E. Iwatani (Digital Promise). "Appalachian problem solving and computational thinking." *Journal of Research on Technology in Education*, 2024.** · C A. Rural computational-thinking pathways rooted in regional funds of knowledge. Thin on gender, but the one place "computational" and "Appalachian" meet.
- **Article on Appalachian women and the gender digital divide via Third World feminist epistemology.** *Information, Communication & Society*, 2011, doi:10.1080/1369118X.2011.649773. (unverified author; pull by DOI) · W C A P.

## 9. The computational artist: criptech, cyberfeminist, cybernetic (added 1 Nov 2026)

Built from five links sent on 1 Nov 2026. Full resolution, issue tables of contents and the ASC programme are in `todo October 26\NOTES_to_chase.md`, section 18. **None of these is held, and none has been read.** The new **D** tag marks disability/crip, which is how most of this material arrives.

### 9a. Crip technoscience: the definition and two artists' method statements

- **Hamraie, Aimi & Kelly Fritsch. "Crip Technoscience Manifesto." *Catalyst: Feminism, Theory, Technoscience* 5.1 (2019). DOI 10.28968/cftt.v5i1.29607. Open access.** · C D
  The term's founding text: disabled people as experts and designers of everyday life, technoscience as critique, alteration and reinvention of the material-discursive world, and a refusal of cure and of independence and productivity as conditions of existence. Read first; the two Leonardo pieces below apply it. Sits beside Haraway and Barad in your frame ("material-discursive" is Barad's phrase).

- **Virdee, Aminder. "Staring Back: Hacking Intersectional Oppression through *Eco-Crip: Cybotanical Futures*." *Leonardo* 57.2 (2024): 188–196. DOI 10.1162/leon_a_02501.** · W C D (N: the Art in Flux chapter in section 10 describes her work as articulating "neurodivergent pain and rituals")
  A disabled South Asian computational artist writes her own 2021 transmedia installation through autoethnography, disability justice and critical theory, reworking medical bioimaging and South Asian botanical archives in code and digital painting with a DIY hacking ethos, traced back to *Keep This Leaflet* (2014). **The nearest published model for the form of your contribution: an artist's computational practice written autoethnographically as intersectional theory, in a peer-reviewed art-science journal.** "Staring back" is Garland-Thomson's (*Staring: How We Look*, 2009). **The article's three supplements are held** in `October 31` (one of them misnamed as a Cremaschi file); the article itself is not.

- **Lincoln, Erika Jean. "Crip-Techno-Tinkerism: A Neurodivergent Learning Style Meets Machine Learning." *Leonardo* 57.2 (2024): 173–178. DOI 10.1162/leon_a_02499.** · C N D
  An artist's own methodology, in which tinkering with dataset creation and transfer learning mirrors the varied ways neurodivergent people learn. The one text found that puts a neurodivergent maker's learning style and machine learning in the same method. Pairs with Yergeau (section 1) and with your own note that computation is "one path that allows you to be maximalist."

- **Gotkin, Kevin & Aimi Hamraie. "Remote Access: Crip Nightlife, Artistry, and Technoscience." *Leonardo* 57.2 (2024): 197–202. DOI 10.1162/leon_a_02489.** · C D
  Interview on the Remote Access crip nightlife parties (first held over Zoom, 22 March 2020) as "an ongoing experiment in access-making." Open-access companion: **Hamraie, Aimi & moira williams. "Remote Access: A Crip Nightlife Party." *Lateral* 12.1 (2023). DOI 10.25158/l12.1.7.** Access as collective aesthetic practice rather than accommodation. (leonardo.info lists the second author as Lichen Hamraie; Crossref gives Aimi. Check the current name before citing.)

- **The whole issue: *Leonardo* 57.2 (April 2024), "Criptech and the Art of Access," introduced and closed by Vanessa Chang & Lindsey Felt.** Also there: Forlano & Barrio, "From Data Doubles to Data Demons" (Forlano writes as a disabled cyborg elsewhere); Johnson, Chandler & Rice, "Resisting Normality with Cultural Accessibility and Slow Technology"; Mondelli & Justice, "Aesthetic In-Access: Notes from the CripTech Metaverse Lab"; O'Neill, "Dandelion Rebellion: Creating Crip Natures"; Ting, "Between Piano and Forte: Hearing with Aids"; Slater & McLain, "Unseen Sound"; Martin, "Experimental Modalities" (209–214). None of the issue's authors is in your comps .bib.
