## 5. How a concept is matched

Whole phrase, anchored on both sides, on text folded with NFKD and rejoined
across hyphenated line breaks. That is the form established on 3 November: NFKD
decomposes the fi and fl ligatures that NFC leaves alone, both-sided anchoring
stops a stem matching the tail of another word, and the line join catches
`assem-` newline `blage`. The longest string wins, so `diffractive methodology`
fires instead of `diffraction` on the same words.

**A shared concept is shared wording, not a shared claim.** Two texts using one
of her terms may be doing opposite things with it. No citation was written into
the proposal, no tag was written anywhere, and every row is a candidate for her
to accept or reject.
