## 2. The one source the draft already attaches it to, where the word is Amy's and not the author's

In `Draft_v37` and in `Comps_Draft_version_40`, identical sentence:

> "The conversion signals a transmogrification of the artist into 'the primary expert of the medium that she herself has created' (Kirkkopelto, 2015, p. 49)"

**The quoted phrase is Kirkkopelto's. The word "transmogrification" is not.** It is the draft's own word for what he describes: the conversion of an artistic medium into a medium of research. His surrounding claim, as the draft quotes it, is that the conversion is never completed: "it remains suspended between two stages; it can only suggest the transition by repeating it endlessly but always anew."

**Citation, traced:**

> Kirkkopelto, E. (2015). "Artistic Research as Institutional Practice." In T. Lind (ed.), *From Arts College to University / Från konstnärlig högskola till universitet*, Yearbook on Artistic Research 2015. Stockholm: Swedish Research Council (Vetenskapsrådet), pp. 41-53.

Two flags on it. **It is not in the reference list of either draft.** And citing sources disagree on the page range: 41-53, 48-53 and 49-53 all appear, so p. 49 is inside every version but the article's own start page is unsettled. A copy is on Academia.edu. The quotation has not been checked against it.

This is a different Kirkkopelto from the one the draft uses for transposition, which is **Kirkkopelto, E. (2018). "Abandoning Art in the Name of Art: Transpositional Logic in Artistic Research," in Schwab (ed.), *Transpositions*, pp. 37-38** (the transponent).

## 3. The only formal definition of the word in any source text

> **Jackson, Zakiyyah Iman (2020). *Becoming Human: Matter and Meaning in an Antiblack World*. New York University Press, p. 3**, quoted in `0275__How eva louise young (1861-1939) found me: on the performance of metadata in knowledge production`:

> "**Plasticity is a mode of transmogrification whereby the fleshy being of blackness is experimented with as if it were infinitely malleable lexical and biological matter, such that blackness is produced as sub/super/human at once, a form where form shall not hold: potentially 'everything and nothing' at the register of ontology.**"

Jackson appears a second time, independently, in Comps: van Wichelen, *More-than-Human Biopolitics*, summarising Jackson on Morrison's *Beloved*, where enslavement "transmogrifies the body and mind, as well as relations with domesticated animals, a biopolitical and ontological rearrangement/derangement."

This is the only place in 51 million words where someone defines the term rather than using it in passing. It defines it as **imposed** scale-and-category shifting, done to a body by a racial order, with "a form where form shall not hold" as the condition. Noting it because it is the formal definition on record, not because it transfers.

## 4. Haraway, who uses it more than anyone else in the non-Leonardo corpora

Four of the five Comps occurrences are hers, in three different books.

| | |
|---|---|
| ***Modest_Witness@Second_Millennium* (1997)** | the modest witness, the FemaleMan and OncoMouse "are **transmogrifications or trans-substantiations of each other; they are kin, tied to each other by the passage of bodily substance**" |
| ***Modest_Witness*** (second use) | Michael Jackson's "transmogrifications of himself and others through computer video technology into Cleopatra, ghoul, panther, machine, and superhero," located by Ramona Fernandez "within the traditions of African American tricksterism" |
| **"Otherworldly Conversations, Terran Topics, Local Terms,"** in Alaimo & Hekman (eds.), *Material Feminisms* (2008), p. 159ff | "my beginning with **the transmogrification of LeGuin's 'Carrier-Bag Theory of Fiction' (1989) to the bag-lady practice of storytelling**" |
| ***Staying with the Trouble* (2016)**, p. 11 | "the human that is **transmogrified in etymological Indo-European sleight of tongue into guman**, that worker of and in the soil" |

The pattern across all four: a shift between forms that keeps a tie, whether of substance, of kin, of lineage or of etymology. The first is the closest to a usable sense: figures that are transmogrifications *of each other*, and are kin because of it.

## 5. The Leonardo article, which is 59 of the 66 Leonardo occurrences

> **Ferguson, John R. & Bussigel, Peter (2021). "TRaNsMOGRiFiER: Fictional Narratives as Catalyst for Experimental Instrument-Building and Musical/Artistic Collaboration." *Leonardo* 54(2), 228-233**, in the "Music and Sound Art" special section.

A week-long installation, durational performance, instrument and fiction. Its frame is stated outright:

> "The frame for this project comes from **Bill Watterson's 1985-1995 comic strip *Calvin and Hobbes***; of particular interest is the March 1987 sequence that begins with Calvin inventing a transmogrifier, a device he claims can be used to **'turn one thing into another.'** Calvin's transmogrifier is an **upside-down cardboard box**, sometimes drawn with a 'destination' dial on the side, but **only Calvin and Hobbes can see the results.**"

The project extends the cardboard box to a gallery, and builds instruments out of randomly acquired objects.

So the word's best-known modern source is a comic strip, and the only art-research project named after it says so. Two of the remaining Leonardo hits are that same article's title reappearing in the section front matter and the annual index, so Leonardo's **independent** uses number five, listed below.

## 7. The dictionary

| | |
|---|---|
| **First attested** | **1650s** (verb); **transmogrification 1660s**. Etymonline calls it **of unknown origin**. |
| **Leading theory** | a vulgar or jocular formation on **transmigrate** (souls passing into new bodies), possibly crossed with **modify**; the OED suggests this. Dictionary.com calls it a pseudo-Latinism on *transfigure* or *transmigrate* + *-ify*. |
| **Early spellings** | *transmography*, *transmigrafy* (17th-18th c.) |
| **Early use** | a 1652 example; Aphra Behn uses it in a 1671 play |

No single origin is established. The **transmigration** root is the live one for scale and body movement: souls moving between bodies, not a thing changing shape in place.

## 8. What the sources do not supply

Nobody in these 51 million words uses it for **moving in and out of scales, spaces and times**. The closest any source comes:

- **Haraway's "transmogrifications of each other... kin, tied by the passage of bodily substance"**, which supplies the *tie that survives the change* but not the scale movement
- **Jackson's "a form where form shall not hold"**, which supplies the ontological instability, under an argument about imposed plasticity in an antiblack world
- **the transmigration etymology**, which supplies the between-bodies movement and nothing else
- **Means 1984's trumpet becoming a drum while pitch and rhythm hold**, which is a small clean statement of continuity across a total change of kind

The sense in the audio notes, data becoming an artifact that moves "in and out of digital space," and asking "how do we transmogrify between gesture texture textile," is not in the literature. It would be new work, and it is already recorded in Amy's own voice on 24 Aug 2026 with a timestamp.
