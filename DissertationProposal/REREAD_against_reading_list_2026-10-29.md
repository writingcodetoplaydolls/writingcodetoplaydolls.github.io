# The four Barad texts reread against the reading list

29 Oct 2026. `READING_PLAN_2026-10-27.md` read end to end, every theorist and concept it is looking for pulled out into a hunt list, then the list run across the four Barad texts read today and against *Meeting the Universe Halfway*, which is held in full and has only ever been read at about ten mapped pages.

**Why this pass was worth doing.** The four texts were read for what they say. This pass asks a different question: what is in them that the reading list is already looking for somewhere else. Eleven things came back, and four of them change a row in the plan. The largest single finding is that **"Diffracting Diffraction" is built out of seven dated Santa Cruz coordinates, not one**, so the place argument is Barad's own organising device rather than something to be assembled; and the second largest is that **the camera material section 1.1 has been hunting for is in *Meeting the Universe Halfway***.

## 2. The eleven findings

### A. "Diffracting Diffraction" is built out of seven dated Santa Cruz coordinates

This is the finding that matters most, and `SANTA_CRUZ_place_and_people.md` understated it badly, because the first read recorded the 1991 scene and the 1988 Trinh presentation and missed that **the essay's organising device is a sequence of dated coordinates in one place**, with Barad's own reason for choosing it.

> "we re-turn to a thicker 'moment' of spacetimemattering, which we might designate by **the spacetime coordinates Santa Cruz, CA late 1980s/early 1990s**, when, **thanks to the enormous labours and persistence of women of colour**, questions of differences broke through the breakwater of Universal Sisterhood, built on the foundations of sameness and shared commonalities, to become vital to, if not the lifeblood of, feminist theorizing. **This moment is dispersed/diffracted throughout the paper**" (p. 169)

The coordinates, in the order the essay gives them:

| Coordinate | What is at it |
|---|---|
| **"Santa Cruz, CA late 1980s/early 1990s"** | The frame, set out on the first page as a "thicker moment" and attributed to the labour of women of colour |
| **"Santa Cruz 1988"** | "Trinh Minh-ha is presenting her paper 'Not You/Like You' **for a UC Santa Cruz Cultural Studies gathering**", followed by two pages of Trinh quoted |
| **"Santa Cruz (the un/holy cross roads), sometime before 1987"** | "**Gloria Anzaldúa is busy at work writing *Borderlands***", explaining the Coatlicue state. A second, earlier Anzaldúa-in-Santa-Cruz coordinate |
| **"Santa Cruz 1992"** | "**Donna Haraway**, feminist theorist, master of the art of figuration, reads Trinh's account of difference through the figure of diffraction" |
| **"Santa Cruz and Claremont, CA 1991"** | The conversation with Anzaldúa, already recorded |
| **"1982 - 2000 Elsewhere / 2010 Santa Cruz. Experimental meta/physics."** | The quantum eraser. **Those three dates are citations**: note 59 gives Scully and Drühl, *Physical Review* A25 (1982), and Kim, Yu, Kulik, Shih and Scully, "A Delayed Choice Quantum Eraser", *Physical Review Letters* 84 (2000). So the coordinate line is a reference list written as a place and a date |
| **"Walking by the ocean in Santa Cruz"** | The closing section, in the first person and undated: "I re-turn again and again to thoughts of diffraction and entanglement. The conversation is ongoing. The redwoods, the ocean, the paths taken and those which may yet have been taken hold the memory of these explorations by foot and by mind" |

Four further things come with them.

**Barad dates her own arrival, which the certificate failure had blocked.** At the 1991 coordinate: "At the time Anzaldúa was living in Santa Cruz. **I am already at the crossroads, but not yet in Santa Cruz.**" And note 21 is a full career note: "at this 'moment in time' that I'm inviting us to re-member, **when I had not yet moved to Santa Cruz** but was already entangled with conversations at the crossroads, I note one reference point. **By the late winter (early spring) of 1991, I had finished writing 'Meeting the Universe Halfway: Realism and Social Constructivism without Contradiction'** (in *Feminism, Science, and the Philosophy of Science*, eds Lynn Hankinson Nelson and Jack Nelson, Kluwer, 1996, pp. 161-194) **and had already received a couple of journal rejections**, which makes for an interesting tale in light of the science wars of the late 1990s. In any case, **it did not find a home for another five years, six total**." So the title dates to a 1991 paper, rejected repeatedly, published 1996, and she was at Pomona physics, not UCSC, when she wrote it.

**The 1988 Trinh paper has a venue and a free URL.** Note 8: Trinh T. Minh-ha, "Not You/Like You: Post-Colonial Women and the Interlocking Question of Identity and Difference", ***Inscriptions*, special issue "Feminism and the Critique of Colonial Discourse", 3-4 (1988)**, at `http://culturalstudies.ucsc.edu/PUBS/Inscriptions/vol_3-4/minh-ha.html` (Barad's access date 26/02/2014). *Inscriptions* was the publication of UCSC's Center for Cultural Studies, so **the 1988 gathering and the text are both UCSC artefacts, and the text the plan lists as not held appears to be free on the university's own server**. If that URL is dead, the Wayback Machine is the next step.

**Two named people tie Barad to the UCSC archive and to the 2009 conference.** The acknowledgements thank "**Irene Reti** for all the many walks by the ocean and conversations about Gloria Anzaldúa and her writings". A person of that name directs the UCSC Library's Regional History Project, which is the oral history programme sitting beside the History of Consciousness Board records the place file wants to request; **that identification is not in the text and needs checking before it is used**. And note 63 cites "**Martha Kenney**, *Fables of Attention: Wonder in Feminist Theory and Scientific Practice* (UCSC dissertation, June 2013)" **(the same Martha Kenney who co-organised the 2009 TransMaterialities conference**, per note 1 of the 2015 article), so one named UCSC graduate student appears in both held texts, once as an organiser and once as a cited dissertation.

**The issue title is confirmed from a primary source.** Note 49 of the 2014 essay cites the forthcoming article as being in "*GLQ*, **Special Issue 'Queer Inhumanisms'**", with "gratitude to Harlan Weaver (2009) for the richly suggestive term 'transmaterialities'". Note also that **Barad's own forthcoming-citation spells her title differently from the published version**: "Transmaterialities: Trans/Matter/Realities" in 2014, "TransMaterialities: Trans*/Matter/Realities" in print.

### B. Barad's position on critique is in a note, and it is not the position the plan has her in

The plan's turn-against-critique cluster (Barnwell ch. 2) rests on Barad's "I am not interested in critique" from the Dolphijn and van der Tuin interview. **Note 63 of "Diffracting Diffraction" says something considerably more qualified**, and it is the methodology statement 2.2 was missing:

> "This is one way in which diffraction might be contrasted with (some forms of) critique, **which is not to suggest that diffractive analysis does not have anything in common with critique**, although questions of temporality and ontology figure differently. As Foucault points out, in 'What is Critique?' (1978), critique is not one thing, but 'seems to be condemned to dispersion, dependency and pure heteronomy'. **Diffraction is indebted to forms of critical analysis such as those put forward by Marx, Nietzsche and Foucault.** Indeed, both critique and diffractive analysis consider fundamental taking account of the (material-discursive) conditions of possibility in their historical-social-political-(naturalcultural) contingency. However, whereas **critique operates in a mode of disclosure, exposure and demystification** (see Eve Kosofsky Sedgwick, *Touching Feeling*), **diffractive reading might be understood as a form of affirmative engagement**. Diffraction is an iterative practice of intra-actively reworking and being reworked by patterns of mattering. **A diffractive methodology seeks to work constructively and deconstructively (not destructively)** in making new patterns of understanding-becoming."

Three things follow. **Barad aligns diffractive reading with Sedgwick's affirmative turn by name**, which puts Sedgwick in the methodology rather than only in the get-hold-of list. **The word "disclosure" is doing opposite work in two held texts**: here it names what critique does and what diffraction does not, while Hekman's chapter of *Material Feminisms* adopts disclosure in the intransitive OED sense as her own positive method term. That is a divergence of the kind this folder already treats as the claim, not a problem. And the same note names **Iris van der Tuin, "A Different Starting Point, a Different Metaphysics: Reading Bergson and Barad Diffractively", *Hypatia* 26(1), 2011, 22-42**, which is a published instance of diffractive reading as a method and brings in **Bergson**, who is on no list in this folder.

### C. Barad rejects reflexivity as a method, which the first-person lane has to answer

Not in the four articles but in the book, pp. 71-72, and it is a problem for any first-person method that calls itself reflexive:

> "Haraway's point is that **the methodology of reflexivity mirrors the geometrical optics of reflection**, and that for all of the recent emphasis on reflexivity as a critical method of self-positioning **it remains caught up in geometries of sameness**; by contrast, diffractions are attuned to differences, differences that our knowledge-making practices make and the effects they have on the world."

With Haraway's own sentence from the epigraph: "reflexivity, like reflection, only displaces the same elsewhere, setting up worries about copy and original and the search for the authentic and really real."

So the standard name for writing oneself into the research is ruled out by the apparatus this project borrows, and the replacement offered is diffraction. Nothing in `Posthuman_FirstPerson_Gap_Sources.md` or `Posthuman_FirstPerson_Anthropocentrism_Notes.md` deals with that, and it is a sharper objection than the anthropocentrism one because it comes from inside.

### D. Metaphor: three positions, all held, and they do not agree

The plan already records a live tension, Braidotti insisting "**these are no metaphors**" of her figurations against Hayles calling the web "**a metaphor**" and "an artifact, a created object". Barad's position is a third one, and it sits between them. From the book, pp. 71-72:

> "The phenomenon of diffraction is an **apt overarching trope** for this book. . . . Diffraction is **also an apt metaphor** for describing the methodological approach that I use of reading insights through one another"

> "**Of course, diffraction is also more than a metaphor.** As a physicist, I am taken in by the beauty and depth of this physical phenomenon that I can't help but see nearly everywhere I look in the world. In fact, I will argue that there is a deep sense in which we can understand diffraction patterns, as patterns of difference that make a difference, **to be the fundamental constituents that make up the world**."

"More than a metaphor" is not "not a metaphor". Haraway, in the epigraph Barad chooses, is more willing still: "Diffraction is an optical metaphor for the effort to make a difference in the world", and "a narrative, graphic, psychological, spiritual, and political **technology** for making consequential meanings." Four authors, four settings of the same dial, all in held texts. Recorded here side by side and left there.

**And Barad states the limits of one of her own figures**, which is a usable method precedent: at the tree-rings passage she introduces the figure, then writes that "the metaphor is" limited and that "another important limitation is that this metaphor does nothing to inter[vene in]" the thing she needs it to do. Naming what your own figure cannot do, in the text, beside the figure.

### E. The camera section 1.1 wants is in *Meeting the Universe Halfway*

Section 1.1 is **Opening: Camera**, and it has been looking outside the folder for first-person and art-practice camera accounts (the masked Hasselblad, Ewald's cameras given to children, the Ivinson and Renold chapter that is paywalled, the TVSS camera-to-skin, event cameras). **The most-cited text in the whole project has 13 camera, 41 microscope, 51 image, 38 drawing, 28 grating, 18 lens and 6 photograph, and none of it has been looked at.** Six things in it, each verified in the text:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Barad builds a camera piece by piece to make the indeterminacy argument** | Printed pp. roughly 115-125 by the extraction's position. The position measurement "is a flash camera mounted on a tripod"; "we need a camera or photographic plate or film for recording"; "If we were to hold the shutter of a camera open and move it around while taking the picture, the photograph would surely be" blurred; to measure momentum instead "the camera would have to rest on a movable platform rather than a fixed one"; and "the light (photon) is part of the agencies of observation". **The whole argument about what belongs to the apparatus and what belongs to the object is carried out on a camera**, by assembling and re-assembling one. This is 1.1 and 2.5 at the same time |
| **Barad took one of the photographs** | Figure 3 caption: "**Photograph 3a by Karen Barad**", of overlapping disturbances in a pond. The theorist photographed the phenomenon |
| **Figure 3b is a Berenice Abbott** | "From Berenice Abbott, '**The Science Pictures: Water Pattern**', reprinted with permission of **Mount Holyoke College Art Museum**, South Hadley, Massachusetts." A major twentieth-century art photographer's science work, borrowed from the art museum of a college where Barad taught. **An art-and-physics camera precedent inside the main text of the project** |
| **Figure 2 is a photograph of a shadow** | "Photograph of the actual shadow of a razor blade illuminated by a monochromatic light source", from Sears, Zemansky and Young, *University Physics*, 6th ed., 1982. The text beside it: there are "alternating lines of dark and light that make **the determination of a 'real' boundary quite tricky**", and dark lines appear in the centre of the lit region. **A trace, photographed, whose edges cannot be located**, which bears on 3.5 and 3.6 and on the skin and boundary lane |
| **Figure 7 is Thomas Young's own drawing** | "Thomas Young's original drawing showing interference effects in overlapping waves", and a chapter section is titled "**Quantum Variations on a Theme by Thomas Young**". An original scientific drawing used as a source, under a musical title |
| **Barad commissioned an illustrator, and co-signed one figure** | Figures 14, 17, 20 and 21 are "**Illustration by Nicolle Rager Fuller for the author**"; figure 34, "Entangled genealogies", is "Illustration by Nicolle Rager Fuller **and Karen Barad**". Also in the figure set: Eigler's scanning tunnelling microscope, a photosensitive brittlestar, a scanning electron micrograph of a brittlestar arm, Bohr's own two-slit sketches, and a **Dilbert cartoon** ("Shift happens") |

Two smaller camera notes: a **"point-and-shoot camera"** is used against representationalism; a **"camera obscura"** appears in a passage about "scientific accounts of bodies"; and a comparison is drawn "to a **digital camera that builds up a picture pixel by pixel**", which is worth putting beside the event-camera material in `NEUROMORPHIC_CAMERA_lens.md`, where the whole point is that each pixel reports on its own.

### F. A ninth reading of superposition, and it is Barad's own plainest one

From the book, in the wave chapter: "when two water waves overlap, their amplitudes combine to form a composite waveform . . . **This way of combining effects is called superposition. The notion of superposition is central to understanding what a wave is.**"

That is the textbook reading, in Barad's own hand, and it is not the same as "superpositions aren't inherent; they are the effects of agential cuts" from 2014. **So the divergence the superposition row tracks across six authors also runs inside Barad across her own texts**, which is a stronger version of the same point and needs no comparison across writers to make it.

### G. Pickering and Latour are held at second hand, in the book

The plan lists **Pickering's *The Mangle of Practice* as "Not held"**, reached through Hekman's chapter, and says "for a practice-based project this may be the better of the two" method sources. Barad's book has **20 Pickering hits, including a substantial critical endnote**: "While Andrew Pickering has been one of the few science studies scholars to take [practice seriously] . . . Pickering explicitly eschews the representationalist idiom in favor of a performative idiom. It is important to note, however, that Pickering's notion of [agency differs] . . . despite their shared rejection of humanism. Significantly, Pickering . . . ignores important discursive dimensions . . ." and ends on "Pickering's and other performative accounts of scientific practices". **Latour is at 21.** So both method figures can be read at one remove from a text already in the folder, with Barad's objections attached, before deciding whether to buy the books.

### H. The monster and mother thread now has a held primary text

The plan has a whole **"Mother and monster thread"**: Shildrick 1996, two extracts from *Embodying the Monster* including "Monstering the (M)Other", and Braidotti's "Mothers, Monsters, and Machines", with the finding that nothing is held in either place and the Italian book has no translation. **"TransMaterialities" is a monster text, held and read**: monster 7 uses, womb 10, Frankenstein throughout, Haraway's "The Promises of Monsters" invoked in note 62, Stryker's monster affinity quoted at length, a section titled "Monstrous Selves, Transgender Empowerment, Transgender Rage", and the "anarchic womb" and self-birthing material. Its epigraph is also a new get-hold-of item on the skin side:

> "The monster always represents the disruption of categories, the destruction of boundaries, and the presence of impurities and so we need monsters and we need to recognize and celebrate our own monstrosities." (Judith Halberstam, ***Skin Shows***)

### I. Barad concedes the Bohr-fidelity charge in advance

The plan's row on the published critiques of agential realism leads with **Faye and Jaksland (2021) on "a substantial misreading of Bohr"**. Note 27 of "Diffracting Diffraction", seven years earlier: "**My account of Bohr's philosophy-physics in this paper, as elsewhere, is not faithful to Bohr (as if it could be)**, but rather is always already diffracted through my agential realist understanding of Bohr's insights." Whatever that settles, it should be quoted in the same place as the critique, because it changes what the critique is a critique of.

### J. Two unpublished book manuscripts, announced eleven and twelve years ago

Note 30 of "Diffracting Diffraction" (2014): "Karen Barad, ***Ghostly Times: Entanglements, Intra-activity, and Différance*, book manuscript, forthcoming**." Note 69 of "TransMaterialities" (2015): "Barad, ***Infinity, Nothingness, and Justice-to-Come* (book manuscript)**." Two different titles, neither known to have appeared under that name. One search would settle whether either became a published book under another title, and the answer belongs in the citation-discipline file either way.

### K. Two smaller things worth keeping

**A sixth Barad etymology for the 1.5 row**, and a limit statement on porosity, in one passage at p. 179: "an **im-passe (from the Latin a-poria)**, but one that can't contain that which it would hold back. **Porosity is not necessary for quantum tunnelling**, a specifically quantum event, a means of getting through, without getting over, without burrowing through. Tunnelling makes mincemeat of closure, **no w/holes are needed**." That is the only appearance of porosity in any of the four texts, and it rules porosity out rather than in for the quantum case. **The same passage is the stutter**, the one hit in the four texts: "A rupture of the discontinuous? A disrupted disruption? **A stutter?** A repetition not of what comes before, or after, but a disruption of before/after. A cut that is itself cross-cut."

**A citation-practice precedent for `TRINH_reference_form_and_clarity.md`.** Note 22 of "Diffracting Diffraction" explains a naming choice in the first person: "This was one of the most sacred conversations I have had in academia. I treasure this memory . . . **I use her first name here because my sense is that using her surname would be too formal, stiff and artificial, and I didn't want to show disrespect in this way.**" An author stating in a note why she departs from the standard form for a person's name.

## 3. The clean absences

These are the plan's own interests, searched and not found, which makes them claims about Barad rather than gaps in the reading. **Across all five texts, 294,000 words:**

**Whitehead is 0**, in the four articles and in all 243,912 words of the book. Section 2.6 is Black Mountain College and Whitehead, and the plan already names it as "a gap to fill, not common ground". **Barad is not a route to Whitehead at all**, and that is now settled rather than assumed. **Merleau-Ponty is 0 in the four articles** and 8 in the book, so the phenomenology route to the permeable body runs through the book's eight hits or not through Barad. **Duchamp 0, infrathin 0, Manning 0, *agencement* 0, Guattari 0, Boccioni 0** across all five, so 2.5 and 2.6 get nothing here. **Malabou 0 and plasticity 1**, so the plasticity strand gets nothing. **Autism 0, neurodiversity 0, disability 0, prosthesis 0, stim 0** (the apparent hits were German and "estimate"), so 1.4 gets nothing, and "stim appears substantively only in Yergeau" survives another 294,000 words. **Deligny 0, Bateson 0, Glissant 0, Shlain 0.** **Alaimo 0, Tuana 0, trans-corporeal 0, viscous 0, Hekman 0, Neimanis 0, Siebers 0, Berlant 0**, so the *Material Feminisms* lane and Barad's lane do not cite each other in these texts, in either direction. **Mess 0, "first person" 0, epigenetics 0, analogy 0, aesthetic 0, music 0.**

One absence is worth stating positively: **art is at most 1 in each of the four articles and 2 in the book, artist 3, sculpture 1, painting 3**. Barad's images are scientific figures, a commissioned illustrator, her own photograph and one Berenice Abbott print. **She does not write about art.** That is a reason the art-and-physics layer has to be built rather than borrowed, and it is consistent with Hayles, whose book has no visual art in it either.
