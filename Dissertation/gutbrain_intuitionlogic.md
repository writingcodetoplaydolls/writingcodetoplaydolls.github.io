## Sources and leads

*Compiled with Claude, October 8. Rewrite or remove before this page goes into the dissertation.*

- **The episode:** *Star Trek: Voyager*, "Rise" (season 3, episode 19; aired February 26, 1997). Tuvok and Neelix crash on a planet under asteroid bombardment and escape by climbing a space elevator. Neelix's gut instinct turns out right. Tuvok, as reviewers quote him: "I am looking for Mr. Neelix's 'instinct.' Perhaps it will be marked."
- Gershon, M. D. (1998). *The second brain*. HarperCollins. The enteric nervous system as a "second brain," a year after the episode.
- Sudo, N., et al. (2004). Germ-free mice show an exaggerated stress response (*Journal of Physiology*): an early experimental link between gut microbes and the stress system.
- Cryan, J. F., & Dinan, T. G. (2012). Mind-altering microorganisms: The impact of the gut microbiota on brain and behaviour. *Nature Reviews Neuroscience, 13*, 701–712.
- Cryan, J. F., et al. (2019). The microbiota–gut–brain axis. *Physiological Reviews, 99*(4).
- Bennett, J. (2010). *Vibrant matter*, chapter 3, "Edible Matter": food as an actant in moods and bodies.
- Wilson, E. A. (2015). *Gut feminism*. Duke University Press. Argues that the gut is an organ of the mind.
- Alaimo, S. (2010). *Bodily natures*: transcorporeality, the body as porous to what it takes in.

## Questions

- The gut as a site of knowing: Neelix's instinct against Tuvok's logic, and the "man of reason" your comps trace.
- Proprioception and the gut: both sense the body from inside.
- Which Bertrand Russell text from the MFA? Russell wrote *Principia Mathematica* (1910–1913) with Whitehead. His neutral monism (*The Analysis of Mind*, 1921) holds that mind and matter are built from the same neutral stuff; that link is an association to check.
