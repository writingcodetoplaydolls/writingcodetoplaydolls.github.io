## 3. What has already been established, so it is not redone

Recorded in full in `K:\OneDrive\UNM\Dissertation\todo October 26\HAYLES_field_theory_of_culture_uptake_2026-10-30.md` (sections 1 to 9) and `K:\OneDrive\UNM\Dissertation\todo October 26\HAYLES_field_without_direct_connection_SOURCES_2026-10-30.md`.

**The thread followed so far is one concept**: at *The Cosmic Web* p. 22 Hayles proposes "a field notion of culture, a societal matrix which consists (in Whitehead's phrase) of a 'climate of opinion'", then writes that "**such a field theory of culture has yet to be definitively articulated, and is beyond the scope of this study**". She never returns to it under that name.

Findings, each verified:

1. **The phrase "field theory of culture" occurs once, at *Cosmic Web* p. 22, and nowhere else** in 1,513 distinct documents of Amy's project corpora, nor in *Chaos Bound*, nor in *How We Became Posthuman*, nor on the open web.
2. **She supplied the theory in *Chaos Bound* (1990) under a different name**: "cultural field" at pp. 4-5 and 23, "cultural matrix" at p. 4. The Eno passage at pp. 4-5 is the load-bearing one, where the connection is allowed not to have happened at all ("Or he might not") and the mechanism is **resonance** rather than transmission.
3. **She said so herself**, in the 1989 interview: "In *The Cosmic Web* I hypothesized a cultural matrix without really looking at what it meant or how it works. In my second book, *Chaos Bound*, I've tried to go more deeply into the questions."
4. **Somebody else did pick it up**: Manuela Rossini, *RCEI* 50 (2005) at p. 25, citing *The Cosmic Web* pp. 23-24 in her footnote 14, uses it for exactly this ("lines of influence between texts or disciplines are never direct but interwoven in very complex ways").
5. **In 2025 Hayles says the call went unanswered and says why**, at p. 193: a fuller analysis "would have included the work of **Alfred North Whitehead, of which I was largely ignorant at the time**", and she lacked the training of an intellectual historian to write it.
6. **In the 2025 reply the concept is nearly gone.** "Cultural matrix" appears once in ten pages; "field theory of culture" and "climate of opinion" not at all. It was absorbed into **constrained constructivism** (1991), then subordinated at p. 196 to the **species cognition problem**: "Why stop with the influence of culture when the biological capacities of a species have an even greater determinate influence?"
7. **The Whitehead phrase is traced**: Alfred North Whitehead, *Science and the Modern World* (1925), Lecture I, "The Origins of Modern Science", where he calls it "the happy phrase of a seventeenth century writer" and does not name the writer. Hayles gives no source note for it and *Science and the Modern World* is not in her 1984 bibliography.

**What the deep read should therefore start from, rather than discover again**: the concept exists, it was renamed, it was absorbed, it was superseded, and Hayles has said all three things in print.

## 4. Amy's own position, in her words, which are hers

Recorded verbatim. **Do not build on these, rewrite them, formalise them into a taxonomy, or adjudicate them.** They mark where her claim is being made.

> I am most interested in the notion that there need not be explicit connections or linearity between nodes in the field for them to have been influenced by each other. They need to be capable of affect and be affected.

> A cultural matrix would also be a transposition in the Braidottian sense.

> I prefer field theory of culture because it leads to assemblage theory of culture

Three facts that bear on these, stated without comment. Hayles's 2025 reply uses cognitive vocabulary 34 times and **affect zero times**, and mentions neither Braidotti nor Barad. Braidotti's own transposition is musical and genetic, "a musical variation that leaps across scales and compositions to find a pitch or a sharable level of intensity", and she says it is "neither a question of flattening out structural differences, nor of drawing facile analogies". Will Atkinson's "Field Theory and Assemblage Theory: Toward a Constructive Dialogue", *Theory, Culture & Society* 41(1), 2024, 79-94, which Amy holds, runs that bridge from Bourdieu to DeLanda with **Hayles at zero mentions**.

## 6. Traps, every one of them already paid for

1. ***The Cosmic Web*: use `9781501722974.pdf` and no other copy.** The two files named `Hayles_N_Katherine_The_Cosmic_Web_..._1984.pdf` and `...1984(1).pdf` are byte-identical to each other, are a bad ClearScan pass, and **render blank**, so nothing in them can be checked. The Cornell file supersedes both. It is **also** ClearScan, so its pages are traced vector reconstructions rather than photographs of the ink, but **it renders legibly and its body text is clean**: the four markers that corrupt the old copy are gone from the body, and the three that survive are all in the back matter (`Wharf` in the bibliography at p. 202, `Lancws` in the bibliography at p. 203 and in the index at p. 206). **Quotations from the body can now be checked by rendering the page.** Treat bibliography and index entries from this book as unreliable and check proper names there against another source. Section 10 has the detail. The twelve other volumes appear to be genuine publisher PDFs, but no quotation from any of them has yet been checked against a rendered page, and that caveat belongs on every file.
2. **Hayles's internal chapter cross-references in *The Cosmic Web* are each one too high.** Chapter 1 sends Pirsig to "Chapter 3" when he is chapter 2, Lawrence to "Chapter 4" when he is chapter 3, and so on. Unnumbered references ("the next chapter") are correct. Convert any chapter number quoted from inside the text.
3. **Printed folios, not PDF indices, and the two *Cosmic Web* files do not agree.** In the superseded copies, PDF index 21 is printed page 22. **In the Cornell file the two coincide**: PDF index 22 is printed page 22, across at least indices 18 to 28. Any PDF index recorded before 30 Oct 2026 refers to the old file and is one too low for the Cornell one. Read the folio off the page and state which of the two every citation uses. The folios themselves OCR badly in this book (`r8` for 18, `2I` for 21, `IOI` for 101), so read them from a render rather than from the text layer.
4. **Citation discipline is a standing rule.** Nothing gets cited unless it is logged in `K:\OneDrive\UNM\Dissertation\todo October 26\CITATION_CHECK_Dissertation.md`. Read that file before quoting anything. It already carries a known-bad list.
5. **`CITATION_CHECK_InteractiveProcess.md` does not exist** anywhere under `K:\OneDrive\UNM`, although the reading plan points to it. The file above is the dissertation lane's log.
6. **Absence findings in this project have usually turned out to be claims about what had been searched.** That has now happened five times. Before writing that Hayles never says X, state the corpus, the pattern and the denominator.
7. **Word boundaries matter.** `physics` without one catches "physical"; `cultural matri` without one catches "cultural matricide". Both errors have already been made and corrected here.
8. **Windows environment.** Bash `python -c` breaks on backslash paths; use forward slashes or write the script to a file. Windows cp1252 stdout fails on U+FFFD; write output to a UTF-8 file and read it back. Never run `sed` on a Windows path.
