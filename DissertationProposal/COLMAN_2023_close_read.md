## 2. What the article argues

Quantum feminicity is proposed as a modal form built from two fields with overlapping genealogies: feminist philosophy and quantum theory, both of which rejected inherited classical temporal frameworks. The object of the whole article is **time**, not the body. Time at 198 against bodies at 67.

The three sections name three modal aspects: countermanding (not linear), superposition (not gender), experimentation (not only experiential).

The argumentative core is in section 5, and it is a single move. Nancy Fraser proposes viewing gender "bifocally," by class distribution and by status recognition, and argues the two must be "superimposed." Colman's claim, contra Fraser, is that **the superimposition is better thought as a superposition**, because superposition allows the states to be genuinely overlapping rather than two lenses stacked. Fraser cite is *Fortunes of Feminism* (Verso, 2013), 162-163.

Her working definition, around p. 23, is that superposition describes matter existing in overlapping states with other matter so that "its entangled state become something else." She takes it from Stacey Moran and reads it as complementary to an agential realist account of entanglement, where entanglement is interference that can be "destructive and constructive."

Section 6 does the other useful thing. Via Arkady Plotnitsky she adopts **"experiment" in preference to "measurement."** Plotnitsky's quantum field as "reality without realism" makes observation and measurement, conventionally understood, inapplicable to quantum phenomena. Colman says she agrees, and makes the experimental the mode of activation.

## 3. What is usable, by section

### 2.5 and 1.5, the strongest join

**Plotnitsky's experiment-over-measurement, which is the Standard Stoppages argument in other words.** Molderings is in the reading plan because the *3 Standard Stoppages* are chance-made standard metres: measurement producing its object. Plotnitsky arrives at the same place from physics, by concluding that measurement is the wrong word and experiment is the right one. Colman endorses it in a published feminist article. That is a three-way join between the Duchamp material, the quantum material and a physics-literate source, and it is not currently in the draft.

- Plotnitsky, A. "Reality Without Realism: Quantum Measurement and Quantum Theory, from Bohr to Bell and from Bell to Bohr." *Entropy* 23, no. 9 (2021): 1197. DOI 10.3390/e23091197. **Open access, MDPI.** Not held

### 1.5, and a third reading of superposition

The reading plan's superposition note records two readings of the word: Manning's 17 uses in *Thought in the Act* against Bohr 0, which is Whitehead's mutual inclusion rather than the physics term. **Colman is a third**, and differently sourced: 20 uses, Bohr 13, a definition taken from Moran, and the term put to work converting Fraser's "superimposed" lenses on gender into a superposition. The proposal uses the word once.

**Refiled 29 Oct**, after Amy's correction. An earlier version of this section treated the three as incompatible and recommended flagging them rather than absorbing them. That was wrong, and it misread what 2.2 is for. Readings of a term do not have to agree to be relevant, and the differences between them are where a new claim gets made. Three sources using one word to do three jobs, with traceable and different genealogies (Whitehead and Forsythe; Bohr and Moran; the proposal's own single use), is material to read through itself, not a tidiness problem. The genealogies above are given so that the differences can be worked with, not resolved.

### Where it does not help

The bodies in this article are gendered and labouring bodies: World Economic Forum gender parity figures, caste-based violence, the housewife's day, Beyoncé's performing body as Fritz Lang's Maria. **permeab\* 0, porous 0, skin 2, membrane 0.** Nothing here reaches the permeable body. For that question this article is a dead end, and that is worth knowing before spending more time on it.

## 5. Citation flags

- **Colman dates Kirby's *Quantum Anthropologies* to 2013.** The copy held in this folder gives Duke University Press, Durham and London, **2011**, on its title page and in its copyright line. One of the two is wrong and the held copy is the better authority. Worth checking before either is cited.
- The article is unpaginated in the extracted text; the page numbers above come from the running footer in the PDF and should be confirmed against the published version.
- Several footnotes lost their opening words in extraction, where the PDF breaks a note across a page. The references list is intact.

## 6. Correction to `QUANTUM_PERMEABILITY_lens.md`

That file, written 29 Oct from search summaries before this text was read, says the article "argues the feminist engagement with quantum physics is not metaphorical borrowing but a shared mode of experimental thinking." **That overstates it.** The article does assert something close to that claim, but what it then does is apply superposition, entanglement and decoherence to afrofuturist music, to Beyoncé's stage persona and to Hayles's readings of science fiction. Decoherence appears as "the decoherence afforded by the vectors of any form of difference," which is a figure. Colman even notes of Hayles that she "does not engage with the language of quantum per se."

Whether that counts as metaphor is the exact thing the critics of this field dispute. The article asserts the answer rather than demonstrating it. The reading plan row has been corrected to say so.
